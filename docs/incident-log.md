# Incident log

ID: R1
what: Code that runs every point, turn or frame walked whole world lists, like every road segment or every obstacle, instead of using a spatial index or a cached result. Derived data was rebuilt on every call, like a cargo search that rebuilt the occupied cells for every spot it tried, and turn work, like a hovered truck's portrait, ran on the main thread. Work computed ahead of a turn posted a full turn to the worker on every world change, like each loot move or order change, and never dropped requests a newer world had replaced, so the worker computed stale turns in a queue that the next turn waited behind. The first fix saw that queue and only kept its wait out of the speed estimate. Any worker that answers only the newest request keeps one running and the newest wanted one, never a queue. Per-frame GPU work did the same: the dust and smoke layer's full-screen multisampled target copied out a stencil buffer that nothing read, 5 to 12 ms on every dusty frame and documented as slow on Windows' Direct3D, though the screen pass beside it had already turned that copy off. Build every per-frame target in one place that copies out only what a later pass reads.
cost: Turns took seconds and froze between moves, one robbery froze a player's truck for minutes, a player's 2x travel speed played at 1x after looting, the committee then met pauses between turns that grew with each order change, the same slowness was fixed several times over, and a player on a strong Windows graphics card reported the game stuttering at 20 to 30 fps.

ID: R2
what: Prices, wages, fees and contract rewards were guessed instead of measured with the economy harness, or taken from a run that had crashed. A supply order paid its crates at the shop's shortage price, so the shortage that posted the order also inflated its pay, and on top paid half a rush haul of the round trip, so one order paid 2000 to 6000 M and nobody measured it against trading. A rescale cut the start money to a figure worked out on paper from a tank fill and a repair without playing the opening, whose scripted first fight strips the truck, and its patch then raised it only to the exact sum of a basic repair, a tank and one cheap gun, with nothing for supplies and no margin. Size start money on what the opening really leaves a new driver needing, with room to spare, never on the cheapest total that technically covers it. The same rescale cut contract pay rules about tenfold but left the pay each contract stored when it was rolled, so carried saves kept showing and paying the old pay. A number a save stores but rules derive is drift that load must rebuild. The same rescale raised part tiers and NPC gear money and retuned the trade premium from the player's wage alone, never measuring the NPCs that live on the same numbers, so NPC traders' trade profit fell to almost nothing and robbers, who judge a hold by its value, held up trucks twice as often, small couriers too, for one crate. Measure a number the player and NPCs share on both sides.
cost: Jobs paid too little to bother with, items cost absurd amounts, broke players got tows they could not pay, a release candidate player got rich from one supply order, and the committee had to send features back to run the economy sim, sent a rescale back twice over its start money until a member set the figure by hand, and then met hauls paying ten times the new rules in its own save, and the release playtest found NPC trade near zero and hold-ups doubled, which took two more cards before the release.
