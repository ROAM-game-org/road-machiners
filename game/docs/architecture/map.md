# Map and world

- The map is baked offline, never built in the game. `src/mapgen/bake.ts` runs fixed layers over one draft: base relief, geology, road and site finish, the old world, the new world, territories, ground types and boulders. Boulders stand at cliff bases and on ridge tops, with odds scaled per zone by `GEOLOGY.boulders.zoneOdds`, so the range is strewn with them and the farm belt and swamp have few.
- Layout positions in `src/data/` are written in layout units and go through `scalePoint()`, so `MAP_SCALE` alone sets the gaps between places. Footprints stay in tiles, so a bigger scale adds space and never size. The landforms keep their size too: `TERRAIN.octaves` are in tiles, so the extra space is more of the same land. Map-wide mapgen counts (`roadWrecks`, old settlements, overlooks, old highways and the `ROADSIDE` bands) are set from the density of the last bake, and ranges that span the map (beacon, survey, clue and rescue) are multiples of `MAP_SCALE`. The Chaos Highway ground keeps the texel density of a 600-tile map.
- Canyon Bridge keeps the ground it was built on. `src/mapgen/ground-stamps.bin` holds the geology-finished ground (height, sand, flow, slump) of its window as it was made, and `stampTerritoryGround()` copies it in after geology, shifted to `CANYON_BRIDGE_GROUND`. The stamp holds `MAPGEN.groundHold` tiles past its edge and fades over `MAPGEN.groundFeather`. The territories have no ground stamp: their ground grows from the land like any other. The Fallen Sun sets `reliefFrom`, the spot its roads and pieces were laid out on, and `elevation.ts` reads its relief noise from there at full roughness, so its authored core keeps the ground it was made for while geology, the macro relief and the zones finish it as usual. A shifted centre must move by whole tiles, so the Fallen Sun snaps to the tile grid.

## Zones and relief

- The land is built in two scales. `src/sim/relief-solve.ts` solves the km shape on a coarse grid of `RELIEF_SOLVE.cell` tiles: an uplift map from `UPLIFT` in `src/data/terrain.ts` (edge uplift for the basin rim with gaps where the dry river enters and leaves, the north-east range along its spine with crest noise, a low floor scaled by zone), then implicit stream-power erosion after Braun and Willett with priority-flood pit filling and hillslope diffusion. The dry river and canyon cells are fixed base level. After the solve, the heights within `RELIEF_SOLVE.roadBed.width` of a region road blend toward a box-blurred copy of the grid, so a road runs smooth at the mean level of the land around it and never in a trough that drains that land into washes beside it. In the mountains that band narrows to `roadBed.mountainWidth`, so a road there is a narrow pass between rough slopes. The range lifts along its spine and along `spur`, a short ridge into Nose's rock, and the mountain zone's roughness keeps its valley floors uneven. `macroHeight()` samples the solve with a cubic and holds every site and the Fallen Sun at the height of its centre. `bakedHeight()` in `src/sim/elevation.ts` adds it in height units after `heightFromElevation()`, so it never meets the steep gain above `mountainFrom`. The solve throws on a diffusion step above 0.125, where an explicit step stops damping the checkerboard.
- A zone is an entry of `ZONES`: a kind and a polygon or a path, in layout units, so it scales with `MAP_SCALE`. `src/sim/zones.ts` turns each into a weight from its signed distance, smoothed over `ZONE_FIELD.band` with a noise-warped edge, normalised where zones overlap, on a coarse grid. `zoneRoughness()` scales today's fBm and broad rolling noise, so the plains are calm and the mesa threshold fires only in rugged zones. `ZONE_RULES` holds each kind's roughness and floor uplift.
- The zone layers run after `finishLayer()`, in this order: `desertLayer()` (dune ridges across `GEOLOGY.wind`, a fixed wavelength, a steep lee share, crest breaks and a star dune, fed by the desert weight and kept off roads, sites and territories), `swampLayer()` (a water table under the macro height, sloughs stretched along the outflow, a wet channel to the dry river, then `blackMud`, dirty water and scrub marks and dead trees and sunk wrecks), `reserveSpurs()` (marks every territory spur as track, so the old and new world keep off it), and `farmBeltLayer()` (a canal per bank traced along the macro contour from its headgate at a fixed fall, turning up to 60° a step, and ended where the base ground has risen more than `cutDepths` canal depths above the lowest ground it has passed, so no cutting runs deeper; laterals down the fall line at grid crossings, leaving at the canal's bed level; every ditch bed graded as the running minimum of the ground less its depth, so no bed rises along its length, with banks beside it that never raise another ditch's bed, and broken at track lines and flattened ground, where causeways cross; plots on a grid turned to the belt's mean slope, groves and farmsteads, with breakage rising with distance from the headgates). Wet canal beds near Bowl are `BUILT_CANAL`, every other bed and wall `BUILT_DITCH`, which bakes as sand and keeps boulders off.
- `exitLayer()` in `src/mapgen/exits.ts` makes every ravine a truck can get into one it can climb out of. It runs after the farm belt, before props exist, and again as the last layer before the report, around every prop. `ravineEscape()` finds floor tiles: drivable tiles at least `EXITS.depth` below a morphological closing of the ground over `closeRadius`, or drivable tiles cut off from the player start, kept only where a truck can arrive by driving or by dropping off a higher edge, and only where a 2 by 2 block of drivable tiles holds a truck. It measures each floor tile's drive to non-floor ground reached from the start. A floor tile may lie `spacing` tiles of driving from a way out, and up to `mountainSpacing` in the range, where a narrow valley leads out along its floor. Every floor tile past `carveShare` of that gets a ramp: a straight notch `width` tiles wide from the last floor tile toward the start-reached ground within `reach`, climbing at `climb` of the steepest grade, with walls that rise `wall` per tile beside it, so it reads as a narrow cut and not a sloped plane. A ramp starts at the wall foot, so it never runs along a floor. Its lane fills where the line crosses a lower gully, like a causeway, but never on ditch or canal ground, so a canal stays one graded bed with notches in its banks. It keeps roads, tracks, pads and every site, territories included, and drops only the small props in `clearKinds` where it cuts more than `propCut` under them. Ground under a prop of at least `blockRadius` is no floor, since no truck stands there. A pocket of up to `fillMax` tiles that no ramp opens, and shallow enough that its fill slopes back to the ground within `rimReach`, is filled up to the lowest start-reached ground above it within `rimReach`, sloping down from it at `climb`, for `fillRounds` rounds. The exit tests check a long trench gets ramps every few tens of tiles, a path out on ground no steeper than `grade`, and no fill in its floor.
- `fillGlassTrail()` in `src/mapgen/glass.ts` runs after the territories and glazes a band along the first part of `DESCENT_PATH` that narrows toward the crater. Its ripples and the Glass Flats glass read the path frame from `src/mapgen/descent.ts`, stretched along the path. `apronLayer()` runs after the rocks and drops rim rocks, hull shards and ship parts round the Fallen Sun, outside every territory. `CRASH.apron.parts.share` of the pieces are ship parts picked from `parts.looks`, and `shards` of the rest are hull shards. `CRASH.apron.reach` is the field's reach, and each try is kept with the product of three weights: `apronShare()` (full downrange, fading to nothing uprange over `bareEase`), `apronThinning()` (falls with the gap from the crater outline as `(1 + gap / edge) ^ -falloff` and tapers to zero over the last `taper` of the reach) and `apronClump()` (a smoothstep of two layers of value noise, so the field has clumps and gaps and no clear edge). The same layer sets `CRASH.apron.caches.count` `shipCache` props in the densest clumps of the outer field, at least `from` tiles outside the outline, `apart` tiles from each other and off the roads. `oldPlaces()` makes each a `wreckage` old place, and `OLD_PLACES.chance` picks about half of them, so `npm run old-spots` must run after a bake. A `wreckage` spot stocks from the hull scrap table and its NPC goal is `searchOldWreckage`.
- `zoneReport()` in `src/mapgen/report.ts` runs at the end of the bake and throws when a zone misses `ZONE_CHECKS`: the range's height spread and cliff share, the desert's sand share, dune relief and the median crest spacing along the wind, the swamp's wet ground share and how far its floor sinks below its rim, the farm belt's field, track and canal share and the worst rise of any ditch bed away from crossings and the riverbed, the glass share along the descent path, a drive flood from the player start that must reach every gate and territory entry, and every ravine floor of at least `EXITS.minFloor` tiles that lies further than its exit spacing from a way out.
- `keepStartClear()` in `src/mapgen/start-ground.ts` removes the props that overlap the opening wreck and the arrival farm, and the roadside layer keeps off them, so every bake leaves the new-game spots free.
- `src/mapgen/newworld.ts` grows scrub from moist ground, fills small basins with dirty water or toxic pools, and places shack camps with fences and junk, and car wrecks, with numbers in `NEW_WORLD`.
- `src/mapgen/oldworld.ts` places ruined settlements, overlook and bend buildings, old asphalt roads, old highways with broken bridges over deep gorges, power lines, billboards, tank hulks, dead fields and the ship debris trail with its stray pods (`shipDebris`, last, so no other rule's layout moves), with numbers in `OLD_WORLD` in `src/data/terrain.ts`.
- `src/mapgen/boats.ts` runs after the roadside layer and only appends props, from its own stream (`BOATS.seedOffset`). It lays small groups of `fishingBoat`, `riverBarge` and `skiff` props on the dry river's floor and on the highest-flow open ground near it (wash beds). Each boat keeps off roads, sites, decks, territories, built ground, steep ground and every other prop, leaves a `BOATS.passLane` open down the river's middle, and never lines three of a kind up in a row. The layer throws when river groups, wash groups or boats leave their bands in `BOATS`. Boat groups are an old place type (`boats`), so they get loot spots and survey sites like the others, but they are no claim wrecks or direction cues.
- `src/mapgen/roadside.ts` runs after the fortresses and only appends props, so every earlier prop keeps its id. It draws from its own streams (`ROADSIDE.seedOffset` plus one per rule) and places leaning `roadSign`s beside region roads and old asphalt, `routeSign` boards at junctions, town approaches and road stations, lone `barrel`s by car wrecks and ruins, `drums` heaps by shack camps, tank hulks and junk, and dead trees on wash banks, in ruin yards and along dead-field edges. It keeps off roads, sites, decks, cliffs, built ground and every other prop, never lines three of a kind up in a row (`ROADSIDE.rowGuard`), and throws when a group's count leaves its band in `ROADSIDE`. None of these kinds is a loot spot or a direction cue.
- `src/mapgen/geology.ts` holds the rain, slump, wind and dune rules, with numbers in `GEOLOGY` in `src/data/terrain.ts`.
- A territory is a location of kind `territory` in `REGION.locations`: no edge, gates, pads or `site` obstacle, no flattening and no hardpan, so trucks drive in over the natural ground. Its shape is a circle of `radius` round `pos`, or the polygon `outline` when one is set. Old Orchard's outline keeps its old basin as its south and west and grows over the open plain north and east of it, to about the area of Glass Flats, and the Fallen Sun's runs inside its basin floor and furrow; for an outline, `radius` is its bounding radius, a reach and never an edge. Its rules live in `TERRITORIES` in `src/data/territory.ts`: a seed offset, the spot and debris gaps, the reactor with its hazard, and either a `wreck` or a `farm`. The Fallen Sun is a wreck: authored hull pieces, caches, decks, dirt roads and spurs, and drawn patches, rim rocks and scree. Its piece numbers are measured in the level concept's pixels and its roads in the second reference's, and each comment names them. The furrow, the wing and the flaps are inferred, and their comments say so. Old Orchard is a farm laid out along its old road, the `spine`.
- `siteGap(site, pos)` in `src/sim/sites.ts` owns every location's edge: tiles from pos to the edge, negative inside, from the outline or the circle. `siteEdgeCrossings()` gives where a segment crosses it. Membership, entries, discovery, the old-world clearance, the old-road router, road paint and grading, nav, the territory bake and the farm bake all call these two, and nothing else compares a distance with a location's radius. The ground paint marks no site with a disc: `yardLook()` in `src/render/groundPaint.ts` fades the baked yard hardpan of a town or site to the look of the ground beside it over `YARD_FADE_TILES` tiles inside the edge, so the yard has no hard rim. Ground type, speed and the map file stay as baked.
- `src/sim/territory.ts` answers every territory question for the sim: membership, spots, entries, inner grounds and hazard zones. `territoryGrounds()` gives the inner grounds, which the Sunward vigil reads. Raider hunting grounds are baked separately, see `docs/architecture/npcs.md`. A territory's entries are where region roads cross its outline, and one with none throws. Only a territory marked `offRoad`, Glass Flats, has no road crossing: its one entry is the outline point nearest a road, and it throws if a road ever crosses it. It also turns a wreck's authored layout into map tiles (`territoryPieces`, `territoryCaches`, `territoryRoads`, `landingStrips`, `reactorPos`), so the bake, the render and the tests place things alike. The reactor's position is the hazard's centre. The hazard's core has two discs, the rod's and the breach light on the floor in front of the breach mouth, which `hazardZones()` places from the data and refuses when it reaches past the drain. `inCore()` in `src/sim/hazard.ts` is the only test of both. `src/sim/hazard.ts` drains driver health inside a zone, and the nav layer blocks zones for routes only.
- `src/mapgen/territory.ts` fills each territory with its own rng, drawn from the map seed and the offset `TERRITORY_SEED_OFFSET + rules.seed`, so adding a territory shifts no other's draws. For a wreck it seats and places the pieces first, then marks its dirt roads, then places the reactor and the caches, each at its authored yaw so the hatch of its section faces open ground (`territoryCaches()` gives both the bake and the glass clearance their yaw), then draws the rim rocks and each patch's field spots and debris. Spots come before debris, so debris never boxes one in. Drawn props keep clear of the pieces' boxes, the dirt roads, the decks, the landing strips past each lip, the roads, the hazard, cliffs, and a farm's old road, pads and tracks.
  - The seat levels the ground under each piece's low boxes to the height at its centre and eases it back to the relief over a few tiles, so a long piece neither floats nor sinks.
  - The ribcage tube and the arch shells are hollow models. Their roofs start above `PHYSICS.truckClearance`, so physics, nav, sight and free-spot checks see only their walls, and trucks drive through with no special case.
  - A wreck's `roads` and `spurs` are `track` roads marked by `markRoads()` in `src/mapgen/marks.ts`, which the farm also calls. A web road stays inside the outline. A spur starts on a web road and runs past the outline into the wasteland, and marking it throws on a cliff, a region road, another site or an earlier prop, so a spur never silently cuts through the map. `throwOnRoadCliffs()` throws on a cliff tile under the width of any web road inside the outline, for wrecks and the farm alike. A wreck whose rules set `gradeRoads` (Glass Flats) grades its web roads first with `gradeTerritoryRoads()`, the farm's grade below, before seating its pieces. The Fallen Sun does not, so its hand-built ground stays as authored. Spurs are never graded, since they run past the outline under earlier layers' props. Boulders keep off every `BUILT_TRACK` tile (`fitsOffRoad()` in `src/mapgen/bake.ts`).
  - `paintRoadMask()` in `src/render/roadPaint.ts` strokes the dirt roads and spurs into the road mask's green channel, a spur fading over its last tiles. Each blurred stroke is clipped to its own bounds plus the blur reach, which keeps boot fast and changes no pixel. The ground shader in `src/three/render/roads.ts` draws them in `PAL.dirtRoad` with a darker soft shoulder. Region roads stay in the red channel.
- A basin (`TERRAIN.features.basins`, `basin()` in `src/sim/elevation.ts`) is a landform with a polygon floor, a per-vertex bank and rim, a depth and floor swells. The Fallen Sun lies in one: cliff arcs where the bank is short and the rim high, a drivable notch, an east hill, and open banks to the south. It applies before geology, so rain, slump and wind roughen it like any land. Its crash furrow is a channel beside Broken Wing's trench. No paint marks a crater or basin floor, so the relief shows it.
- A wreck territory may also hold `buildings` (groups placed by `placeBuildingGroups()` in `src/mapgen/farm.ts`, which throws when one touches a wreck piece), and its `rimRocks` may be null when no basin lies under it. A territory's `glass` rules (`GlassRules`) are filled by `fillGlass()` in `src/mapgen/glass.ts` after the wreck or farm: it marks fused glass on open tiles with a cover that grows from the centre toward the edge, clear of every prop, scaled by `glassFade()`: a smoothstep of the depth inside the outline over `fade.band` tiles, bent by value noise of `fade.warp` tiles, so glass and spires fade in with no sharp border. It stands spires wholly on glass, each kept `SPIRE_LANE` from any other spire edge, which is twice the largest chassis radius, so the widest truck drives between any two. A test checks every pair on the generated Glass Flats spires. Glass is the `BUILT_GLASS` code and the `glass` terrain type; the ground shader draws it as seamed plates and no boulder or scatter lands on it. Glass Flats is the one territory that uses all three.
- `fillFarm()` in `src/mapgen/farm.ts` lays out a territory's `FarmRules`. Every position is authored in the road's frame: `onOrchardRoad(s, c)` in `src/data/region.ts` turns tiles `s` along the road toward its north end and `c` across it toward map west into an offset from the centre. `ORCHARD_HEADING` sets the road's heading so it shows at the concept image's 25° screen angle from the fixed camera. Building turns are relative to that heading. The authored skeleton is the roads, pads, canals, building poses, run lines and blocks; everything on it is jittered, broken or scattered from the territory rng. The Orchard's grown part is laid out by `orchardGrowth()` in `src/data/territory.ts`: it cuts the open plain into field cells, trims each cell to the outline, and gives each cell a grove block or a yard of buildings, with tracks between cells and canals, runs and emplacements on them. Its lists come after the old skeleton's, so the old farm draws first. The steps run in order, and each mark takes only a tile no earlier mark took:
  - `roads` are marked in list order: an `oldRoad` as asphalt and a `track` as `BUILT_TRACK` (the `track` ground: hardpan to drive, painted paler so roads show). The first five are the main roads, and each ends within a tile of the outline, so it leaves the farm. The spur road in `REGION` ends at the old road's south end.
  - The roads are graded into the ground first, in one pass of `gradeTerritoryRoads()` in `src/mapgen/marks.ts` over `gradePaths()` in `src/sim/road-grade.ts`, the envelope `gradeRoads()` uses. Each road holds its own `grade`, and the ground blends back over `farmGradeMargin` tiles past its edge, so the ridges stay ridges. The main roads and tracks hold the road grade, so the old highway cuts through the north ridge. The army's dozer track up to the ridge shelf holds 0.25. Each building pose gets a pad in the same pass: the corners under it level to their mean height and blend back over `levelMargin` tiles. A pad ring the grading turns into a cliff throws.
  - Pads are marked as `BUILT_PAD` (the `concrete` ground: drives like asphalt, painted pale grey) and `canals` as `BUILT_CANAL` (the `canal` ground: drives like dirty water, painted blue-grey).
  - Each building pose becomes a loot spot, turned by up to its group's `turnJitter` and shifted by up to `shift`. `spotTableAt()` in `src/sim/territory.ts` finds its table by its look, so one look has one table.
  - Runs of segment props (fences and barriers) go along their polylines. A `broken` share of segments is dropped and each left segment turns and shifts by its `jitter`, and a `knocked` share turns and shifts much further, shoved out of line. A segment that touches a road, track, canal or pad, reaches into a building or crosses another segment is dropped. Old Orchard's perimeter is `PERIMETER` runs inside its open sides, with authored gaps and a road left open at every crossing.
  - Each emplacement stands 1 to 3 sandbag arcs side by side across its `face`, each turned to bulge toward the threat, then draws its `tankTrap` hedgehogs ahead of them on the approach, never behind an arc. Numbers live in `EMPLACEMENT`. An emplacement point off the outline, on a cliff or on a New World road throws. Arcs and traps on marks, in a spot's parking gap or on other props are dropped.
  - Every drawn or decoration prop but trees is dropped when the ground under its footprint rises past the territory's `relief` (`footprintRelief()` in `src/mapgen/bake.ts`). So no prop floats or sinks on a slope, and render and physics keep seating props at the ground under their centre. The Fallen Sun sets `relief` to `null`, so its hull debris stays dug in.
  - Every block marks its field, turned `turn` off the road's frame. Then `clutter` scatters loose pieces round the buildings of the looks each rule names, from the debris gap to `reach` tiles past the building, off roads, marks and fields.
  - Each block gets trees on a grid in its row direction, with jitter, missing points and random turns, then `strays` single trees stand just past the block edges, up to `groves.maxTrees`. Trees keep off the roads, marks, runs, clutter and every building's parking gap. Strays also keep `OPENING.clear` tiles off each opening, the gap of up to `OPENING.gap` tiles between two consecutive perimeter runs, so a truck drives straight through it.
  - No three run or clutter pieces of one look stand in a ruled row, within 0.1 tile of one line and 0.02 rad of one turn: a run segment that would make one turns a little more or drops, and a clutter draw that would is redrawn.
  - After the farm, `src/mapgen/territory.ts` draws the farm's `debris` in the band along its `spine`, the debris gap from the buildings. The farm draws no field spots: its army caches are authored with its buildings, since its groves leave no open band. Its inner grounds lie in the `grounds` band beside the spine, inside the outline.
- The farm fails loudly on authored parts and drops decoration. A building, pad, road point, canal point, run point, emplacement point or block corner off the outline, on a cliff or on a New World road throws. So does a building on a farm road unless its pose sets `shoulder`, or on a canal. A road's outer end may lie up to a tile past the edge. Trees, strays, run segments, sandbag arcs, tank traps and clutter on bad ground are dropped, but a block whose ground rejects more than 30% of the trees it plans (its grid points not left empty) throws. Move an authored position onto good ground and note the old and new `(s, c)` in its comment, never loosen a check.
- The Roadhouse, `marshal-post`, is a baked `landmark` site beside the crossroads east of the Granary, a fixed 16 tiles east of the junction at any map scale, with a straight spur road from the junction and a `compound` fort. Its interior is a relay mast, a shed, a lean-to and a hearing table (`buildRoadhouse()` in `src/three/render/interiors/outpost.ts`). The new-world layer keeps its car wrecks and fences off the Sunward Hollow, which is no baked place, so a rebake cannot put a prop into the Hollow's fort.
- The Sunward Hollow is no baked place and no `REGION` location, so the map file and its hash stay as they are. `HOLLOW` in `src/data/sunward.ts` holds its point, its site def `HOLLOW.site` with one gate toward the crater, the interior pieces, the laager's stores, bays and lamps. Its fort is the `'sunward-hollow'` entry of `FORTRESS_SITES`, a scrap circle of radius 5, smaller than every small site, with the scrap gatehouse, whose model is a shut gate, so nobody enters. The order's eleven bays stand outside the curtain in two rows along a lane that runs from the gate toward the crater. `src/sim/hollow-layout.ts` owns the layout: `hollowObstacles()` checks it once and turns `fortressProps(HOLLOW.site)` and the interior pieces into landmark obstacles with ids `hollow-<n>`, which `newWorld()` and load add to the world and `saveOf()` leaves out, like baked props. `discoverHollow()` finds it by sight. The Hollow stands where no road comes within a maxed driver's day discovery reach and a margin, and no town or site within the gray vision, as `scripts/hollow-site.mjs` checks. It stands in the southern desert west of Glass Flats, about 860 tiles of open ground from the Fallen Sun along the glass trail, so the Cavalcade spends over an hour off the roads each way. The ride keeps off roads like a hiding raider (`keepsOffRoads()` in `src/sim/off-road.ts`), and the picker rejects any spot whose ride passes a town or site within the gray vision. The committed bake predates that move: it kept props off the old spot, which stays open ground until the next rebake, and the picker proved the new footprint clear of the baked props, sites and decks. Every load gives each Sunward car its own bay, nearest its saved home first, so a moved Hollow never stacks cars in one bay. `hollowReach()`, the farthest point of the fort and the laager from its centre, sets how far the next rebake keeps off (`inHollow()`), how far the player must be to find it and how far the order spawns out of sight. `scripts/hollow-site.mjs` checks the whole footprint, not a circle.
- Baked props stay out of saves and are rebuilt from the map file on load. `decodeMap()` in `src/sim/terrain.ts` reads the file. The file stores a prop kind as its index in `PROP_KINDS` and a ground type as its index in `TYPE_IDS`. A change to either order, or a removed entry, bumps the file version and needs a re-bake, so a file with the old codes is refused rather than misread. Boot fetches it in `src/three/main.ts`, and tests use `TEST_MAP` from `src/test/map.ts`.

## Road network

- Bowl and Nose each have a ring road outside their walls, so a truck goes from one gate road to the other without entering the town. `withTownRings()` in `src/data/region.ts` appends it to `REGION.roads` after the authored roads, so road indices do not move. `townRing()` finds where the town's two roads cross a circle `TOWN_RING.margin` tiles past the town radius, past the pads, and runs the shorter arc between them in steps of `TOWN_RING.step`. It throws unless exactly two roads cross the circle. The ring stays outside the circle, so a town keeps its two gates.
- The new-game arrival (`ARRIVAL` in `src/data/arrival.ts`) lies `ROAD_OFFSET` tiles south of the road from Bowl, about two screens of driving, at a fixed layout x east of Bowl. The road is out of sight at the start, and `layout.test.ts` checks the gap to the nearest road and that Bowl lies west within two map squares.
- `roadNetworkOf(world)` in `src/sim/road-network.ts` builds a graph from `atlas.roads` once per atlas and caches it by `atlasKeyString()`. It is never saved. Nodes are road ends, junctions, site gates and territory entries. A road end within `DIRECTIONS.joinSnap` of another road splits that road and joins there, and roads that cross split each other. Road pieces inside a site that blocks driving are dropped, so no route runs through a town. `buildRoadNetwork()` throws when a site has no gate node or a territory no entry node. `shortestPath()` is Dijkstra by length, `nearestRoadPoints()` reads a bucket index over the road segments, and `splitAt()` adds the start and the goal as nodes. Directions read the network and nothing else does yet; NPC driving still uses `route()`.

## Generated maps

- Chaos Highway's highway is the one map the game builds itself. `highwayMap(seed, window)` in `src/sim/highway.ts` returns a `BakedMap`, as `decodeMap()` does for Icarus, so props become obstacles through `mapObstacles()`, stay out of saves, and every grid reader sizes from `terrain.size`. Its hash is `highway:<HIGHWAY.version>:<seed>:<window>`. A change to the generator rules bumps `HIGHWAY.version`, which sends open Chaos Highway saves to the rescue. Version 4 put the outposts across the road.
- A window is 410 tiles square. The highway runs north (map -y), which the fixed camera draws from the bottom left of the screen toward the top right at about 27°. Each stretch has its own length from 230 to 310 tiles, drawn from the seed by `stretchLength()`, and `milestoneAt(seed, j)` is the running sum. Window k's local tile (x, y) is absolute (x, y - o), where o is `milestoneAt(seed, k)` minus 50, so the next window lies one stretch length further north, and `windowShift(seed, k)` is that move. `toRoad()` and `fromRoad()` in `src/sim/highway.ts` convert a map spot to the road frame and back: `n`, tiles along the road northward, and `u`, tiles across it, positive to the right of travel, which is map +x. They take the seed because the window origin depends on it. `roadVector()` turns a step along and across into a map step, and it, `windowShift()`, `toRoad()`, `fromRoad()` and `centerU()` are the only code that knows where the road runs on the map. `alongOf()` and `acrossOf()` measure a road spot from its true foot on the bent centerline. Milestone j sits at `n = 50 + the lengths of stretches 1 to j`, local (205, 360) as a window's south outpost and (205, 360 minus the stretch length) as its north one. Windows k and k+1 overlap in a band of 410 minus the stretch length rows, at least 100, that holds the north outpost's fort.
- The cross-section goes by the distance d from the centerline: four 2-tile lanes of asphalt to d 5, a flat verge of hardpan and sand to 18, badlands of gravel, scrub and scree to 26, then a ridge that rises 3 height units over 4 tiles. The ridge is a cliff band no truck climbs, so with the closures it fences the drivable corridor. The centerline is a bend plan per stretch, `bendPlan(seed, j)`, drawn from the `bends` stream and memoized by `seed:j`: straight for 40 tiles by each milestone, and between them `bendCells()` cells of at least 40 tiles, with a cosine swing of 4 to 7 tiles to alternating sides at each cell end, or straight in one stretch in ten. It throws if a ramp would turn past 35° off north. The land measures the cross-section per row, as the distance from the row's center over the slope's length, which is exact on straight road and within 0.3 tiles at the asphalt edge on the tightest bend. Every height, ground type, prop and deck is a pure function of the seed and `(n, u)`: the line from the bend plan, the profile from 1D noise, relief from 2D noise, scenery from one stream per 16-tile chunk along the road, and layouts, fort sides, group plans and stock from one stream per stretch (`stretchStream()`). No pass spans the window, so overlapping windows agree and the land is the same in any order of generation. A window draws scenery for road a little past both ends of its span, since on a bend a prop beside the road leans into the window from road outside it.
- `stretchLayout(seed, j)` in `src/sim/road-hazards.ts` lays stretch j as open arenas of at least 36 tiles between hazard scenes, memoized by `seed:j`. A scene is a recipe in the road frame from `HAZARDS` in `src/data/modes.ts`: a pileup, a jackknifed hulk, a checkpoint chicane, a tank line, a rockfall, craters, a hull-plate ramp or a roadblock. A roadblock walls every lane and one verge and leaves its passage on the open verge instead, checked by `leavesVergeGap`. Each keeps its vehicles at the recipe's skid angles and leaves a passage: after every blocking piece grows by the widest truck's radius plus half a tile, a free stretch across of 3 tiles must remain, partly on the asphalt. A layout that cannot keep that rule redraws, then throws. Scene pieces are laid along and across the bent road, so a scene turns with its bend. Rockfall rocks keep a wider gap, since the inside of a bend draws pieces together.
- Scene vehicles include truck hulks. A baked prop with `hulk` set to a chassis id becomes a `wreck` obstacle with id `hulk-<k>` and the charred hulk render. It is part of the map, so nav treats it as static and the kill-wreck cap never prunes it.
- Craters dip the ground and turn it to gravel. A ramp is a deck: `decksOf()` in `src/sim/bridge.ts` returns `highwayDecks()` for a highway key, each a 3-tile `ship_flap` deck rising 0.35 over 5 tiles in one lane. A deck's `look` names its model, so `src/three/render/ship-decks.ts` draws highway ramps with the Fallen Sun flap.
- Each outpost from milestone 1 is a fort site in the highway atlas, standing across the road. `outpostFort()` centers it on the road at its milestone, on the straight road the bend plan leaves there, with `look: 'outpost'`, a `turn` that squares its walls to the heading and two `gates`, one in its south wall and one in its north wall, on the road axis. `fortressOf()`, `isFortress()` and the site decor read `look ?? id`, so the fort is the `outpost` fortress style: walls, towers and 6-tile gatehouses from `fort_outpost_*`, and the interior from `src/three/render/interiors/outpost.ts`. `fortressProps()` in `src/sim/fortress.ts` is the one way fort pieces become baked props, for the Icarus bake and the highway alike. `outpostGateSpots()` gives each gate's center, outward direction and width. The yard has the road's own height, and is concrete outside the asphalt.
- A gate that is closed for good is the baked prop `fortOutpostShut`, the two leaves shut in the opening, so physics, nav and sight block on it like a wall: the next fort's north gate, and from window 1 the last fort's south gate. A gate that moves is not baked. `src/sim/outpost-gates.ts` owns the yard test, the park spots, whether the gates are shut and each gate's state, and `src/three/render/outpostGates.ts` draws the two movable gates from it.
- Each outpost carries a barricade line on its center line, `fortLine()` in `src/sim/road-hazards.ts`: two staggered barrier rows from its side walls to the ridges, and sandbag nests on the verges. Window j closes to the north with outpost j+1's line and its closed gate, and from window 1 to the south with outpost j's line and its closed gate. Window 0 closes to the south with a burnt pileup 26 tiles behind its start. The line belongs to its milestone, so both windows that hold a fort lay the same pieces, and only the shut gate props differ in an overlap.
- `src/sim/atlas.ts` owns what a map holds: its roads with their widths and lanes, a road index, towns and locations, decks, hazard zones, and whether it has old-world loot spots and Icarus landforms. `Terrain.atlas` is a plain key, `{ kind: 'icarus' }` from `decodeMap()` or `{ kind: 'highway', seed, window }`, and `atlasOf()` resolves it. The Icarus atlas is `REGION`, `ROAD_INDEX`, `DECKS` and `hazardZones()`. The highway atlas holds its one four-lane road, the window's outpost forts and its ramp decks. Every reader on a path both modes share asks the atlas, never `REGION`: site obstacles, discovery, oasis and click snaps, nav and hazards, decks under `heightAt`, old spots, road paint, pads, sites, ship decks, scatter, radio words and town music. Roaming-only systems, like markets, traders, lawmen and tows, keep reading `REGION`.
- The road painter strokes atlas roads with no lanes as dirt roads in the red channel of the road mask. A road with lanes gets a smooth band at 0.3 in the blue channel, which the ground shader draws as lighter highway asphalt over the `asphalt` ground. Crater holes clear it, so the gravel shows. Scatter keeps off the atlas roads. The ground texture keeps the Icarus pixels per tile on any map size.

## Fortresses

- `src/mapgen/fortress.ts` runs last in the bake. For each site in `FORTRESS_SITES` (`src/data/fortress.ts`) it lays a closed curtain, circle, square or bastioned trace, of props named for the site's style and the piece, 26 kinds such as `fortPumpworksWall` and `fortShipGate`. `FORT_PROPS` in `src/sim/fortress.ts` is the one table of them, and gives each kind its model. Fort props take the ordinary `<kind>-<index>` id.
- `FORTRESS_STYLES` gives each style its gatehouse size, its pieces and whether its gate is flush. Laying a piece a style lacks throws.
  - At the pumpworks, cistern and scrap styles (`DEEP_GATE`), each gatehouse's outer face sits on the site circle at a `siteGates()` point. A barbican joins it back to the curtain where the curtain misses it.
  - At the five flush styles (Bowl, Dustwell, Granary, Salvage Yard and Nose), the gatehouse stands in the curtain with its outer face on the curtain line, on the gate's bearing. The ground between it and the gate point is a forecourt outside the curtain.
  - Either way the gate points and pads stay on the site circle, so gates, pads and tows work as before. `site.radius` is only the layout bound. `fortressGates()` is the one owner of each gatehouse's face, bearing and size, which the gate furniture reads.
- A bastioned outline (Bowl) is built by `bastionedCorners()` from `bastions`: capital bearings, the curtain radius of the inner vertices, the salient radius, and the gorge and flank lengths. Each bastion is an arrowhead of five corners, with a tower on its salient and on both shoulders, and each shoulder stands out past the curtain it flanks so a tower sees the foot of every curtain and face. A test (IV25) samples the foot of every wall and checks it. The layout throws when a tower leaves the circle, a curtain is too short for its gorges, the outline crosses itself or a gate lies by a gorge or tower. `fortressCore()` returns the main enclosure, the inner vertices of a bastioned site and the outline of any other. A circle with `towers: false` (Granary) gets no towers, and `towerEvery` sets the sections between a circle's towers (Nose, 3).
- A site with a `pit` (Bowl) has its crater dug by the fortress layer, since a render-only pit would be covered by the terrain mesh. `pitDepth()` in `src/sim/fortress.ts` owns the crater's shape, measured from `fortressCore()`, so a bastion is a rim-level platform: the ground stays at the rim within `margin` of the core's edge, then steps down `stepHeight` every `terraceWidth` tiles to a flat floor. The layer lowers only the height corners inside the core, over the outline's bounding box. It types the flat terrace and floor tiles `field` and the risers `scree`. The road ends inside the curtain fall into the pit, so the road grade test skips road behind a fortress curtain, where no truck drives.
- A site with `rock` (Nose) leaves those stretches of its outline to a rock mass. No wall or tower stands on a segment whose middle lies in one, and the walls either side end in the rock. Nose's ring was built around the crashed colony ship, up to the flanks of the mountain it came down on. The mountain is ground, not props. `noseRocks()` in `src/sim/nose.ts` poses the `nose_rise` and `nose_crag` shapes at the site center, turned to `noseFrame()`, the ship's frame, and `fortressLayer()` raises the ground to the models' own surface. `noseRiseAt()` in `src/mapgen/nose-rise.ts` ports the height functions of `tools/blender/nose_rise.py` and `nose_crag.py`, with their constants in `NOSE_RISE`: a 6 m terrace and a rubble slope toward the yard, a cradle under the ship's belly, and the mountain's crest behind it, with value noise of `NOSE_ROCK.jitterCell` tiles for the facet jitter. A change to either model's shape goes into the port too. Past the footprint, outside the site circle, the foot slopes down over `NOSE_ROCK.run` tiles, along a chamfer distance with a ragged edge, and keeps off roads and pads. The yard inside the circle stays level, so the scaffolds stand on the ground. `UPLIFT.range.spur` runs a ridge from the range spine into it, so the rock is the end of a ridge. The two models are no longer drawn and stay only as the source of that shape and of the ship's sockets. `NOSE_FRONT` in `src/data/fortress.ts` fixes it, so the mountain stays put wherever the roads cross, and it names its two gates by side: `west` is the gate on the ship's nose side and `south` the other, and it throws unless one gate lies on each side. The rock reaches well past the site circle. The layer drops every earlier prop it would bury and types the raised ground outside the site margin as scree. Tests check that the walls and the rock close the outline, and that the roads and pads stay clear of the rock and the raised ground. A site's interior may run past its curtain only where the rock closes it, as the ship runs into the mountain.
- A fortress site has no `site-<id>` circle obstacle, and a fortress oasis has no pond obstacle. The pieces collide through their `prop-shapes.json` boxes in Rapier, the nav grid and sight. A wall stretches along its length to `2r`.
- `isFortress()` in `src/sim/sites.ts` names these sites. `isFree()` rejects the ground inside one, and discovery looks as far out as a pad is long, since the walls hide the inside.
- `groundClear()` in `src/sim/spawn.ts` is the one ground rule for a standing truck: no tile under its footprint may be a cliff. `isFree()` calls it, and so does `standsClear()` in `src/sim/far.ts`, so spawns, cheats, repairs and wakes all keep trucks off cliffs.
- `flattenFactor()` in `src/sim/elevation.ts` flattens the ground near roads, near sites and under every spot in `reservedGround()` in `src/sim/start-pose.ts`. Those spots are the start poses, the opening wrecks and the intro farm pieces. A start kit never stands in a hill, and `keepStartClear()` removes the props there.
- `buildSites()` fits each interior inside `insideCurtain()`. Abandoned sites keep their circle and edge.
- Broken Wing's hoop is a baked prop, `shipWing`, placed first by `shipWing()` in `oldworld.ts` from `TERRAIN.features.wing`. It is the wing's torn root, bent up and over the road. Its feet block like any ruin. Its boxes over the road start at least `truckClearance` + 1 m up, so physics, nav and sight leave the road under it open like a canopy. `BROKEN_WING` in `src/data/region.ts` places everything at Broken Wing: the straight road (map yaw 0), the hoop, the deck ends, the two sand mounds (`TERRAIN.features.mounds`, bowls with a negative depth, so road grading turns them into ramps), the crash trench (`TERRAIN.features.trench`, a channel) and the site.
- `src/sim/bridge.ts` holds the decks as a list, `DECKS`: Canyon Bridge, the Broken Wing deck and the Fallen Sun's wing and flaps (`FALLEN_SUN_DECKS` in `src/data/territory.ts`). One drivable surface is one deck: a deck's `line` runs through stations on its straight axis, each standing its `rise` over the ground, so the Fallen Sun's wing climbs, runs level and comes down as one deck. `deckSegments()` in `src/sim/terrain.ts` is the only source of a deck's straight pieces and their end heights, which slope, physics, models and pick quads follow. `buildDecks()` throws on decks whose outlines touch, so a surface is never split into decks whose seams every reader must find. A raised end is a lip: it blocks routes like a rail, physics gives it a skirt down to the ground, and a truck that drives off it flies. A prop box inside a deck outline whose top is under the deck line is out of reach (`underDeck()`), and `blockingBoxes()` drops it, so the wing rests on its hull drums without them blocking the deck. `src/three/render/ship-decks.ts` draws the wing and flap models on their decks, with a skirt down to the ground under each rail and lip. The map stays one level on every deck. `heightAt` in `src/sim/terrain.ts` returns the deck height on a deck, and `groundAt` returns the ground under it. Rails on both long edges block routes and physics, so trucks get on only over the ends. A deck with `cut` removes the road's flattening in its gap, as Canyon Bridge does over its canyon. The Broken Wing deck has no `cut` and has a `skirt`: its physics rails reach down past the lowest ground under them, so a truck on the ground cannot drive in under the slab. On screen the skirt is the lattice and pylons of `wing_deck`, drawn by `buildWingDeck` in `src/three/render/sites.ts`. Inside a deck outline the terrain mesh and scatter draw at least `DECK_FLOOR_GAP` under the deck (`deckFloorCap()` in `src/three/render/terrain.ts`), so abutment ground never pokes through the plates, while `groundAt` keeps the baked ground.
- A ramp prop is a baked prop whose model is in `RAMP_MODELS` in `src/sim/mapgen.ts`, today the `wing_shard` of the ship debris. It is no deck. `RAMP_MODELS` holds the slab's pitch and size, and `rampSpan()` turns them into the surface line from the toe on the ground to the tip. `propShape()` gives a ramp model the boxes of that shape instead of its `prop-shapes.json` boxes, so nav, sight, spawn and the bake all read the same ramp: thin side skirts along the slab and a lip across the tip, with the slab lane open from the low end. Physics builds the ramp in `rampParts()` in `src/phys/frames.ts`: a plate that counts as ground, side skirts and, where the slab is lower than truck clearance, a wall across the lane. A truck drives up the plate and a fast one leaves the tip. From the tall end it passes under the slab and stops at the wall. The lane is 6 m wide, so the nav clearance leaves it open only to straight-line checks, and a route round the slab is the fallback. `wing_shard.glb` has a root wedge that carries the slab line to the ground, so the toe matches the plate. A model that is a ramp is added to `RAMP_MODELS`, and its glb must carry the surface down to the ground.
- `src/sim/weather.ts` owns weather. `stormStrength` gives a storm's build-up and clearing over time from the turn it was born, and `stormDepth` multiplies it by the edge falloff. `weatherAt` is the settled weather at a place: the target for exposure, and the region heat that `src/sim/sun.ts` reads. A truck's saved `stormExposure` holds how far each storm has got into it, and `advanceExposure` steps it toward `stormDepth` by at most `1 / stormExposeTurns` a turn, at the end of `advanceWeather`. `weatherOn` turns that exposure into the effects every truck reads: sight, speed, aim spread, wear and the dust trail. `stormShare` gives the player's largest share, which sets the screen tint and the wind loop. `isOvercast()` is the one test of an overcast sky, read by the cloud shadows. The storm haze reads `stormStrength`, and only eases its steps between frames. `src/sim/sun.ts` owns time of day and sun heat, and `src/sim/engine-heat.ts` the player's engine heat. `src/sim/vision.ts` owns sight and fog of war, and `src/sim/locations.ts` discovery. `visibleTiles()` is the one computation of the player's seen tiles from a point. It casts `TERRAIN.vision.raysPerRimTile` rays per tile of the sight rim, and each ray walks out at `samplesPerTile` steps keeping the steepest ground slope so far. A tile takes the ray nearest its bearing and is hidden when the slope to its eye point is below that ray's horizon. Props stay exact: each tile tests `propHides()` only for the sight props whose footprint covers its ray. The fidelity test in `vision.test.ts` holds it to a line to every tile on the baked map: at most 0.5% of tiles differ, and only on visibility edges or where the line grazes the ground. NPC sight between two trucks and lines of fire keep the single exact line test.

## Charts and the map views

`src/sim/charts.ts` owns charts: the nearest chart town that sells each map grid square, the price and `buyChart()`. A chart is one square of the map grid: `gridSquareSpan()` in `src/sim/map-grid.ts` gives its whole tiles, where a tile belongs to the square that holds its origin, and `gridSquareArea()` gives its edges in map units for drawing on the grid's lines. A chart writes only `player.explored` and `player.discovered`, so it needs no save step. `siteArea()` in `src/sim/locations.ts` owns a site's area, the tiles within its outline, or within a pad length of a fortress wall, which sight discovery (`seesArea()`) and the map share. `mappedSites()` gives the sites with an explored tile in their area, and `mapSites()` marks only those, naming an unfound one `map.unknownSite`. `src/render/minimap-paint.ts` paints the whole map once per atlas key and a fog mask of bare paper that changes only where `explored` changed. `src/ui/map-geometry.ts` owns the viewport, which draws the map unturned, north up: point conversion, zoom around a point, the pan clamp and the edge pin. `src/ui/map-view.ts` draws the base and the fog through it for three views: the minimap follows the truck (`src/ui/minimap.ts`, built only while `src/ui/minimap-setting.ts` says on), the Map tab of the character screen zooms and pans (`src/ui/map-tab.ts`), and the town's Charts tab fits the whole map. The minimap and the Map tab exist only where `hasExplorationMap()` holds, which is every mode on the Icarus map and not Chaos Highway. The views read the world and never change it.
