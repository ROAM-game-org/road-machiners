# Movement, routes and travel

- `src/sim/ai.ts` issues NPC orders each turn and decides what NPCs drive around and when they stop for other trucks. `followsTraffic()` is true for NPCs and for the truck the player drives, and false for the Chaos Highway hauler, which keeps its lane rule in `hauler.ts`. Every `followsTraffic` truck on a route outside combat is covered by the rules below, the player included. `yieldsToTraffic()` is the player's brake: it holds the drive still for the turn when `trafficStops()` says so, and never touches the order, so automatic travel resumes. An NPC's yield is already its brake order. A moving truck counts as a conflict inside `RULES.traffic.crossCone` (parked trucks keep `parkedCone`), so a truck converging from the side is seen. They route around parked trucks and the path of a moving truck on a collision course. They stop only when that path blocks the way. Of two drivers closing on each other that both give way, the lower id goes around the other's swept path and the other holds its line (`givesWayTo()`), so they never swerve into each other. The lower id brakes only when the way around is blocked, much longer, or passes too close to the other truck. That last test follows the way around from its first point ahead of the driver, since a driver never backs up to a point behind it to start a pass. A stopped driver waits only for a closing truck whose path meets it. The roll counts a predicted moving truck only as a speed cap (`clearMoving`), and backs out only from solid clearance (`clearAhead`: props, cliffs, parked trucks and ground fields). A parked truck ahead makes them wait only while it holds a move order, since its order is what it will do. Far NPCs skip the moving-truck check, since far travel holds short of any truck. A stop order in either movement layer arrives at the route's end, which is the closest point the planner reaches, and a goal to a point ends when its move arrives. `noteStuck()` is the catch-all: an NPC that stays put for `RULES.unstick.turns` turns with its goal point out of reach drives to a random free spot nearby. A driver braking for its escort to catch up is waiting, not stuck, so it never counts toward the unstick wait. Recovery backing goes through `backsToDestination()`, which holds the player's cone and reach for recovering NPCs, so the driver goes nose first to a spot outside the cone.
- `src/sim/steering.ts` holds the shared driving rules, the route speed rule, the throttle zones of manual driving and `clickOrder()`, which turns a click into an order.
- `src/phys/` runs vehicle movement in Rapier. `endTurn(world, physicsMove(...))` plugs it into the turn pipeline in place of the sim's 2D movement. A turn restores the physics world from a snapshot and simulates one second. Every Rapier step goes through `stepWorld()` in `src/phys/snapshot.ts`, which skips Rapier's per-step remap of every handle, since all of ROAM's creates and removes keep the map themselves. The path preview runs the same physics as the turn. `planTurns()` in `src/phys/plan.ts` is the preview: up to three turns of a world clone and the route on to the order point. `PlanPreparation` in `src/three/plan-preview.ts` runs it in its own instance of the turn worker, so a preview never freezes a frame or waits behind a turn. It keeps only the newest request and hands an answer to `game.ts` only for the world it was asked for, and the old path stays until the new one lands. The turn hands the renderer one pose per physics step. Playback shows the pose at the exact play time with `frameAt()` in `src/phys/frames.ts`, which blends the two nearest steps, so a truck moves evenly at any frame rate. Crashes and prop breaks still fire on whole steps. `src/sim/clearance.ts` owns the body footprint test. At turn start `planTurn()` builds a `Clearance` from the solid props, the route blockers and the cliffs, and the driver rolls its own pure-pursuit steering forward with it to cap speed and to back out of a nose-on contact until the way is clear. The roll also checks `meetsMoving()` against each live truck that is not the driver's ram target or combat foe, at where that truck will be by then plus `PHYSICS.driver.moverSpread` per second, so a truck that turns into the driver mid-turn is braked for. Manual driving skips it. Physics numbers live in `src/data/physics.ts`. `rideOf()` in `src/sim/body.ts` gives the collider center's height over flat ground at rest, and each look's `comHeight` puts the center of mass at a fixed height over the ground, as `comY` in the collider frame, so a bigger tire lifts the body without lifting its weight. Wheel side friction is set per step in `applyTerrainGrip()` from the ground and `rearSideGrip`, so the rear tires hold less than the front. `applyDriven()` in `src/phys/turn.ts` owns the rescue of a body that physics holds. It counts `strandedTurns` for a truck that ends a turn flipped, lifted, or hung: holding a move order that did not arrive or pass, moved less than `RULES.stranded.still` tiles, with a wheel off the ground. After `RULES.stranded.turns` such turns in a row, `setDownSpot()` in `src/sim/steering.ts` places it on the nearest free ground. A braked, nearly stopped truck gets a brake hold in `holdStill()` in `drive.ts`: after the controller and the harpoon pull each step it removes the slide along the ground and the yaw spin, and pulls back to an anchor kept in drive memory, capped by the ground grip, so a truck holds on any slope its tires grip and slides past that. Trucks with no brakes are not held.
- `src/sim/path.ts` plans routes and caches them. `src/sim/nav/` holds the grids and the search. Grids are built once per terrain and vehicle radius. `warmRoutes` builds them for every chassis in the turn worker when it gets the terrain. The preview worker builds a grid the first time a preview needs it. The main thread routes only for a tow deal the player opens, so it builds that radius's grid then. Wrecks and parked vehicles are stamped per query. Long routes search a coarse corridor first. Each NPC driver scales route cost by its own smooth noise field, so drivers between the same points take different ways. `keepsOffRoads()` in `src/sim/off-road.ts` is the one owner of which drivers keep off roads: raiders that retreat, flee or are stranded, and raiders on a raid, a patrol or an investigation (`huntsOffRoad()` in `src/sim/hunt-style.ts`), so they travel unseen by road traffic. `route()` asks it and plans with an off-road taste (`offRoadTaste()` in `src/sim/nav/layer.ts`) that multiplies road tiles away from sites by `REGION.navigation.roadShyCost`. The multiplier falls linearly to 1 at `REGION.navigation.roadShyBand` tiles from the nearest road tile, from a per-terrain distance field in `TerrainNav.roadShy`, so the tile beside the road costs more than open ground and routes keep a band away from it. It is a cost, never a block, so such a route still crosses a road where it must. The route cache key tells the two tastes apart. A kept route, in `continueRoute()` and in far travel's `farRoute`, records whether it was planned off roads, and is planned again once the driver's style changes. Each NPC keeps its own spot on the pad it uses, so drivers do not stack. A driver that `avoidsCrater()` in `src/sim/sunward.ts` holds is planned with the night taste: `nightTaste()` in `src/sim/nav/layer.ts` multiplies the cost of every tile in the Fallen Sun's night zone by `SUNWARD.avoidCost`, from a mask built once per terrain. It is a cost, never a block. A kept route and far travel's `farRoute` record the night style and are planned again when it changes. Every driver, the player included, plans with a climb limit: `climbGrade()` in `src/sim/stats.ts` is the steepest grade its truck climbs with its current load, from the same constants as `climbForce()` in `src/phys/drive.ts` times `PHYSICS.truck.climbPlanShare`, and `route()` rounds it down to `REGION.navigation.climbStep`. `climbTaste()` in `src/sim/nav/layer.ts` multiplies the cost of every tile steeper than that by `REGION.navigation.overClimbCost` (`TerrainNav.steep` holds the steepness), and the coarse corridor search scales each step by the share of such tiles in its blocks. It is a cost, never a block, so components and reachability do not change and a truck in a true pit still takes the cheapest wall. A kept route is planned again at the next stop, when the load can change. `watchStalls()` in `src/sim/npc-watchdog.ts` counts progress as a new goal or job, or a move of more than `NPC_BEHAVIOR.progressReach` tiles from `brain.progress.at`, so a truck that swings back and forth against a wall raises a `stall` and takes a fresh goal. Deck rails and lips (`Deck.rails`, `Deck.lips` in `src/sim/bridge.ts`) block routes like walls (`nearRail`, `crossesRail`), so a route gets onto a deck only over an end on the ground, and never plans a jump off a lip.
- Oil patches and harpoon lines reach physics only at turn start: `src/phys/drive.ts` reads `oilPatches()` from `src/sim/hazards.ts` and `lineAnchors()` from `src/sim/harpoon.ts`, and the steps never read the sim. A wheel whose hub is over oil loses grip. A stretched line pulls both bodies, and a tear comes back in the turn result for `tearLine()`. A shut-down truck gets no engine force from `vehicleStats()` and coasts. `fieldBlockers()` gives every route planner, the player's and NPCs', the ground fields its driver sees or dropped, as parked obstacles.
- NPCs farther than sight radius plus `PERF.liveMargin` from the player have no physics body. `src/sim/far.ts` moves them along stored routes. A stored route holds while its stop point moves less than `RULES.arriveRadius`, so a parking spot that shifts a little with the truck's approach never makes it plan again and turn back. They never crash or ram, but they hold short of any other vehicle, so two trucks never share a point. A truck on a tow rope has no body, so it does not block the tower's route. A truck held behind another drops to that truck's speed, not to a stop. A new far route steers around parked trucks and around moving trucks slower than its own top speed within a turn's drive, so a far truck overtakes slow traffic as a driver would.
- `src/sim/fidelity.ts` owns the cheap far rule. A meeting where neither point lies within sight radius plus `PERF.liveMargin` of the player plays by cheap rules: `canVehicleSee()` is the sight radius alone with no props, hills or dust screens, `cloudsSeenBy()` skips risen clouds seen over hills, and heat and the shade search treat the ground as open sun. A meeting with the player always uses the full rules. `setHeadless()` is a process-level switch the progression recorder sets. It counts every truck as far, so the player bot meets the cheap rules too and keeps its far route between turns. It is never saved on the World.
- `src/sim/prop-index.ts` indexes obstacles for sight lines, shade rays and breakable props on far walks. It is keyed by each obstacle's id and place, since `update()` clones the world every turn. Route planning builds its static set the same way in `src/sim/nav/layer.ts`.

## Player travel

`src/three/travel.ts` owns automatic order advancement and held-Space state. A click sets a drive-through order. A click on its point switches it between drive-through and stop-at, and Shift-click sets stop-at. A click on a site stops at its nearest pad. `src/three/render/path.ts` draws the order point with an icon for its kind, also while turns play.

Space starts automatic travel outside combat. Its `TurnPreparation` prepares a turn in the dedicated worker entry point `src/phys/turn-worker.ts` while the current turn plays. Only that entry calls `startTurnWorker()`. `src/phys/turn.ts` exports and starts nothing on import, since `plan.ts` imports it and the dev server would otherwise load it twice in a worker and start two Rapier instances. `initPhysics()` hands every caller one Rapier init. The worker uses the same turn pipeline and portable physics snapshots. Each turn request carries the active quest language, and the worker sets it before the turn, so a scene the turn opens plays in the player's language. At boot it runs one warm-up turn and drops it, so the first real turn finds its grids, compiled code and routes ready. `game.ts` commits a prepared turn only after playback finishes and while advancement is still requested. Replanning invalidates stale results. Travel pauses on Space, danger, arrival, panels and focus loss. Danger is combat, a new hostile in sight, a crash into a truck and the player's `hazard` or `core` note, entering a reactor's drain zone or its core, as [turns](../wiki/mechanics/turns.md) lists. `updateWorld()` also lets go of a held Space on those notes, so a hold never plays across either edge. Knocked-out, towed and beacon turns use the same chaining: the next turn is prepared during playback and begins in the frame the last one ends, with the leftover time carried over. Panels, a focused text control and a hidden tab hold them back. While turns run on their own, the turn button reads Auto. A tap of Space or a click stops them, and a hold fast-forwards them. Travel state is not saved.

## Camera

`src/three/render/camera.ts` owns the view. The canvas fills `#game`: `Game.fitView()` sizes the renderer from `#game` on window resize and on any change to its size, and the camera measures the canvas for its frustum, rays, `screenOf()` and pans, so the drawn picture and the tap ray share one box. `TruckFollow` decides when it follows the truck. A pan stops following until F, the recenter button, danger in sight, or the truck driving out of view. F and the recenter button put the truck at the screen center by dropping the look-ahead, which grows back while turns play in auto mode. Danger in sight and the truck leaving the view resume following and keep the look-ahead. The view moves only while a turn plays. In auto mode it leads toward the order point. On touch, `src/three/touch-map.ts` turns the canvas's touch pointers into intents: a drag of more than 10px pans through `TruckFollow.pan`, the same as a right-drag, and two fingers zoom by the ratio of their distances through `CameraRig.zoomByFactor`, which holds the same limits as the wheel. A tap acts at the point where the finger pressed, and a hold of 500ms sets a stop point, the touch twin of Shift-click. A first tap on a truck only pins its card, and `src/three/touch-tap.ts` decides between showing and acting.
