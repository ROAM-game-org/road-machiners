# NPCs, combat and deals

## Behavior

NPC behavior has three layers. Traits in `brain.traits` are permanent and replace classes. A goal stack in `brain.goals` keeps long-term goals under interruptions. Decision points pick reactions by weighted chance with world RNG. A weight of 0 is only for an option the driver physically cannot take or its traits forbid. Each trait's `robs` field forbids robbing always or while the driver follows a leader. Every available option gets at least `MIN_CHANCE`.

- A world's mode rules gate behavior through `modeRules()` in `src/sim/settings.ts`. With `yielding` off, `optionWeights()` drops every option in `YIELD_OPTIONS` and the give-up, back-off and service steps skip, so a Chaos Highway group truck fights until it gives up at the mode's nerve share, and a fighter with no gun rams. `src/sim/fury-road.ts` spawns the groups from their cards, paces them by place and renews their feud and track on the player each turn. `src/sim/fury-road-director.ts` plans each stretch's groups, sizes, gear money and elites. `bothInWaves()` in `src/sim/combat.ts` keeps group trucks from turning on each other. `runsDown()` in `src/sim/hunters.ts` makes a group truck drive through to its hunting point, and `holdOnStretch()` bounds every order of it to the stretch between the window's two forts.
- `src/sim/npc-decisions.ts` owns trait profiles, option availability, the weighted rolls and robbery checks. `tradeSpend()` is the one budget of a trade load: the wallet above the upkeep reserve, capped by the trait `tradeStake`. A spawned driver's wallet is its template's `money` in `src/data/npcs.ts`, and traits live in `src/data/npc-traits.ts`.
- `spawnRoom()` in `src/sim/spawn.ts` is the one test of where a new leader may appear: free ground, `SPAWN.minPlayerDist` from the player and fewer than `SPAWN.crowd.most` trucks within `SPAWN.crowd.reach`. `spawnRequired()` walks a driver's ordered places and throws only when all fail. Escorts check only the player's distance. `firstTimer()` derives each template's first respawn phase from the template table, so templates of one interval never fire together and `spawnTimer` stays empty in a new world.
- `fightOdds()` in `src/sim/fight-odds.ts` owns how a fight between two trucks or groups goes: the win odds, with the faster truck picking the sides, and the loser's odds to get away. It walks rounds with `sidePlanner()`, so it follows the real damage rules. Danger in `npc-decisions.ts`, escape XP, the progression bots and the spawn gear score all read it.
- `src/sim/haul.ts` owns what a deal hands over. `ransomOf()` is the one price of a hold-up: the `cargoHaul()` the robber can load and its value, or with no haul a cash cut of the victim's money above `getUpkeepReserve()`, `cashCut()`, which `RULES.holdUp` caps. `cargoHaul()` and `surrenderHaul()` list what an NPC winner can stow now, `handedOver()` in `parley.ts` picks the whole load for a player winner, and `dropHaul()` in `salvage.ts` drops exactly that on its own pile. The radio line names it through the `haul` call var and its effect throws if the haul changed.
- `holdsUp()` in `npc-decisions.ts` owns the hold-up rule: a robber that wants the prey's loot, is not weak or outgunned and has a ransom from the prey answers a truce with its demand. `answersPlea()` and `plead()` in `parley.ts` call it. `yieldTo()` drops cargo and `payOff()` moves M's through `transfer()`, and both end in one private settlement that makes peace, writes the `robbed` memory, credits Bounty talk and raises the `holdUp` event. `giveInTo()` is the NPC victim's rule: pay when its money above the reserve covers a named price, else drop the cargo. It draws no random number.
- `src/sim/look-over.ts` owns the look-over for every truck: `canLookOver()`, the `lookOver` and `sizedUp` states, `knowsCargo()` and `perceivedLoad()`, the one way a robbery choice reads a load. Without a look-over a robber reads the visible hold times the mean crate value times `LOOK_OVER.guessShare`. It also owns the robber's `sizeUp` goal and its `sizedUp` decision, the start of an NPC robbery and hold-up, and the looked-over driver's `lookedOver` reaction. NPC look-overs start and reactions roll in `resolveNpcActivities()`, at the end of the turn, so an NPC look-over takes one turn of play like the player's. The radio warning is the `lookWarning` noticed key and topic. `holdsUp()` and `demandsOnTop()` need `knowsCargo()`.
- `holdsOffRobbery()` in `npc-decisions.ts` owns the stranded robbery rule: option availability, wanting loot, and the give-up step in `thinkNpc()` all call it.
- `src/sim/npc-activities.ts` owns the goal stack and fires the decision points in `thinkNpc()`.
- Site business is a `business` job (`NPC_UPKEEP.businessTurns`). `dealDue()` in `npc-business.ts` starts it when a driver reaches the pad of a resupply, sell, trade or haul goal, and the resolver runs the deal on the turn the job ends `done`. Moving, combat or a new top goal cancels it like any job, and the deal waits for a fresh job. Retreat refits are not business.
- `src/sim/tracks.ts` owns `brain.tracks`: every truck a driver sees or detects, where and when it last sensed it, whether it has seen it, and its choice on it as a hostile. `senseTracks()` in `thinkNpc()` is the one place sensing becomes a track, from the same pass over the trucks that gives the contacts. A truck seen and then only heard keeps its track. A driver decides on a hostile once per track, by sight or sound. A choice made on a sound alone still lets the sighting roll. A truck it ran from it runs from again on sight and never goes to look at. A fight chases its target to the track's last point. New features that need to know a nearby truck read its track.
- `onContactSpotted()` in `npc-activities.ts` ends an investigation whose driver sees its target. The track holds no choice made in sight, so the sighting rolls like any new hostile in sight.
- `steerInvestigate()` in `npc-activities.ts` leads an investigation with `ledPoint()`: the goal's `heard` is the raw centre it heard last turn (saved, so a led destination never feeds the next lead), and the destination is the new centre plus the drift beyond the contact circle, capped at the investigator's `maxSpeed`. `interceptOf()` in `ai.ts` gives a fighter that sees its target beyond its longest gun range the point the target reaches, at most `maxSpeed` ahead. `runsDown()` trucks keep their own approach.
- `src/sim/npc-repair.ts` picks the part, the shady spot and the jobs for field repair goals.
- `src/sim/hunt-style.ts` owns the route style of hunting raiders. `huntsOffRoad()` holds for the `raider` trait with a top goal in `HUNT.offRoadGoals`, and `keepsOffRoads()` in `src/sim/off-road.ts` reads it, so the route planner gives it the off-road taste. A new goal that should keep off the road joins that list rather than a second mechanism.
- `src/sim/watch-posts.ts` owns watch posts and the watch. `postsOf()` maps hunting grounds and camp patrol stops to hides, built once per terrain and cached by its identity, so a map always gives the same posts. A hide is off the road within a dash of its ground, and the pick is the least road exposure (the road sample points within day sight that it has a line of sight to), then a spot out of sight of the ground, then the nearest ring. A road ground so gets a spot that hills, props or distance hide from road traffic where the terrain allows. `passReason()` in `npc-decisions.ts` owns why a hunting raider lets prey pass, and the sighting roll and the hover both read it. A raid ends with a watch of `HUNT.watchTurns` at its post, shorter than the stall limit, and `isWatching()` marks a raider on watch, whose investigation reads "spring on prey it heard". `raiderGrounds()` and `raiderPatrolPosts()` in `npc-decisions.ts` give a camp's posts and throw when it has none. A watch is a goal phase, not `workOf()` work, so it shows no progress bar that would give the ambush away.
- `src/sim/npc-service.ts` owns service at a site and the `rearm` lie-up. It also owns NPC fuel judgment: `fuelForWay()` is the one reserve formula, `isLowOnFuel()` adds the leg check at a pump, and `canTow()` in `tow.ts` reads it for NPC towers. `fitToHunt()` in `npc-decisions.ts` is the one test of a raider's readiness. A driver that only fresh gear can make fit, defeated, stranded or a raider unfit to hunt, lies up at a site that serves it until its `until` turn, the template's `cap × interval`. The `rearm` goal's destination is its lie-up spot, a random free point off the pads beyond the site's edge, and `liesUp()` holds while the driver is at it. `refitAtHome()` in `defeat.ts` runs only when a lie-up ends.
- A scavenger that chose to scavenge heads for an old-world loot spot within `OLD_PLACES.npcRange` tiles at `OLD_PLACES.npcShare`, uniformly among them, in `siteGoal()`. Old spots are known fixed places like territory spots, so `scavengeTargetInvalid()` keeps the goal out of sight, and a driver learns a spot is empty only on arrival.
- A scavenge goal at a territory targets one loot spot stock, not the territory, and a trip to one ends at the road end that enters it, never a pad. Hunting grounds hold a ring inside each territory. No NPC goal destination lies in a hazard zone, since `src/sim/nav/layer.ts` blocks zones for routes and the grounds lie outside them.
- `src/data/npcs.ts` holds traits, decision weights, state durations, thresholds and weighted spawn equipment tables.
- `src/sim/npc-loadout.ts` rolls each NPC a gear level from `GEAR_LEVELS`, from poor to loaded, then a chassis from the template table, using world RNG. `pickGear()` then buys parts one step at a time from every part in the game, limited by the level's gear money and judged by `gearScore()` in `src/sim/npc-gear-score.ts`. A template table in `src/data/npcs.ts` holds level odds, chassis odds, the four priorities, cargo parts, goods and spares. Gear money does not spend driver wallets.
- `chooseVehicle()` in `src/sim/npc-loadout.ts` rolls one utility per truck from `NPC_UTILITY_PARTS` in `src/data/npc-utilities.ts`, keyed by template. `src/sim/npc-utility.ts` picks NPC utility orders each turn. Its rules only choose orders, and each order passes the player's checks in `src/sim/utility.ts`.
- NPCs know fixed places but perceive current vehicles only through their own sight and detection. `src/sim/detect.ts` gives player and NPCs the same sound, dust and scanner contacts.
- `src/sim/encounters.ts` owns roadside encounters: the `strangerSeen` roll, the pending slot, the pacing in `player.encounters` and the courier story across meetings. It is the only writer of `player.encounters`, and a test enforces it. Topics, conditions and effects stay in `src/data/dialogue.ts` and `src/sim/dialogue-rules.ts` and call the aid, parley, combat and economy owners. `src/sim/rumors.ts` picks the site, wreck and trading tip a driver can tell.
- `src/sim/memory.ts` owns driver memories in `brain.memories`: the subject rule, `remember()`, `recall()` and the daily expiry in `forgetOld()`, run every turn. It is the only writer, and a test enforces it. A new memory kind adds a `MemoryFact` member, a lifetime in `MEMORY.turns` and a save step, and lands with the code that reads it. The `wronged` kind marks a truck that attacked or robbed the driver, written by `noteDeed()` for a calm attack or robbery and by `yieldTo()`, with its deed. Every thinking driver also remembers each driver it sees as a `sighting` (position, heading and turn) through `rememberSightings()`, one per truck, for a day. Talk about prices reads only memories, never live shops: `tradeTip()` and `lastTownMemory()` in `src/sim/dialogue-rules.ts` give the trading tip and the Market ears prices.
- `src/sim/sunward.ts` owns the Sunward order: its facts in `world.sunward`, the night clock (`phaseAt()`, `duskTurn()`, `returnTurn()`, `dawnAfter()`), the dusk roster and the hunt roll, the Hollow's derived props and bays, the members' goals and the night ledger and the collapse. Every member runs the whole `thinkNpc()` pipeline, but `planSunward()` in place of the service rules sets its base goal by the clock: rest at its bay, ride in the `cavalcade` column, hold its vigil post, hunt, drag or ride home. `gateOrder()` ranks tonight's roster by bay, the gate's first, and the column is a chain: each car follows the car ahead of it, not the leader, and waits in its bay until the car ahead is past the bays, then drives to the apron outside the gate before it follows. The home ride leaves the posts in the same order. `vigilPosts()` gives one free post per car on a ring `SUNWARD.postGap` outside the drain zone, cached per terrain, and throws when fewer than eight fit. A car holding its post (`holdsPost()`) counts as progress for the watchdog. A `cavalcade` follower never holds its leader back: `waitsForEscort()` in `ai.ts` waits only for the `AWAITED_KINDS` of `companies.ts`, escorts, crews and hires. A defeated member drives home to its bay instead of retreating to a town, never jumps home, and is refit in its bay by day. `noteDefeat()` in `relations.ts` hands every defeat to `noteSunwardDefeat()`, which counts a Cavalcade car once a night when the player's company beat it, and `collapseOrder()` turns every member into an independent roamer. `hashRandom()` of the seed and the day picks hunt nights, so no world stream moves.
- `src/sim/sunward-crater.ts` owns the order's business at the crater: the meeting (`meetStrangers()` gives the nearest free car at its post that has a fresh track of a stranger in the Fallen Sun a `meet` goal with reason `meetStranger` over its post, and `meetingLapsed()` ends it after `SUNWARD.meetTurns` or when the stranger leaves or is bound), the vigil calls (`vigilCallDue()`, which also holds for the call on the line, the call count in `world.sunward.night.calls`), the words given tonight in `world.sunward.night.offered`, the road drivers' accounts (`knowsTheCrater()` and `craterNews()`, which picks a driver's account from `CRATER_ACCOUNTS` by `hashId()` of its id), the `offering` state a truck enters by giving its word, `seize()` (a feud and a fight goal for every member in sight, and one incident), NPC strangers' `vigilCalled` answers, the drop spot in the drain ring and the drag's ending at the light, which holds a truce with every member until dawn.
- `tow.ts` owns the `drag` state too: a rope like a hitched tow, so `followTower()`, `isOnRope()` and the far and physics layers move the victim behind its dragger. `startDrag()` throws unless the victim is knocked out by the dragger's side or stranded in a fight with it. An awake victim cuts free with `unhitch()`, and an awake NPC victim breaks the drag at once. While the order stands, `avoidsCrater()` in `sunward.ts` makes every other driver give up a goal in the crater zone at night (`shunsAtNight()`), leave it (`nightSafe()`) and plan routes with the night taste.
- A driver never waits without an end. `watchStalls()` gives up the top goal of a driver with no new tile, job turn or goal for `NPC_BEHAVIOR.stallTurns` turns, and sends an idle one to explore. Each give-up logs a `stall` event, which is always a bug, and `npm run stuck` and every recording fail on it.

## States and hostility

`src/sim/states.ts` holds timed states between two vehicles, like a feud or a tow. A state ends as expired, fulfilled or broken, and its hook for that ending runs once.

- `src/sim/relations.ts` owns attitude. `attitude()` is what one driver thinks of another truck, from feuds and truces, the faction table and the player's standing in `src/data/npc-behavior.ts`, the driver's memories of deeds, and the law role. `bandOf()`, `isAlly()`, `isOutlaw()` and `isRaider()` are the only faction and role tests. `isHostile()` in `src/sim/combat.ts` composes `attacks()` and `preys()` from it, so a feud, the law, a grudge or a raider's prey makes trucks hostile, and a truce cancels it. [Factions and relations](../wiki/mechanics/factions.md) has the rules.
- `reportIncident()` in `src/sim/relations.ts` is the one witness rule. A calm attack, robbery start, refused demand or crash retaliation calls it. It sets the victim, its escorts and the witnesses who step in on the attacker, and calls `noteDeed()` once. `noteDeed()` writes the victim's memory through `remember()` and the player's standing. It is the only writer of `player.standing` besides `driftStanding()` at each day change, and a test holds that.
- A crash between trucks at peace starts no feud. It leaves the NPC victim a grievance, which it forgives or answers with a feud.
- A `combat` state runs from an aggressor to its target. `recordAttack()` and `noteEngagements()` (fight goals in sight) in `src/sim/combat.ts` start or refresh it, it lasts `STATE_TURNS.combat` turns, and it breaks once the two trucks are no longer hostile. `inCombat()` and `inCombatWithOther()` in `src/sim/combat.ts` are the one test for whether a driver is in combat. A hostile in sight is a warning only, so no other code decides combat from sight.
- A state kind that `binds` is a deal. `boundTo()` and `givesWord()` in `src/sim/states.ts` answer who gave whom its word. The two sides never rob each other, and `canRob()` also refuses a truce partner. The holder starts no decision `src/sim/npc-decisions.ts` marks as a venture, except about its partner. An attack by a partner gives the victim revenge.

## Combat

`src/sim/combat.ts` runs simultaneous fire and decides hostility. `src/sim/armor.ts` walks a round through the part grid, and its `gunSpans()` gives the angles a mounted gun can fire through: its arc minus the slice each tall cell hides from the gun's center. `vehicleStats()` carries them on each weapon, and auto-mounting picks deck spots that keep the most open. `src/sim/crash-contact.ts` owns crash damage and `src/sim/defeat.ts` knockouts. A broken cab knocks out the player and NPCs alike, and `RULES.npcDeathChance` turns an NPC into a wreck instead. Two mode flags gate it: `npcKnockouts` and `playerKnockouts`. Chaos Highway keeps NPC knockouts and ends the run on a player knockout, and with `looting` off nobody loots a knocked-out truck (`canLootTruck()`, `downedListNear()`). A knocked-out NPC is nobody's foe until it retreats home and refits at the end of its lie-up, and `defeat.ts` owns that retreat. In `thinkNpc()` a defeated driver whose top goal is a deal goal (`meet` or `patch`) acts on it before `retreatHome()`. `src/sim/stats.ts` is the one source for speed, turning and capacity. Working guns draw power from the engine's capacity and slow the truck, see `gunDrag()`.

Utilities and the claymore ram:

- `src/sim/utility.ts` owns utility charge, order checks, the `activateUtilities()` step before `fireWeapons()`, reload ticks, `hasWorkingUtility()` and `workTimeMult()`. Its last section owns the emitter shutdown: `settleShutdowns()` writes the window at the end of the pulse turn, and `isShutDown()` is the one test that stats, fire, detection, engine heat and the HUD ask.
- `src/sim/hazards.ts` owns smoke clouds, ground fields and flares. `smokeCrosses()` gives the `smoke` spread cause in `combat.ts`, `litAt()` gives flare light to `vision.ts`, and `flareSightings()` feeds `detect.ts`, which builds the contacts.
- `src/sim/harpoon.ts` owns harpoon lines. The harpoon is a gun with `line` in its def: the fire phase in `combat.ts` fires it, and a round that strikes its target calls `attachLine()`. NPCs roll it with their utilities in `npc-loadout.ts`.
- `src/sim/claymore.ts` owns arming, the blast on a truck crash, which `crash-contact.ts` hands it, and `settleClaymores()`, the one place that disarms a broken or moved charge.
- Smoke, fields, flares and lines are saved world objects with `turnsLeft`. `advanceUtilityEffects()` ages them each turn. Render only reads them.

## Dialogue and deals

- `src/sim/dialogue.ts` runs radio calls and the horn. Topics live in `src/data/dialogue.ts` as lines and replies that name conditions, effects and prepare steps in `src/sim/dialogue-rules.ts`. A new topic is data plus any new named rule. `talkOf()` is the one place talk reads traits and personality. A trait talk marked `only`, like the Sunward `cultist`, is the whole talk of a driver that has it, so an extra trait adds no topic to it.
- A personality is how open a driver is on the radio. It is not a trait. A trait is a behavior class like trader or raider. `rollPersonality()` in `src/sim/spawn.ts` draws one for each driver at spawn with the world RNG, and it is saved in `brain.personality`. A personality changes talk only, never decisions, combat, trade or movement.
- `src/data/personalities.ts` sets a gate for each information topic: directions, market news, the site tip and the trading tip. An open topic is answered. A closed topic is not offered. A price topic first asks `TALK_PRICE` and answers after the player pays. A help topic is held back until the player has helped that driver with aid, a tow or a patch, which `helpedByPlayer()` reads from `player.talked`.
- `src/sim/parley.ts` owns what truces, mercy and threats do, for NPCs and the player alike. It also owns the stripping of a stranded player: a robber alone with one radios the `surrender` strip offer once, any other driver radios the plain `giveUp` offer that takes nothing, and `aimAt()` sends its shots at the cab after a refusal. It also owns pile claims: an NPC handed a pile claims it, and `claimSpill()` lets a robber in the fight claim cargo spilled by the `spillDeadRows()` sweep in `src/sim/spill.ts`, with the `spillClaim` topic for a player victim. A claimant warns other drivers off through `warnTruck()` and the `claim` topic, and fights a refusal or a take in its sight. `complyFactor()` in `npc-decisions.ts` scales the comply weight of `threatened` and `warnedOff` for every threatener alike: up by `threatComply` against a threat, and down by `guardedComply` while an awake follower from `followersOf()` is in sight. The claim's data and lapse live in `src/sim/salvage.ts`, and a claimant starts no venture while it holds one.
- `src/sim/dialogue-rules.ts` owns the rules settlement talk reads: which locals a town has, the journal, and the board contract talk offers with `townWork()` and takes through `takeContract()`. Locals and notes are data in `src/data/locals.ts`. What locals say is ink in `src/data/quests/`, run by `src/sim/quests.ts`. See [Quests](quests.md). `src/sim/dialogue-rules.ts` is the only writer of `player.notes`, for locals and radio topics alike. Story wrecks are `STORY_WRECKS` in `src/data/salvage.ts`, placed after every seeded placement with no random draw.
- `src/sim/loot-warning.ts` owns arguments over loot for every pair of trucks. `heldByLooter()` runs when a driver parked on a loot goal finds another truck looting the target: `contestLoot()` rolls `lootContested` once while the looter stays in sight (`lootContested:<id>` noticed on both sides). Every warning goes through `warnTruck()`, which adds a `lootWarning` state, and every NPC answer through the `warnedOff` decision. `settleLootWarning()` applies the answer, and an NPC warner rolls `warnRefused` on a refusal. The player answers through the `lootWarning` topic and warns through `warnOff`. A `comply` state keeps the warned truck off the target, so `looterOf()` skips a warned-off player, and a player that takes from it breaks the state. `passesUpLoot()` is the rule `lootTaken()` reads: taken loot stays visible until the driver argued with its looter. NPC-to-NPC arguments push a `lootArgument` event.
- `src/sim/lessons.ts` owns the `roadLesson` pick: `truckWeaknesses()` asks the owners (`bareSides()` in `armor.ts`, `firepower()`, `canTakeWornPatch()`, `vehicleMass()`) and `pickLesson()` hashes the world seed and the driver's id, so it draws no RNG. `src/data/lessons.ts` holds the trades, the voiced traits that map to them and the 35 line slots. The prepare step saves the picked line in a `line` call var.
- Deals that outlive a call, like a tow or a patch, are states in `src/sim/states.ts`, and their fulfilled hook pays once.
- `src/sim/tow.ts` owns tows: NPCs tow the player and each other, and the player tows NPCs for a fee or for free. An NPC's offer to a broke player is free (fee 0, decided at the offer, with its own `towFree` radio topic). Raiders tow only raiders, and only raiders or the player tow a raider. A truck on a tow rope has no physics body and is pulled by its tower on a tow bar: it rolls on its rear wheels, cuts a little inside on bends and takes up the slack after hitching. An NPC tower's tow ends when its client can use the site, see `runTow()`. The tower stops on the pad's center line for it. The player as tower ends on the player's own position (`checkPlayerTow()`).
- A tower's `answering` claim has a state timer of 20 turns. It holds while the tower cannot see its client or the client is in combat, and `lapseClaim()` logs `towDropped` with reason `blocked` on expiry. `towInvalid()` then drops the tower's goal. `src/sim/meeting-stop.ts` picks the parking spot beside a truck or stock, and goes around a truck that stands on the approach side.
- `escort-job.ts` is the player side of escorts. A trader posts an offer from `onEscortWanted()` in `escort-job.ts` (the `escortWanted` decision, rolled once per trade trip) and waits with a `wait` goal of reason `awaitEscort`. A trader led by a player escort reads `traderWaits()` in `ai.ts`, and the watchdog treats both waits as progress. Hold and go are radio topics on the trader.
- `tow.ts` also owns escorts: an `escort` state makes its holder follow the other truck, fight its attackers, tow it when stranded and collect the fee on arrival. The `follow` goal is the reusable group activity. It holds while its driver keeps any following state toward the leader, so new group work adds a state kind, not a goal. Mercs are hired through the `escortSeen` and `hireOffered` decisions, and convoy guards hold a free escort with no destination.
- `directionsTo(world, from, target)` in `src/sim/directions.ts` is the one way to tell where a place is, for any caller. The caller decides what the speaker knows and passes a `site` target, a place known by name, or a `spot` target, a rough place like a wreck. It returns ids only, as `DirectionStep`s: `join`, `follow`, `turn`, `leave`, `straight`, `arrive`, `search`, `askAgain` and `noRoad`, each with road names, a side, a cue (site, bridge, tall prop, fork count) and a span. A `follow` step also carries its `heading`, the octant of the first leg's chord over `DIRECTIONS.headingReach` tiles from where the route meets the road, from `headingAlong()` in `direction-cues.ts`. It is null only in an answer saved before headings. `src/sim/direction-cues.ts` picks cues, and its `countable()` owns the fork count rule (third fork at most, junctions at least `cueReach` apart, otherwise no cue and the answer ends in `askAgain`): a prop look is never named when another of its look stands on the leg before it. The planner draws no random numbers and never reveals a spot closer than an octant and a span from a road. `src/ui/directions-text.ts` turns the steps into words. The answer is planned when the call opens and saved with the call, so a load reads the same words.
- `src/sim/companies.ts` owns following states: `escort`, `crew` and `hire`. `joinCompany()` is their one writer and throws on a second following state for one truck or on a loop, and `checkCompanies()` checks the same at load. A company is the tree of following states, and its leader is the truck at the root, so the player escorting a trader with a hired merc forms trader, player, merc. `followersOf()`, `followingOf()`, `companyOf()`, `sameCompany()` and `combatStatesOf()` read an index built once per `world.states` array and cached by that array, so every write replaces the array: `addState()` and `endState()` do, and a test fails on an in-place write or on a following state added outside `joinCompany()`. `inCombat()` and the follower queries in `tow.ts`, `pairs.ts` and `ai.ts` read the index.
- `src/sim/sides.ts` owns who stands with whom in an encounter. `perceivedGroup()` is what an observer sees of a truck's group: the truck, its company, its allies within `SPAWN.neighborHelp` and any truck in combat with one of its foes that is hostile to none of its company, each counted once and only if the observer sees it. `fightOddsAgainst()` in `npc-decisions.ts` weighs `ownSide()` against it, so every danger roll is a group judgment. `fightSide()` is what a truce or a surrender spans in `makePeace()`. `spokesman()` is the company leader when it is an NPC, awake and in sight, and `answersPlea()` rolls with its brain. A player leader never speaks for its merc.
- `src/sim/crews.ts` owns raider crews. A fit, free raider at its camp may pick the `muster` idle option while it sees a free raider of the same camp there. Its `muster` goal drives to each candidate and asks once within `CREW.askReach`, the candidate rolls `crewAsked`, and the muster ends full at `CREW.size`, or after `CREW.musterTurns` with a `raid` for the crew or nothing. `brain.musteredAt` holds the next muster off for `CREW.retryTurns`. Members follow with a `crew` state of `CREW.huntTurns`, roll `crewCalled` once per target their leader fights, and defect when they flee. `settleCrews()` runs after the knockout step: a fallen leader's crew passes to the awake member with the most firepower times condition, lower id on a tie, and a lone survivor disbands.
- `src/sim/help-call.ts` owns the player's call for help in a fight: `canAskHelp()` for the topic, `helpAnswer()` for the answer, `joinPlayerFight()` for a yes and `turnDownCall()` for the `helpRefused` state. The hold-back tests are `isRestrained()`, `favors()` and `heedsCall()` in `src/sim/relations.ts`, shared with `reportIncident()`, and `sideAgainst()` declares the feud for both. `canAnswerCall()` and `dangerJoining()` in `src/sim/npc-decisions.ts` give the refusals and the odds, and the `helpCalled` decision rolls the yes. NPCs do not call each other for help.
- `src/sim/hire.ts` owns the player's hired guard: `guardFee()`, the `hireGuard` and `dismissGuard` radio effects, `playerGuard()` for the contracts dock, and the `hire` state's endings, each logged once as `guardEnded`. The fee moves once, at the hire, through `transfer()`.
- `src/sim/patch.ts` owns roadside patches for stranded and worn trucks. The agreement fixes which parts a patch lifts.
- `src/sim/aid.ts` owns fuel and supply aid: an `aid` state that moves the goods after a one-turn handover the player starts with [E] while both trucks are parked side by side, agreed through the `offerAid`, `askAid` and `aidOffer` topics and the `needySeen` decision. Context targets for aid and trade carry the NPC's vehicle id, and the UI reads `inMeetingReach` from the sim, so a deal shows only while its own driver is in reach. `aidHolds()` is the one test of whether an aid deal can stand, read by `checkAid()`, the `offerAid` and `askAid` topics and the unprompted offer.
- `src/sim/parcel.ts` owns the parcel handover of a search job: the `parcel` topic adds a `handover` state (target holds, player is other) and a `meet` goal with reason `pullOver`, like a trade. The state refreshes while both trucks meet, breaks on combat through `BROKEN_OFF` and on a feud or a lost parcel through `checkHandover()`, and `handOverParcel()` ends it `fulfilled`. The context target `parcel` carries the target's vehicle id.
- Chaos Highway groups choose their prey with `groupPrey()` in `src/sim/combat.ts`, which `huntConvoy()` in `src/sim/fury-road.ts` hunts every turn and `autoOrders()` fires on first. Each group truck holds a feud on the hauler, and `markHostile()` in `src/sim/states.ts` gives it a feud on the player. The prey is the gun truck while the gun truck can hit it or is in its `brain.attackers`, and otherwise the nearer truck with `FURY_ROAD.playerMargin` in the gun truck's favor. A driver whose top goal is to flee a truck also fires on it while that truck holds a `combat` state against it, so a runner that broke sight keeps defensive fire. A robber with a robbery feud is excepted.
