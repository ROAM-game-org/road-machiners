# Rendering and UI

- Truck surfaces come from `surfacedMaterial()` in `src/three/render/models.ts`. Each material class weathers in its own way: paint, metal, rust, grime and plain. Crease shadow comes from one shared mask atlas, baked so each masked face owns its pixels and pieces too thin to hold them read white. `loadModels()` packs every truck model's baked mask into a slot and moves its UVs there, and `paintSurfaceMasks()` paints the atlas once at boot. Edge wear comes from each triangle's distance to the model's convex folds, computed at load by `edgeDistances()` in `src/three/render/truckSurface.ts`. Chips and paint grain are shader noise, and the shader skips them where they cannot show. Each `VehicleView` also lays one soft contact shadow quad under the truck.
- `src/three/render/vehicle.ts` draws a flat silhouette of a seen vehicle where terrain or props cover it, one merged twin mesh per moving piece. Truck meshes mark stencil bit `TRUCK_BIT` and props mark `PROP_BIT`, so other views must not write those bits.
- A frame at 120 Hz has 8 ms, so work that can spike moves off the frame. A changed truck builds its new look in steps on a hidden twin, for `REBUILD_STEP_MS` a frame for all trucks (`FRAME_REBUILDS`), and the twin takes over when done, so the old look shows meanwhile. `TruckViews` in `src/three/truck-views.ts` also builds every truck in physics range that is out of sight, nearest first, and keeps its view out of the scene, so a truck shows the frame it comes into sight. Gun stands find their braces with `bodyRays()` in `src/three/render/bodyRays.ts`, a triangle tree built once per body model, since a three.js raycast per brace made one build step take up to a second. `PixelReadback` in `src/three/render/pixelReadback.ts` is the one owner of whether the browser returns honest pixels, found by a probe of known colors read back once, with a small tolerance for light canvas noise. Where it refuses (Firefox with resist-fingerprinting or fingerprinting protection on all targets), the portrait and garage queues build nothing, the condition panel and truck card draw `ConditionPlan` from `src/ui/condition-plan.ts`, the inventory draws its plain outline with icons, and `chassisPortrait()` keeps the standard sheet; one console warning is logged and nothing is reported. The road mask and graffiti read no pixels back. The truck portrait of the cards and the HUD diagram follows the same rule on its own queue: `TruckPortraits` in `src/three/render/truckPortrait.ts` builds portraits in steps, one render pass or mesh at a time with `readRenderTargetPixelsAsync`, for `PORTRAIT_STEP_MS` a frame from `Game.frame()`. It builds the player's truck first, then the hovered or pinned one, then the trucks in sight nearest first, and builds only the first two while a turn plays. It never builds on request: `shown()` returns the ready portrait or the previous one of that truck, and the cards draw again when `onReady` fires. It keeps every truck in sight and `PORTRAIT_KEEP` out of sight. While the world clock is held (a panel, the menu, a focused text control or a hidden tab), `ShaderWarmup.render()` draws the world only when the camera or the view size changed, since time is held and redrawing the frozen world behind the panel made drags under it hitch. An open radio call is no hold, so the world draws under it and the trucks' slowdown and the antenna flash show. `ShaderWarmup` in `src/three/shader-errors.ts` draws the world: an object whose material has no compiled shader yet stays out of the frame while `compileAsync()` builds it. The turn worker computes the sight samples along the player's path once per turn (`sightAlong()` in `src/phys/turn.ts`). The same samples mark the explored tiles, so playback only applies them to the fog. `FogView.update()` redraws only the tiles whose sight changed or whose explored byte differs from its last copy. The main thread holds the physics world only as snapshot bytes, and each worker restores and syncs it before a turn or a path preview. Workers get the terrain once at boot and the obstacle list only when its content changed (`SentObstacles`), and a turn sends the list back only when it changed it. `npm run playtest` reads FPS, and a Playwright probe in `tmp/` timing `Game.frame` in a headed window on the real display finds hitches.
- A per-frame view rebuilds its geometry only when the inputs of its last build changed: `ZonesView` bands, `WeaponRangeView` fans and `VehicleView` look signatures each keep that key, and the zone hover ring is built once. `CameraRig` reads the canvas rect once per frame, on the first `screenOf()` after `tick()` or `resize()`. `syncVehicles()` finds before-turn trucks through a `Map` built once per playback. A translucent double-sided overlay takes its material from `overlayMaterial()` in `render/overlay-material.ts`, which draws it in one pass and throws on a `map` or vertex colors. three.js draws a transparent double-sided material twice and flags it for a program re-check before each draw, so every visible overlay would rebuild its shader parameters every frame. `npm run perf` reports these program checks per scene.
- The inventory grids draw the truck from `GarageRenders` in `src/three/render/garageRender.ts`, owned by the `Hud` and handed to the screens through `UiHost.garageRenders()`. Its camera looks straight down with the nose up, at `GARAGE_PX_PER_METER`, and every picture is placed in meters from the grid's top left corner (`gridOrigin()`), so `GridMetrics` in `src/ui/inventory-draw.ts`, built from `cellSteps()` in `src/sim/body.ts`, puts it on the cells at any zoom. The origin sits one row step ahead of the nose, so the front armor row lies outside it and the rows between hold the body. It draws the bare truck once per chassis, paint and look of its built-in parts, then every loose part at every spot it can mount on (`mountSpotsOf()`), several parts per truck build when they share no cell (`packPlacements()`). A part's picture draws only its own meshes and lets the rest of the truck write depth, so whatever covers it on the truck still covers it. It keys pictures by chassis, paint, part id, wear look step and spot. The `Hud` asks for the player's trucks, their parts and the storage (`backgroundNeeds()` in `src/ui/garage-draw.ts`) and steps the queue for `GARAGE_STEP_MS` a frame while no turn plays, like the portraits. Unlike the portraits, a grid never waits: `InventoryView.render()` calls `ensureNow()`, which builds whatever that grid will draw and is not ready yet, at once and with a blocking readback, before the grid draws. A gun's picture also carries its turret pivot in meters (`GarageSprite.pivot`, from `VehicleView.partPoint()`), and the fire fan of the grid starts there. The cab is baked into the base model, so its own picture is cut to its cells along the truck (`cabWindow()`), and a chassis whose cab draws nothing fails its render. Any failed garage render, blocking or background, is reported through `noteError()` and stays failed for the session, so `garageLooks()` gives no look for a truck without a body picture and its grid draws the plain outline with icons. A part whose wear look changed shows its last picture until the new one is built. `chooseLayout()` in `src/ui/inventory-draw.ts` is the pure fit: it takes the room per column count and the truck in meters, and returns the columns, the cargo's place and the pixels per meter. `InventoryView.fitTo()` measures the room once per fit key, the truck, its row count, the UI size and the room, so a click never refits. `GridMetrics.cargoBeside` moves the spare rows to the right of the truck, and `rectOf()`, `rowTop()`, `cellAt()` and `gridSize()` stay its one geometry. The field screen renders as `.inv-bench` from `InventoryView.render()` when `InventoryScreen` hands it a head, and every other host and the phone render the stacked `.inv-wrap`.
- `src/three/pointer.ts` decides what a click on the map does. The player truck is picked by its drawn shape, through a ray hit on its model, and the same decision drives the stop cursor and the ground highlight. NPC picking keeps the screen radius.
- `VehicleView.aim()` turns each turret only within the `gunSpans()` of its gun, through `aimWithin()` in `src/sim/armor.ts`, so a barrel shows where the gun can fire. A target outside the arc leaves the barrel at the nearest edge. The spans center on the gun's facing, `facingOf(item)` in `src/sim/grid.ts`, so a gun turned at rotation 2 has its arc over the rear. A gun with no target rests along its facing, or at its nearest allowed edge, and a gun that can fire nowhere rests along its facing. The range wedges, the hover arcs and the grid fan use the same facing, and each weapon's grid picture turns by it. The head's lane axis comes from the facing (`laneAxis()` in `vehicle.ts`), and the mount plate turns with the cells (`cellsTurned()` in `grid.ts`), so a gun can point across its footprint. The head stands clear of its sweep: `VehicleView.build()` draws the body, items and wheels first, then raises each gun's post over every drawn item and body surface the head can turn over, by `GUN_GAP` (`src/three/render/gunClearance.ts`).
- Trucks and every prop in the prop scope get a dark outline 0.5 CSS pixels wide from `outlineOf()` and `outlineProps()` in `src/three/render/models.ts`. It is geometry, back faces pushed out in screen space, so antialiasing smooths it. Outlines draw only on pixels no model has marked in the stencil.
- `src/three/render/truckMotion.ts` leans the drawn truck body and swings its antenna and tow chain from the acceleration in each physics frame. It is render-only, so physics never reads it. Shocks and axles stretch between the leaning body and the physics wheel hubs. The lean holds while a turn is paused. A body longer than `SWAY.reachHalf` pitches less, by its share of that length, so its ends move no more than a 4 m truck's.
- A rig draws one `UnitView` per unit in `src/three/render/vehicle.ts`. The rear unit's group is a child of the front root, posed from the frame's `rear` body relative to the front, so it bends and pitches on its own. Wheels, guns, harpoon lines, dust, ruts and volleys take the unit of their part, and `TruckMotion` uses the whole rig's span, so a tractor leans as a long truck, not a short one. A kept frame whose units no longer match the chassis, after a chassis swap, is dropped through `staleFrame()` in `src/phys/frames.ts`. While a view rebuilds toward a chassis with another unit count, it poses its old build's front unit and skips the rear, and it throws on a mismatched frame only once the build is done. A rig hulk draws folded at its saved `bend`.
- `src/three/render/beaconPulse.ts` draws the player's beacon as red full rings every `PULSE.period` seconds of wall-clock time while `player.beacon` is on, under the sound arcs. It keeps a fixed set of meshes and reads no turn state.
- `src/three/render/scope.ts` detaches map chunks outside the camera view or past gray vision. A chunk inside the sun's shadow box from `sunCasters()` in `src/three/render/daylight.ts` stays attached even off screen, so a hill toward the sun casts the same shadow at every zoom. That box reaches a fixed depth toward the sun, twice the streamed ground. Gray vision reaches `TERRAIN.vision.grayFactor` sight radii from the truck. Its `SightLimit` clips ground and props at that edge, keeps the clear-sight tiles in a texture window that covers only their bounding box and grows in steps of 32 tiles, so each sight sample uploads a few kilobytes and not the whole map, and greys props on tiles out of clear sight, where tiles in clear sight are the rule tiles plus the tiles under every prop the player sees, from `seenPropTiles()` in `src/sim/vision.ts`, so a prop never greys behind its own boxes. The ground fog keeps the rule tiles only. The worker adds the same prop tiles to each live sight sample. A mesh with a `sightAt` attribute greys by that point instead. Deck models carry their deck's centre-line point from `deckCenterAt()` in `src/sim/bridge.ts`, so their grey edges run straight across the deck, not in steps along the rails. The camera cannot pan past the edge. Static views register with a scope instead of adding to the scene. `RenderScope.add()` refreshes the object's world matrices before it measures, and throws when the bounds leave the circle the caller claims plus the scope margin, so a chunk's box holds only its own objects.
- `RadioLights` in `src/three/render/vehicle.ts` plays antenna light cues on wall-clock time, so an open call still glows with frozen turns. The open call comes from `callTrucks()` and starts its cue only when the call is shown (`note()` takes `callShown`). `CallReveal` in `src/three/call-reveal.ts` decides when a turn's call shows: its opening events, picked with `radioSpeakers()`, stay out of the log until `CONFIG.callBeatMs` after the playback ends, and the notice, the `radio` sound and the cue wait with them. A call no playback raised shows at once. Short talk comes from `radioSpeakers()` and the beacon switch-on from `world.player.beacon`. `radioLit()` gives the light from a cue. `VehicleView.radio()` lights the bulb and its halo.
- `VehicleLights` in `src/three/render/daylight.ts` gives each lamp-on truck a headlight beam. The three nearest the player truck cast shadows from 1024² maps, so props, trucks and terrain block them. Farther beams light without shadows, to cap the depth passes, and only the nearest of those gets a beam (`PLAIN_BEAMS`). Every light costs uniform rows in every lit program, and a program must fit the WebGL 2 floor of 224 fragment and 256 vertex uniform rows, counted per row with no packing as Android's ANGLE counts them: a spot light is 7 rows and a shadowed one 9 more. A new light or ground uniform must leave the worst program at least 7 rows under 224, and data fixed at build time is a shader constant, not a uniform. The ground's per-material tables are GLSL constants, the ink knobs pack four to a `vec4` (`inkTuneA` to `inkTuneE`, refreshed with the light each frame), and the truck surface and armor cut values pack the same way. Both beam pools only grow through the night and by day hold just the lamps that are on, because a change in the shadowed-light count recompiles every material. Each shadow map adds a sampler to every lit shader. The ground program samples 10 textures by day, with the landform canvas on Icarus and the wear texture on Chaos Highway, and its worst night program uses 16 on both maps, the 16 fragment samplers that WebGL 2 guarantees. `node scripts/look-board.mjs samplers` prints the counts. A new shadowed light or ground texture must keep that count, so new ground data must pack into a texture the ground already reads. `failLoudOnShaderError()` in `src/three/shader-errors.ts` turns a program that fails to build into `ShaderBuildFailed`, naming the material by its patch key, such as `|roads|wear|ink|pools|fog|haze|sight`, with the sampler limit, the uniform limits and the GL log. The playtest emulates a GPU at the WebGL 2 floor, 16 sampler units and 224 fragment and 256 vertex uniform rows, and fails a program over any of them. Its night check lights every beam and, in Roaming, stands at a town gate so the site pool is on, prints the rows of each ground program, and checks in Roaming and Chaos Highway that every program links and no ground is missing. A lost WebGL context is a device event, not a shader fault. `GpuContext` in `src/three/gpu-context.ts` is its one owner: it follows `webglcontextlost` and `webglcontextrestored`, counts losses as an epoch and calls the restore hooks on a later task, so three.js has rebuilt its own state first. A failed link is held as a verdict in `ShaderVerdicts` and thrown as `ShaderBuildFailed` only if the context is still live and no loss came within `SHADER_LOSS_GRACE_MS`, since Firefox reports the failed link before the loss; the detail ends with `context lost: yes|no`. On a restore `ShaderWarmup` forgets what it compiled, `GarageRenders` and `TruckPortraits` drop jobs from an older epoch without marking them failed and build again, `SiteLights` and `VehicleLights` ask their shadow maps for a redraw, and an open inventory draws again. While the context is lost nothing draws and `Game.timeHeld()` holds the world clock. A context not restored within `CONTEXT_RESTORE_MS` raises `WebGLUnavailable`, which shows the WebGL notice and sends no report.
- The ground is drawn in ink by `drawGround()` in `src/three/render/groundShader.ts`, on top of the painted ground canvas. `groundFields()` in `src/three/render/groundFields.ts` builds everything it reads: the road mask, a float road field with each corner's distance from, along and across the nearest road and the ground height in alpha, and one tile texture with each tile's ground material id, desert weight, glass mask and field furrow turn. Two more layers of that texture cache each 4 by 4 tile block the border reads, from `blockWriter()`: up to three materials in the order the block meets them and which of the 16 tiles hold each, so a pixel reads one or two texels instead of 16 tiles. A block with four or more materials, or past the map edge, takes the full 16 tile search. The blocks take about 300 ms on Icarus, so they build in short tasks after boot and `groundBlocksReady` turns the cache on once all rows are in. Until then every pixel takes the full search, which draws the same ground. On maps with landforms it also reads a landform canvas with the canyon, the dry river and the scree arcs. Each ground type has one row in the material table of `src/render/groundPaint.ts`: its color, its mark, its edge look and how far its sand spills onto lower neighbours. The shader finds the two strongest materials in the 4 by 4 tiles round each pixel and draws the border between them as a smooth B-spline curve, bent by a slow wave whose size the edge look sets. Only scrub edges add a fine wiggle and pepper noise, with a fringe of upright slivers, so grass edges break up like grass. Gravel edges crumble at pebble size, scree edges half as much, and mud and water take the calm shore look. Sand spills a streaked halo over plainer ground in two flat tones, scaled by the `halo` knob, but never over scrub, which stands through it. A border fades over the `edgeSoft` knob in tiles plus one pixel, so material planes keep crisp edges. Material colors are calmed in two ways, and concrete pads keep their own color in both, while old asphalt off the road settles like any ground, since the road itself draws its own color. `materialContrast` lifts each ground darker than sand toward the lightness of sand and keeps its hue, so black mud reads as dark ground, not a hole. `surround` blends each ground toward the average color around it, from a blurred color map that `surroundTexture()` in `src/three/render/groundSurround.ts` builds at load, so a mud pit in sand moves toward sand while a wide swamp stays dark. Each material has a `stand` height in the table, and a thin edge shadow of `edgeShadowWidth` meters falls on the lower side of its visible edge, darkened by `edgeShadow` times the height step. Each material keeps its own mark at a fixed size in meters, fading out once too small to draw: cracks on hardpan and asphalt, pale cracks on salt crust, long wavy plow furrows on fields, each connected field turned its own way by `fieldTurns()` in `src/three/render/groundFieldTurns.ts`, which bakes the turn into the tile texture and carries it a few tiles past the field's edge, puddles on mud, wind ripples on sand, glints on water, pebbles on gravel and scree, grass tufts on scrub, smudges on ash and seams on concrete. Flat marks show only on flat ground away from borders and react to the border within `DRAWN_EDGE_REACH`: ripples and water lines run along it and cracks add a finer layer. Pebbles and grass tufts stand up, so each one is planted only where its root is on its own material, gathers toward the edge, and draws over the neighbouring ground and the outer edge of a road. Every level-of-detail fade, for the edge noise, the edge shadow and the marks, reads `drawnDetailPx`: the longer side of a full-resolution pixel on the ground, rounded down to a power of two. A mark line thinner than a pixel keeps a share of one pixel's ink set by the `farMarks` knob, so cracks, ripples and grass blades still read at mid zoom. `holdGroundDetail()` sets it from the camera before each draw, so the ground cache's coarse draw while zooming keeps the same shapes, and antialiasing still reads the real pixel size. Roads are cracked dark asphalt with a worn dashed centre line and an ink edge line, and dirt tracks blend in from the mask's green channel. The ground is one smooth surface that is drawn, lit, shadowed, driven on and stood on. `GroundSurface` in `src/sim/surface.ts` owns it, and `groundAt()` in `src/sim/terrain.ts` reads it for the sim, props and placement. It is a cubic B-spline over the 4 m heights, blurred by `LAND_BLUR_TILES` so small bumps cast no spiky evening shadows. A square whose own corners are steeper than `TERRAIN.drive.maxSlope` is a cliff, and the corners of a cliff stay unblurred, so cliffs stay sharp. Corners within `RAW_REACH` tiles of a cliff fade from raw to blurred with distance. Near cliffs the surface blends halfway, by `CLIFF_SHARPNESS`, toward a cubic curve that runs through the corners, so a cliff stays steep and inside its raw heights. Nothing in the rule follows the tile edges, so a cliff that runs diagonally to the grid keeps an even lip and casts an even shadow instead of a row of steps. Past the map edge the heights continue the slope at the edge, so an even slope stays even out to the border. `chunkGeometry()` in `src/three/render/terrain.ts` lays mesh vertices on it every 2 m (`MESH_STEPS`), with normals from the same curve, and `groundField()` in `src/phys/frames.ts` builds the physics heightfield from the same 2 m points, so trucks sit on the ground that is drawn. Only the drawn mesh dips under a deck, by `deckFloorCap()`, so the ground never shows through it. Deck stations stand on `groundAt()`, so deck ends meet the drawn ground. Map generation still levels and checks the raw corner grid through `cornerGroundAt()`, so the map bake does not depend on the blur. The route summary does, because deck grades and prop seats read `groundAt()`: after a change to `LAND_BLUR_TILES` or the fold rule, run `npm run routes:bake`, or the game throws when it builds a route cell. The mesh casts and receives the sun's engine shadow, so hills shade their back slopes and cast shadows, and anything behind a hill is blocked by the hill itself. Inside a 2 m cell the flat mesh stays within about 1 cm of `groundAt()` for 95% of the ground and within 3 cm for 99%. The worst cells sit at cliff feet. The ground writes the `InkSurface` struct for the ink paint in `src/three/render/inkPaint.ts`: road share, edge line, the mesh height and slope, the rise over the ground 8 tiles around, the landform slope and the crest line. The paint tints height bands and draws the optional elevation lines from them. `GroundLandform` in `src/three/render/groundLandform.ts` blurs the heights twice at load: by `LANDFORM_BLUR_TILES` for crest tracing, and lightly by `LANDFORM_DETAIL_BLUR_TILES` for a slope texture the shader samples as a B-spline. Ground is in shade where the shadow map blocks the sun, the mesh turns away from it, or the landform tilts away from the sun's direction by more than the `shadeCut` angle. The three combine so any one shades fully, which leaves no lit seam where they meet. The cut gives each ridge a crisp edge between a lit face and one flat shade, in the Yoshida way, and since it reads the landform, small bumps do not split into shade pools. Inside shade the relief gradient goes flat. Ground facing the sun more than flat ground brightens and warms by the `litFace` knob. `GroundCrest` in `src/three/render/groundCrest.ts` traces crest lines once from the landform and again when `crestFold` changes. A line goes where the fold depth, twice the height minus the heights a reach away on both sides, is largest across the strongest bend and at least `crestFold` meters. It measures over 4 tiles for sharp ribs and cliff lips and over 10 tiles at the same bend for broad dunes. A shallower fold, down to half of `crestFold`, also gets a line when the raw corner heights bend sharply there and the fold is at least 40% of the height range within 10 tiles, so low dune lips on flat sand are lined while ribs on a big slope and soft humps are not. A cliff foot folds the other way and gets no line. Each traced point snaps onto the fold of the raw corner heights, so a line sits on the drawn lip, then the line is smoothed. A texture stores, for each corner, the nearest line point, the line direction and a taper toward the ends, and the shader measures the true distance across the line from the four corners round a pixel. The line is a `crestWidth` hairline in the ground's own color made darker and redder by `crestLine`, with gaps and a slight swell from noise along it. Once it is thinner than a pixel it keeps one pixel and fades. The line belongs to the form, so it stays put while the shade moves with the sun and meets it only when the sun is across the ridge. Steep ground gets short tapered streaks down its fall lines, scaled by `cliffStreaks`, in the same darker tone. Each 8 m cell lays its own streak rows along the landform's fall direction, so streaks stay straight. Streaks show in light and in shade, and steep shade keeps its shadow strokes under them. Cast shadows are flat cool shapes with no ink line, because the shadow map's steps would show in a line. They darken by a fixed share inside a crisp edge. Lamp light and emissive light skip the ink rescale: the lights chunk sets lamp light aside before the sun loop and emissive after the emissive map, lamp light joins the color before the tone curve, and emissive lays over the inked color, so headlights, lit glass and the reactor core glow. The ink pale mix dims with daylight below golden hour, so nights read dark. Models get the same paint through `paintModel()` from `ModelFill`. The two share data only through `InkSurface` and `InkSun`, which the lights chunk fills with the sun's cast and facing shade. The live knobs are `KNOBS` in `src/three/render/inkTuning.ts`. Their URL values count only when the URL has `tune`, so a stray `?color=` in a player's link changes nothing. The Look menu from `mountLookMenu()` in `src/three/render/inkMenu.ts` moves them in groups. The console command `shaders` shows and hides it, and `tune` in the URL opens it at boot. Moving a knob writes it and `tune` into the URL, so a reload keeps the look. Each road tile takes the look of the nearest ground off the road, from `lookTypes()` in `src/render/groundPaint.ts`, and the ground paint and the scatter read it. `scatterPlacements()` in `src/three/render/scatter.ts` raises the stone chance on desert tiles within `SHOULDER_TILES` past `ROAD_GAP` and keeps scrub and cacti off them. Open desert moves toward denser desert stones, scrub and cacti by `desertWeight()`, and cacti gather within `CACTUS_NEAR_ROCK` of rocks and crags. Ground with no desert weight keeps the sparse small pebbles and dry scrub, shoulders included. On the Icarus map each zone scales those chances by its row in `SCATTER.regions` in `src/data/view.ts`, blended by zone weight with the plain taking the rest, and low-frequency noise scales stones and brush apart, so the mountains are stony, the farm belt brushy, the desert sparse and the swamp nearly bare, in patches. `SCATTER.glassFlats` thins all three again round Glass Flats, at full strength inside its radius and fading out by `fadeOut` radii. The highway map has no zones and keeps the base chances. Scatter stays decoration without collision.
- The game view draws that ground once per screen pixel and keeps it. `GroundCache` in `src/three/render/groundCache.ts` holds three screen-sized targets: color with road share, the height, slope and rise, and the distance to a road edge line. The writer material from `writeGroundCache()` fills them, and the ground material in the scene is the reader from `readGroundCache()`, which fetches its pixel and runs light, shadow, ink, fog, sight and night pools live. The camera is orthographic, so a pan moves every ground pixel by the same offset. `GroundCache.draw()` snaps the camera to whole pixels for the frame and stores the targets wrapped around, so a pan draws only the strips that came into view, plus a 32 pixel margin for heat haze. Haze shifts the fetch by its offset in pixels instead of bending the ground coordinates. A change of size, zoom, camera turn, ink knobs, ground uniforms or visible chunks redraws the whole view. While the zoom or turn keeps changing, that redraw goes into a second target at half the width and height, and the first frame with a still lens redraws it sharp, so a zoom gesture costs about what a pan does. The reader throws if anything draws it outside `GroundCache.draw()`, so offscreen renders such as survey pictures use the full material from `groundMaterial()`, as does a GPU without `EXT_color_buffer_float`, and checks that render the view call `ShaderWarmup.drawScene()`.
- The Chaos Highway highway wears in the same ground shader. `paintHighwayWear()` in `src/render/highwayWear.ts` bakes one seeded texture per window, at the mask's resolution, from the highway line, as four smooth fields that the linear filter can interpolate: paint strength along the road, sand cover, the signed distance across the line, and a patch field that is 128 on plain asphalt, lower inside a dark patch and higher inside a pale one. The shader draws each mark as a flat plane edged by `wearStep()`, a smoothstep over the field's `fwidth`, so every edge is one pixel wide at any zoom: a faded and broken yellow double centre line with no white marks, sand drifting in from each edge and in tongues, a sand rim at the outer edge that wanders with the road tone, and dark and pale patches. The band also takes the ink ground's asphalt crack mark, as Roaming roads do. It is paint only, so the mask, the tiles and the lane and verge widths stay as they are. `asphaltModel()` in `src/three/render/scatter.ts` adds pale stones and tufts on the asphalt, thicker toward the edges. Scatter never blocks driving.
- Props collide, block routes and block sight by their model's shape. `npm run models:shapes` turns each prop model into boxes in `src/data/prop-shapes.json`, and a test fails when a shape is stale. `propPose()` in `src/sim/mapgen.ts` is the one turn and scale of each prop, read by the views and every collision user. `propBoxes()` gives its posed boxes and `propReach()` the circle that holds them, for cheap tests. Physics gives colliders only to props in one turn's reach of a truck, and leaves out boxes above `PHYSICS.truckClearance`. Sight is blocked only by boxes that span eye height.

- Trucks draw part wear in `WEAR_LOOK_STEPS` look steps from HP (`wearLookStep()` in `src/render/partLooks.ts`). Each step darkens the part's paint and rust toward char and dents it (`dentsOf()` and `dentOffset()`). `wornTone()` in `src/three/render/truckMesh.ts` mixes them toward `CHAR` by `charShare()`, which reaches `CHAR_SHARE` at the last worn step, 25% HP. Gun trim, gun bases, armor, the ram strip, metal and tires keep their colors while the part works. A broken part mixes every material further toward char, so a broken gun loses its role color and keeps its shape. The dents grow by `wearShare()`, the square root of the step, so a part at half HP already shows most of them. Dents skip flush details, the trim strip, lamps and reflectors, which would sink into the panel behind them. `smokeSources()` in `src/three/render/smokePoints.ts` gives each broken part a smoke point at the center of its cells, and the cab one when it is under `HURT_CAB`, and `TruckFx` puffs ink smoke from each while a turn plays. The base is dented last, after everything on it is placed. Top dents land only on the base, and no base vertex moves under anything that stands on it, so guns, antennas and goods never float. The truck rebuilds only when a part crosses a step, so a repair restores the look. - `CLOUD_SHADOWS` in `src/three/render/cloudShadows.ts` owns cloud shadows, render only and unsaved. `RenderScope.add()` and `VehicleView.finishLook()` call `shadeTree()`, which patches each `MeshLambertMaterial` as the night pools do (basic materials are unlit and skipped). The patch adds one world-XZ varying and `|clouds` to the cache key, and scales `reflectedLight.directDiffuse` by a tileable mask of two value-noise octaves computed in the shader, so it costs no texture unit and only two uniform vectors (R11). `game.ts` calls `sync()` once per frame: the cover target is `WEATHER.clouds.clear`, which is 0 so a clear sky casts no cloud shadow, or `overcast` while `isOvercast()` holds, and it eases only by play time, so it moves only while a turn plays. - Each dust cloud draws the run from its own spot to the next younger cloud of the same truck, so one cloud per turn reads as one unbroken trail. A puff's age comes from where it sits on the run, so puffs appear one by one behind the truck as the turn plays, and the newest cloud draws nothing until a younger one joins it. A truck's own plume shrinks to stay clear of a circle of the truck's half length and grows as the truck drives away (`fitToTruck()`). Both are look-only, and the sim cloud position, which contacts read, does not move. A plume's look comes from `plumeShape()` in `dust.ts`. It builds card size, wobble, vertical scatter and opacity up from a small faint start over the first `buildTurns` of its age, and the fit reads the same shape. Thin ground keeps a minimum of tire spray and haze puffs per meter and makes each one fainter and soft (`sprayLook()` in `fx.ts`), so the same dust reads as a streak and not as separate poofs. Thicker tire spray, tire haze and emission dust draw with clump shapes (`clumpShape()` in `cards.ts`), lumpy cells of the card atlas with a grainy broken edge. A plume mixes those with the soft smoke shapes half and half, and its grainy puffs fade sooner, so a plume is lumpy near the truck and smooth far behind it. A cloud's opacity follows its sim `range`, so thin ground draws a faint plume. The drawn rise stops at `riseCap`, while the sim keeps rising for sight over hills. A plume puff between the camera and any drawn truck thins through `cutShare()`, the rule smoke uses over the player's truck. A dust cloud and a storm haze bank move once per sim turn. `src/three/render/turn-blend.ts` owns how they are drawn while a turn plays: `driftAt()` carries each from its before-turn state to its sim state on the turn's movement progress, along a curve whose ends follow the sim velocities, and between turns they rest on the sim position, which is where a dust contact sits. A cloud the player starts or stops seeing fades over the turn, and its ground tint blends between the tiles under its two spots. The look age runs one turn ahead of the sim age, by the same progress.
- Lit lamps and windows glow in their own color: a `PAL.lamp.on` box in `SiteBuilder` and a model material named `glow` get an emissive equal to their color. Fortress gate lamps stand on the gatehouse's outer face from `fortressGates()`, and `dressGate()` in `src/three/render/sites.ts` adds a banner pole for towns and raider camps.
- The Fallen Sun reactor's rod glows by emissive alone and adds no light. Its light is one unshadowed spot in the bow's breach mouth, owned by the bow view (`lightBreach()` in `src/three/render/obstacles.ts`) and aimed out, so every ray leaves through the opening and it stays in by geometry. `obstacles.test.ts` raycasts it on the real models. A teal disc just inside the breach glows with the same pulse, so the source reads from outside, since the rod itself stands deep in the hull. The sim's core light disc lies under this spot, and `obstacles.test.ts` checks it. A new unshadowed prop light follows the same rule.
- The ten inhabited sites, the fortresses, light up at night with two kinds of light. Most light is baked lamp pools, which are cheap and cannot shine through walls. A few spot lights add moving shadow and accents.
- Lamp pools live in `src/three/render/lightPools.ts`. Every lamp head of a fortress, from `SiteBuilder.addLampHead()`, `addWallLamp()`, `addLantern()` or `addFirePit()`, adds a warm pool to one world texture, `POOLS.texelsPerTile` texels per tile, capped at `POOLS.maxSide`, the WebGL2 minimum texture size, so a big map gets fewer, and gets a glow sprite called `halo`. A pool only lights the ground on its lamp's side of the curtain, and never inside a wall, tower or bastion footprint. `NIGHT_POOLS.light()` hooks a Lambert material so it adds the pool at its world spot, pushed along its normal by `POOLS.push` (0.7 tiles, more than half a texel on the big map, where a texel is near a tile wide, so a wall face never reads the pool across the wall), which lets wall faces pick up the pool on their own side. `buildSite()` hooks every mesh of a fortress, `buildProp()` hooks the fort pieces and `terrainMesh()` hooks the ground. `SiteLights` bakes the texture at boot and passes its night level, which eases over `SITE_LIGHT_FADE_S`, to the pools and halos every frame. The ground keeps smooth normals by day and night, so lamp light shows no triangle facets.
- Fort shell lamps come from `dressGates()` in `src/three/render/sites.ts`, the same for every fortress. Outside lamps mark the outline: towers are tried farthest from the site centre first, and a tower gets one lamp on its most outward face only if its bearing is at least `SCONCE.outsideApart` from every outside lamp already hung. A tower hangs at most one inner lamp, on the mount that points most toward the centre. Wall lamps hang on inner faces only and fill a stretch only where no inner lamp lies within `SCONCE.gap`. The outer faces of walls and bastions get no lamp, so the fort reads as dark walls with warm points. Gate lamps light the apron.
- Interior lights belong to each site's builder in `src/three/render/interiors/`, one file per site or per shared look. `camp.ts` holds both raider camps and `masonry.ts` the three masonry forts. `shed.ts` is the shared shed piece. `outpost.ts` furnishes the Chaos Highway road forts: drums, crates, a shed, a parts rack and one lantern on the concrete strips outside the asphalt, and nothing on the lanes. The fort shell already lights a small yard, so a small compound needs at most one lantern at its main work spot. Only big towns like Bowl and Nose spread lanterns over the yard. A free-standing lamp is always `addLantern()`, a wooden post with a hanging lantern, because a flood head on a pole in the middle of a yard makes no sense. A camp fire is `addFirePit()`. Keep pale surfaces off strong light, because pale walls under strong light read as a hospital.
- `SiteLights` in `src/three/render/siteLights.ts` drives the spot lights. `SiteBuilder.addWorkLight()`, `addFill()`, `addWash()` and `addLight()` register an anchor: a `flood` aimed at a subject, a `wash` long-throw light that aims level across the yard, a `gate` lamp aimed at the apron, a low `fire` in the raider camps, a `window` light on a shed lamp and one `fill` over each fortress that casts shadows. `LOOKS` holds each kind's color, strength, range and cone. The pool holds `SITE_LIGHT_POOL` spot lights, and the first `SITE_SHADOW_SLOTS` cast shadows and only take shadowed kinds. A shadow slot asks for one shadow render when it is made, because a shadow map that never renders blacks out every lit material. Each frame the pool ranks the anchors in gray vision, in view first, then fills, then nearest the camera focus. The pool joins the scene whole in the first `!sunAt()` frame and leaves at dawn, in the same frame as the beam pools, so it adds no recompile. Each site switches on by `lampsOn()` like NPC lamps. A light handed to a new anchor starts dark. Nothing is saved. Unshadowed floods stay in by geometry: every cone ray that meets flat ground within range lands inside the curtain, and the wash never meets the ground. `groundHits()` checks both in `sites.test.ts`.
- `HollowLamps` in `src/three/render/sites.ts` draws the Sunward Hollow's lamps on Icarus: a wall lamp on the outer face of the gatehouse, the shrine lantern and the fire pit. Their glass and their halo sprites glow only at night while `hollowLampsLit()` holds, from the night level `SiteLights` passes, so they add no light and no texture unit. After the fall they stay dark.
- `buildSite()` ends by merging the static meshes of a site with `mergeStaticMeshes()` in `src/three/render/siteMerge.ts`, so a town draws a few meshes, not one per box and model part. It bakes every plain Lambert mesh into one mesh per look, a look being the material's own values, its name aside, plus its shadow flags, render order, layers, culling, gate furniture flag and attribute layout. The merged mesh takes the first material of its look and stands at the site center, with its geometry baked relative to that point, so vertices keep the precision of the unmerged parts. Parts stay as they are when they sit in the subtree of a mover, a lamp head or a fixture head, or are transparent, textured, hidden, mirrored, instanced, outlines, or carry children or attributes a plain material does not read. The merged mesh's outline is each part welded alone and then merged by `registerBakedOutline()`, so the outline matches the per-part one. `HollowLamps` merges its static meshes the same way. `buildSites(terrain, false)` leaves the parts unmerged, for tests that read the layout of an interior by name.
- `npm run site:shot -- <site-id> --hour 23` takes a screenshot of a site at an hour, for any lighting change. It needs the dev server.
- Moving scenery (the Bowl windmill, the Dustwell pumpjack, the Granary conveyor sacks, the Salvage Yard crane and the Nose radar dish) is render-only, so no rule reads it. `SiteBuilder.addMover()` registers a part whose origin is its pivot, and `addSites()` hands every mover to one `SiteMotion` in `src/three/render/site-motion.ts`. It ticks through the prop scope's `onFrame()` hook, once per drawn frame, and it sets each part's local pose on real time from its rest pose. The motions are `spin`, `rock`, `slew`, `hoist` and `travel`, which carries a load along a belt and jumps it back. Each site's time offset comes from `hash2` of its position. `tick()` touches only the registered parts.
- `vehicleLampsOn()` in `src/three/render/daylight.ts` decides whether a vehicle's lamps shine. The player's truck follows `player.headlights`, the L key switch, and every other vehicle follows the clock (`lampsOn()`). `nightLightsWanted()` decides whether the night look exists: the full beam pool and the player glow. It counts only the clock and NPC lamps. By day `VehicleLights` keeps a beam only for each vehicle whose lamps are on, which is the player's, with no glow. Beam strength follows `Daylight.beam`, 1 from sunset on and lower under a high sun. A day toggle adds or removes one light, so it costs one material recompile.
- Shop and NPC part lists are compact rows from `PartRows` in `src/ui/part-rows.ts`, one open at a time, with the trade button in the row's own line. `keepPlace()` holds scroll and focus across the screens' full redraws. `RowSlots` in `src/ui/row-slots.ts` owns the order of every per-row trade list: part rows, truck cards, board offers, held contracts and desk cases and settlements. A row that leaves a list by its own action stays in its slot as a spent row, a clone of what was drawn with a past-tense `.tag` in the action's place, so the next row never slides under the pointer. Rows the player has not seen join at the end. Each screen calls `forget()` when it opens a fresh list: open, close, back, page, tab and filter change. A trade command does not.

- Sun shadows: `lightScene()` snaps the shadow box to whole texels around the truck, so static shadows hold still while it drives. `src/three/render/shadowFilter.ts` owns the shadow filter, a fixed 3×3 grid of compare taps that replaces three's noise-rotated disk. Heat haze never shifts the shadow term.

## Boot screen

`#boot` is static markup in `index.html`, so it paints before any game code loads. A tiny inline ES5 script in the page marks it failed before the game code runs. A game script that did not download shows `boot.failedToLoad`, the connection text. A game script that threw, or a browser without module scripts (`<script nomodule>`), shows `boot.failedToStart` with the error message in the step list. The script picks the Russian twins when `roam.lang` is `ru` and reads nothing else. `src/ui/boot-progress.ts` (`BootProgress`) holds the boot steps and throws on a step that is misused or changed after a failure. `src/ui/boot-screen.ts` (`BootScreen`) renders it. `src/three/main.ts` wraps each boot step in `boot.track()`. The bar counts finished steps, and models count files, so nothing on it is a guess. Sounds are not a boot step: `main.ts` starts the sound bank after `roam:ready` (the `roam:sounds` mark is set when it settles), and the combat song kit waits for the bank. New boot work goes through `track()`, so the bar stays honest. The screen hides while the save rescue panel waits and is removed after the first frame. The report desk (`#report`, built in `main.ts` right after the crash screen) shows its Feedback corner over the boot screen while it is up, so a hung or failed load can still send a report. Every boot string is in the `boot.*` catalog entries and shows in the chosen language once the code adopts the screen. `index.html` repeats the English of `boot.code` and `boot.loading`, and both languages of `boot.failedToLoad` and `boot.failedToStart`, for the paint before that, and a test keeps them equal. `npm run boot:check` tests it in a browser. A model or map download that never arrives (`download()` in `src/download.ts` retries first, and a try that gets no bytes for `DOWNLOAD.stallMs` counts as dropped) throws `DownloadFailed`, and `crash.ts` shows the connection screen and sends no report. Sounds that never arrive, or whose decode does not settle in `BANK.decodeMs`, get stand-ins instead, and so does a map prop model: `loadModels()` builds a grey box from its `prop-shapes.json` extents, returns it in its failure list and `main.ts` reports it. A truck part or weapon model has no shape and still throws. `npm run itch` refuses to upload a build that lacks a file of `public/models`. `withinMs()` in `src/stall.ts` is the deadline for a boot wait that can stall. Every file under `public/` is fetched through `publicUrl()` in `src/public-url.ts`, which appends the file's content hash from the build (`maps/icarus.bin?v=<hash>`), so a browser or CDN copy from another build is never used. The dev server has no hashes and uses plain URLs. `fetchMap()` in `src/three/map-file.ts` checks the decoded map against its build hash, fetches once more with `cache: 'reload'` on a mismatch, and then throws an error naming both hashes. Every game file loads through `fetchAsset` in `src/fetch-asset.ts`, which retries a dropped, stalled or transient-status request (any 5xx, 408 and 429, but not 404) three times through the same silence watch as `download()`. A file that still does not arrive shows Try again on the boot screen instead of the crash screen. A permanent status makes `fetchAsset` fetch the page once more, and a page that fails too means the host replaced the build, so the file throws `BuildGone`, an unreachable file that only gets a console warning.
- `OutpostGateViews` in `src/three/render/outpostGates.ts` draws the movable gates of a Chaos Highway window. For each gate `outpostGates()` returns as `movable` it builds two `fort_outpost_leaf` meshes on the gate's rail line, 1.6 m inside the curtain, and slides them sideways between meeting in the opening and resting behind the wall, over `OUTPOST_GATE.slideSeconds`. `sync()` sets their targets from the sim after a turn's playback and after a command, `tick()` moves them with a frame time clamped to 0.1 s, and `settled()` tells `game.ts` when the sliding is done, which is when it opens the pinned outpost screen. A view built with a truck in the last fort's yard starts that fort's north gate closed and slides it open. The closed gates are baked `fortOutpostShut` props drawn by the prop renderer, so each gate has exactly one drawer. Nothing in the view is saved, and no rule reads it.
- Old-world graffiti is one faded sign, Spiral, Race or Chaos, painted on a cliff face or a permanent prop, so a long drive shows one now and then. `graffitiRegions()` in `src/three/render/graffiti.ts` cuts the map into squares of `GRAFFITI.regionTiles` tiles, and each region's target comes from the running map area per `GRAFFITI.tilesPerSign`, so the whole map stays at about one sign per that many tiles. `GraffitiCandidates.sitesIn()` lists a region's props, ranked by `hashStr` of the id, and its cliff tiles, ranked by `hash2` of the tile, each at least half of `minGapTiles` inside the region edge, so signs in two regions never come closer than the gap and no region depends on another. `chooseRegionGraffiti()` fills props up to `propShare` of the target, then cliffs, at least `minGapTiles` apart, so the same map always gives the same signs. Cliff tiles face the fixed camera, and props are the flat-faced baked kinds in `GRAFFITI_PROP_LOOKS`, never breakable ones. `fitSite()` casts rays at the real terrain or model and drapes a grid on the hit surface, so a sign lies on the surface or is dropped. It tries sizes from large to small. A fit with another obstacle in front of the sign is dropped (`blockedInFront()`, through `ObstacleBuckets`). `GraffitiViews` hands each region with a target to `RenderScope.place()`, so the streamer fits and builds that region's one mesh the first time it comes into view, and boot does no fitting. `GraffitiViews`, built by `CraterViews`, paints the four signs from `signPaths()` into one weathered atlas, tints each sign with one dull colour from `PAL.graffiti` and draws each region's mesh with one shared material, in the crater scope, which greys in gray vision and has no outlines. The material's shade lift fades with the sun's intensity to `NIGHT_SHARE` of its day value, so signs keep a weaker glow at night. It writes no stencil bit, reads no rule and saves nothing.
