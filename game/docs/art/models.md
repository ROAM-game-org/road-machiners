# Art pipeline

Static props, obstacles, landmarks and truck parts are low-poly Blender models. Settlement houses, ruins, water and some hull sections are built from Three.js shapes in code. Models placed many times are drawn as instanced meshes, one per terrain chunk: rocks in `src/three/render/obstacles.ts`, and dead trees, fences, fort walls and other plain landmarks in `PropInstances` in `src/three/render/propInstances.ts`, since a grove or a fort holds hundreds of copies. The ground is one painted canvas texture over the whole map, and a shader draws roads and pads on it. Open desert mixes toward the warm sand `PAL.desertSand` in slow, gentle patches, rust-brown roads fray into light rim sand with grey stones, and the ground shader tints each terrain triangle a little apart so flat ground reads as low-poly facets. Pebbles, scrub and cacti are instanced 3D models from `src/three/render/scatter.ts`, without collision. Open desert takes `desert_stones`, `desert_scrub` and `cactus`, and ground without a desert look keeps the small brown `pebbles` and dry `scrub`. Desert scrub is an upright clump of olive `PAL.brush` stems, and cacti stay under 1.3 m so driving through one never looks like a crash. Desert stone clusters mix a light warm grey `PAL.desertStone` stone, a cool grey `PAL.stoneGrey` one and a terracotta chip. Loose boulders and crags use the terracotta `PAL.stone`, and stone walls and other models keep the grey-brown `PAL.rock`.

Some models come from Blender scripts in `tools/blender/`. Blender is installed with `brew install --cask blender`. Each script writes a `.glb` into `public/models/`, and both are committed. Rebuild one with `blender --background --python tools/blender/<name>.py -- public/models/<name>.glb tmp/<name>.png`. The second path is an optional preview render from the game camera angle.

A truck model, any name in `SURFACE_MODELS` in `src/render/partLooks.ts`, also needs its surface bake after every rebuild. Run `npm run models:bake <name>`, then `npm run icons`. The bake writes a crease shadow mask onto the model's own faces, in place. It unwraps a welded copy of the model, so a panel built from many pieces becomes one UV island, and it splits off faces that fold over each other. An island thinner than `SURFACE_MASK.minIslandPx` at the 0.1 m texel, like a lamp, louver, hook or thin rail, gets no crease shadow: it points at a white block in the mask's corner. The mask side doubles from the smallest that could fit until the packed islands meet the texel or reach `SURFACE_MASK.maxSide`. The bake throws on a masked island under the least thickness, on masked faces that share mask pixels, and on a missed texel under `leastFill` of the largest mask, and `src/three/render/surfaceMask.test.ts` checks every baked model the same way. Boot fails on a truck model without its current bake, so a forgotten bake shows at once.

## Adding a model

1. Copy `tools/blender/wreck.py` as the template. It shows the script shape: a `COLORS` table, a `build(kit)` function and a `main()`.
2. Build from `Kit.box()` and `Kit.cylinder()` in `tools/blender/kit.py`. `tools/blender/shapes.py` adds struts, tapered cylinders, ladders and wall patches. Add shared helpers to those files and one-model helpers to the model's script.
3. Work in meters with Z up and the front facing +X. Keep the origin at the model's ground point.
4. Size the model to a reference radius or footprint from the sim, and state it in the docstring. The game scales it from there.
5. Take colors from `src/render/palette.ts` and name the palette key in a comment. Blender cannot read the palette, so keep both in sync.
6. Keep the low-poly style: few vertices per cylinder, flat shading, and small seeded `dent_by` values for worn metal. A piece that should read round takes the shared counts in `tools/blender/parts_common_core.py` instead: a wheel is drawn by `drawn_wheel()`, with a `TIRE_SIDES` (48) tire, a `RIM_SIDES` (32) rim and a `HUB_SIDES` (16) hub, and a lamp, tank end, pipe, stack or spare takes `ROUND_SIDES` (24). A round arch or flare over a wheel takes `ROUND_ARCH_SEGMENTS` (16) from `parts_common_base.py` through the `segments` argument of `round_arch()`, `flare()`, `flare_profile()` and `arch_cut()`. Never copy the counts into a script. `src/three/render/wheelRound.test.ts` fails when a tire, rim or hub on the `wheel` model or a base has fewer sides. The courier's lamps, tubes and fenders take their sides from `round_sides()` in `tools/blender/kit.py`: the fewest sides, in steps of 4 and at least 12, whose polygon strays at most `ROUND_SAG` (4 mm) from the circle at the size it is drawn. The tractor and the other bases with big round parts take `ROUND_SEGMENTS` (32) or `SMALL_ROUND_SEGMENTS` (16) from `parts_common_core.py`, and `check_round()` throws when a round part has fewer sides than its count.
7. Fix the seed, so a rebuild gives the same file.
8. Render the preview and look at it before wiring the model into the game. Run the surface check from [Asset review](asset-review.md) on the new file. Once the model is in `NAMES`, look at it with `npm run shot -- --model <name>`, and with `--camera game` at the size a player sees it. [Tools](../tools.md) has the flags and scene files.
9. Add the name to `NAMES` in `src/three/render/models.ts`. Use `model('<name>')` in a view, or `instancedModel()` for many copies. Boot fails if the file is missing.
10. Take an in-game screenshot with a Playwright script in `tmp/`, and run `npm run playtest`. A truck model also takes the independent review in [Asset review](asset-review.md). The user confirms small visual details.

`models.ts` loads every model at boot. It swaps the glTF materials for flat Lambert, so models match the procedural meshes.

## Models from a concept image

When a place has a concept image, like Old Orchard's `docs/concepts/old-orchard-issue-111.jpg`, model each building it shows from a crop of the image with the `blender-image-to-3d` skill, then write the result as a Kit script by the steps above. Keep the briefs and review sheets in `tmp/models/<name>/`. The concept is a perspective painting, so take sizes from scale cues (a road about 10 m wide, a truck about 8 m long, a storey about 3.2 m) and judge the review sheets by eye.

Old Orchard's models were built this way: `farmhouse`, `barn`, `quonset`, `guard_post`, `army_truck`, `barrier`, `drums`, `woodpile`, the `bunker` blockhouse with its sandbag ring, and `dead_tree`. The `sandbags` arc carries no hedgehog. `tank_trap` is its own prop, the steel hedgehog the orchard's emplacements stand ahead of their arcs, with the girders the sandbag model once held. `LANDMARK_MODELS` in `src/sim/mapgen.ts` maps each prop look to its model, and `MODEL_RADIUS` holds the footprint radius from each script's docstring.

The riverbed boats are `boat_fishing` (10 m long, 3.3 m tall, 25° on its side, 5.6 m radius), `boat_barge` (14 m, 3.2 m tall, 7.6 m radius) and `boat_skiff` (5 m, 1.2 m tall, nose up, 2.8 m radius). Each is modeled tilted and sunk with `Kit.settle()`, so the game needs no tilt or sink rule, and pinned by the size table in `src/data/prop-shapes.test.ts`.

The roadside models are `road_sign` (2.6 m post leaning 14°, 1 m radius), `route_sign` (3.8 m board on two posts, one bent, 2.2 m radius) and `barrel` (a tipped 200 L drum, 0.6 m radius), plus two boulder shapes, `rock_slab` and `rock_split`, built to the same unit radius as `rock`. Their boxes reach about 9% past the circle, as `rock`'s do.

Glass Flats' models were built the same way from `docs/concepts/glass-flats-game-style-issue-112.jpg`, sized against its 6 m pickups: `engine_nozzle` 26 m long and 7.9 m tall, `engine_frame` 32 m and 15.6 m, `ruin_compound` 17 m and 6.2 m, `watchtower` 4 m and 9.6 m, `glass_spire` 10 m and 7.5 m, and `scrap_wall` 7.6 m and 3.6 m, pinned by `src/data/prop-shapes.test.ts`. The nozzle's roof and upper ribs start above `PHYSICS.truckClearance`, so trucks drive in; the frame keeps only its feet below it.

The `desert_scrub` and `cactus` scatter models were built the same way from `docs/concepts/wasteland-reference-issue-129.jpg`, sized against the 5.5 m van in it.

Size a building against the 8.1 m army truck, the one size cue a concept and the game share. The orchard's models are measured from the concept in army trucks and pinned by the size table in `src/data/prop-shapes.test.ts`:

- `quonset` 22 m long (2.7 trucks), 12 m wide, 6.5 m tall;
- `barn` 24 m long (3 trucks), 16 m wide, 10 m tall; its shed is the same model posed smaller;
- `farmhouse` 26 m with its wing, 14 m deep, 11 m tall;
- `bunker` blockhouse 14 m square and 5 m tall, 22 m with its sandbag ring;
- `dead_tree` crown 6.5 m across and 5.5 m tall;

Boxes whose bottom is at or above it block neither driving nor nav, but they still block sight. `scripts/prop-shapes.mjs` merges the dead tree's boxes below and above that height apart, so the crown never reaches down to the ground.

## Territory loot sections

The loot spots of the Fallen Sun and Glass Flats are sections of their wreck, not crate heaps: `hull_bay` (hull caches), `cargo_pod` (field spots) and `engine_section` (Glass Flats caches). Each is built to a 2.8 m footprint radius like the heap it replaces, stays under the 2.4 m eye height so its load shows, and is open on top with its cargo in bright ship colors. A crate heap now means a dropped pile or an army depot.

## Trucks

Each truck is one base model per chassis plus shared kit parts on its inventory grid. The grid is the layout for balance: slots, armor lanes and what shields what. Every cell is a 0.45 m square, `GRID_CELL` in `src/sim/body.ts`, centered on the body, and every deck, engine, bay and built-in cell lies over a flat model surface, so a part stands on its own cells. A part's balance numbers and model scale read its old footprint, `bulk`, not its cells. The model owns everything physical: the collider, the wheel positions, the engine spot and the surfaces parts stand on. One projection in `src/sim/body.ts` links them, and nothing else converts between cells and meters.

- `cellCenter()` and `cellRect()` stretch the inner cells evenly over the model's footprint and put the armor ring cells on its outer faces, so a side plate is skin that adds no width. The front and back armor rows lie outside the nose and tail, so the body spans the rows between them, each `2 * half.x / (h - 2)` long (`cellSteps().row`).
- `restOn()` picks where a part rests: on the surface that keeps the most of its footprint, shrinking the drawn part away from anything taller, like a bed wall, so it cuts no more than 5 cm into the model and stands on at least half its footprint. A part on a clean slope, like a raked window or a sloped hood, tilts to lie on it. A good or a spare wheel that fits nowhere would float, so it is not drawn. Other parts always show, on legs where they must. `restOn()` never throws: over air, like an armor row where the model is narrower than the grid, it returns a perched rest, and a test places every part shape on every cell, ring included. Spare armor is a loose part: it rests and shrinks like one, is drawn inside its rest and is not drawn when it fits nowhere. Guns and mounted armor always show. A gun's mount stands at its rest, or higher to clear the cab ahead, and its riser post stands on the highest surface under the post's column, so a gun never hangs over a lower surface. The post's thin foot plate may overlap a taller edge beside it, like a cab wall. `gunRisers.test.ts` checks every deck spot on every chassis, and `body.test.ts` checks that every chassis seats its engine on its bay floor. A post lifts the head over the cab ahead and over every drawn item and body surface the barrel can sweep, so a head never passes through cargo or the body (guns stand side by side and are not obstacles for each other: `buildWeapon()` narrows each head across its facing to its own lane less `LANE_GAP`, slides it along its facing so its tip stays within `reach` of its footprint center and its back stays within its footprint, and `src/sim/barrels.ts` keeps other guns out of its barrel lane, so no gun stands over another. Guns still pass each other while they aim). A test checks every chassis, cell and part size and reports how many perch.
- `engineAnchor()` is the model's hood hole, where a mounted engine is drawn wherever its `E` cells lie. Engines show in the hood hole on every chassis but the longbed, the van and the buggy. The longbed and van hoods are closed, so the engine sits hidden under them. The buggy's closed nose deck holds a full-size spare where an engine 0.44 to 1.6 m tall would stand, so its chassis sets `showsEngine` to false and `hidesInside()` counts the mounted engine without drawing it. The grid still shows the engine.
- A chassis's `showsCores` flag says whether the view draws its transmission and fuel tank. It is true for the junk-built trucks where parts stick out, the scout and the wagon, and there the two parts must stand on a low surface, never a cab roof. It is false for the others, whose bodies cover the parts, so they count in the grid but are not drawn.
- A grid may have more rows or columns than its model, since the projection stretches it over the model, and every chassis but the scout keeps a free 2x2 block of deck cells. The cab, transmission and engine bay keep clear of the wheel columns, the transmission covers the middle column or columns, the tank keeps off the rows right behind the front and back armor, and a cab is a 1x2 open seat or a 3x2 closed cab, either way round. So a grid change for balance never changes the look or the driving. A tank or transmission sits where it costs the fewest gun spots on the deck, but only where the rounds to stop the bare truck from each side stay within 5% of before, so a core never becomes free armor for the cab or bares the drive.
- Loose tests in `src/sim/body.test.ts` keep the grid roughly true to the model: the `E` cells lie in the half of the truck with the hood hole, each wheel cell in its corner, and every inner cell over the model. Cells may be about half to a full-size cell.
- `npm run models:shapes` turns each `base_*` model into collision boxes and a top surface height map in `src/data/truck-shapes.json`, and a test fails when a shape is stale. `bodyOf()` returns those boxes, cut at the chassis bottom and at `PHYSICS.truckRoof`, and takes `half` from their bounds. Wheel positions and the engine anchor are per-model data in `PHYSICS.bodies`.
- One rule sizes every truck's tire from its body, so no truck rides on city-car tires and none on comically large ones. The radius is the larger of `0.16 × √(L × Hb)`, with L the length and Hb the height of the base's collision boxes in `truck-shapes.json`, and the truck's stock radius from before the rule, rounded to the centimeter. The share 0.16 came from the old Utility van body, which gave 0.58 m; the remade van rolls on the rule's 0.51 m. The longbed's base draws its inner rear duals down to the ground, so the rule measures its body only down to its skirt, `halfHeight` plus 0.22 m below the center. Trucks built around big tires, like the carrier, loader and Bukhanka, sit at their stock size. The buggy's stock floor is 0.4 m, the courier's size, so it is about 4.9 tire diameters long like its photo. The semi is the one exception at 0.80 m, since the rule reads its trailer box, and its tandem spacing and photo height hold it. A tire widens by three quarters of its radius growth over stock, rounded to 5 mm. `src/data/wheelSize.test.ts` owns the rule and its stock table, and fails when a number in `PHYSICS.bodies` leaves it, so a new chassis takes its tire from the rule. `wheelRadius` and `wheelHalfWidth` in `PHYSICS.bodies` size the drawn tire and the physics wheel, and each base copies them as `WHEEL_R` and `WHEEL_HALF_W`. The hub hangs `PHYSICS.truck.suspensionRest` below `wheelY`, on the drawn axle, so a bigger tire lifts the body by the radius gained. Arches, flares and well walls grow from `WHEEL_R`. A shape beside an arch, like a door seam, a waist line or a bucket, must clear the tire and stay inside the footprint; `flare()` stops at the footprint ends. A door, door seam or door window keeps `DOOR_GAP` (0.25 m) of body panel from the flare's outer edge, measured in the side plane, so a door line may end above an arch. `check_door_gap()` in `parts_common_base.py` throws when it does not, and `door_sill()` gives the lowest end that keeps the gap. ## Part models

- Build a part for its rotation-0 footprint: w cells across in Blender Y and h cells along in Blender X, with the nose at +X. Truck right is Blender -Y. The origin is the footprint center on the deck top.
- The view turns a part for rotation 1 and 3 and stretches it to the turned footprint. Rotation 2 and 3 draw like 0 and 1. So keep parts boxy.
- A weapon's mount keeps that footprint where the body under it allows. Only the gun head turns, by the gun's facing: rotation times 90 degrees clockwise from the nose.
- Build armor as a front-edge row with its outer face at +X. The view turns it to the side its cells lie on. A mounted side plate is drawn thin on the model's outer face, and rams replace the bumper. Accessories, the rams and spikes, use the same row build. Over a plate they are drawn one armor depth further out, in front of it. Armor rows are 1 or 3 cells long.
  - Keep armor shallow. Front and back plates fill their cell, and spare plates fill their rest, so a strut behind the plate shows on the truck and as a stem in the icon. `check_fit()` in `tools/blender/parts_common_armor.py` stops the build when a vertex lies more than `BACK_DEPTH` (0.40 m, the spaced armor's foot) behind the outer edge. Hold a plate up with a gusset inside its foot, and end cage brackets at a back rail.
- Items stand on their row surface. An engine on its mount cells shows through a cutout in the base. A weapon whose mount stands more than 5 cm above the surface under it stands on a riser post from that surface. The post lifts its turret over the cab ahead and over everything its barrel can sweep, see `gunClearance.ts`.
- Every drawn piece stands on the real base mesh, not on the height map, which keeps only the top of each 10 cm column and misses overhangs. `gunStand.ts` casts rays at the base's triangles. Glass, lamps and the radio light never hold anything up.
  - A gun mount plate moves within its cells, by at most half its own size, to the nearest spot where it sits level on the body, then to the nearest spot fully over the body, and shrinks toward the riser top when none fits. A plate corner more than 2.5 cm over the body gets a leg, the `wmount_leg` model. A corner with nothing below gets a brace to the nearest body wall under it, aimed toward the plate's center first, else toward the truck's center, else straight down, and as a last resort through glass to the solid under it. The plate only takes a spot where every corner gets a leg or a brace. The head, the muzzle and every shot effect follow the moved mount.
  - A good or a part stands where 90% of its real bottom face touches the body and nothing pokes through it. Else it moves within its cells to such a spot. Else a part stands on legs and braces at the corners of its bottom face, a leg that stands on the body moves to the nearest point of the part's real underside when its corner hangs in air, and a brace keeps its corner, and a good is not drawn.
  - Mounted armor keeps its height and moves in until it touches the body behind it.
  - The antenna stands on the highest flat spot of the cab, the tow chain hangs from the real rear underside, and a dent never moves a triangle under a standing piece.
  - `truckSupport.test.ts` checks every piece of every truck in a new world against the real triangles: 90% of a bottom face within 5 cm of support, every plate corner region on a leg or the body, every leg tip on the body, and no hidden mounted part.

The mount stretches to fill the def's footprint, so a 1x3 rifle and a 2x3 cannon of one look differ, and the head keeps its authored size. `weaponHead()` in `src/three/render/weaponHead.ts` assembles the head for both the truck view and the icons.
- `ICON_VIEWS` in `src/three/icons/render.ts` picks the view per category, and `iconView()` maps each entry to it. Every icon uses the diagonal view: a side view with the nose to the right, turned 35° toward the rear and raised 30°, so a barrel reads from lower left to upper right. A part seen straight down reads as flat boxes. Armor is built as a front plate, so it turns a quarter before drawing and shows its outer face. Flip a view and rerun `npm run icons`.
- The style follows the category, and `ICON_STYLES` is its one owner. Parts and goods draw as vector blueprints from `src/three/icons/blueprint.ts`, in colors that `blueprintColors()` mixes from the item's tone: a light line, the tone behind, a deep fill, a deeper shadow, and a pale glass for faces with the `glass` material, so cabs show their windows. Goods draw in the color style: inside the line, each face keeps its model's own color, and the shadow and highlight lie over it see-through. A color patch under `MIN_SHADOW` across drops out and shows the deep fill. Chassis portraits draw toon: each model keeps its flat colors through a three-step ramp under one key light, with a dark outline and crease lines.
- A blueprint's shape is the union of the model's triangles as drawn, so its outline has exact straight edges and sharp corners. Faces turned from the light, by a normal pass, fill with the shadow color. The outline draws thick. Inside it, a model edge draws thin only where its faces turn away from the viewer, so a line marks one part's outline in front of another. A part's own folds are left to the shading. A ray toward the camera hides the parts of an edge behind other faces. Edges close to a model axis draw along it, so dented boxes keep parallel sides. Pieces smaller than `MIN_PIECE` of the drawing drop out, and so do inner lines shorter than `MIN_INNER`. Line widths are a share of one grid row, `OUTER_CELLS` and `INNER_CELLS`, so every item draws the same weight in the grid.
- The truck condition panel and the truck card draw a live render of the truck itself, not a sheet. `TruckPortraits` in `src/three/render/truckPortrait.ts` renders the pristine truck once per truck build through the game's renderer, from above with the nose down and tilted 25°, and the panel tints each part's pixels by its condition. Docs/ui.md describes the panel.
- `src/ui/plans.svg` holds, per chassis, from straight above and nose up, a calmed `plan-<id>` outline, drawn through `truckOutline()` in `src/ui/plans.ts` under an inventory grid that has no garage renderer, as in tests. The game's grids draw the rendered truck instead, as [Rendering and UI](../architecture/render.md) describes. It comes from `blueprintOf()` and is written by `blueprintSvg()` with colors and pens as CSS variables (`--plan-*`), stretched over the inner grid columns and every row with `PLAN_PAD` cells around them, so the body edges run along the outer edges of the wheels. The icon build still writes `top-<id>` views into the sheet and the tinted part blueprints into `src/ui/tint.svg`, and the game draws neither.
- The items sheet `public/icons/items.svg` is one SVG of every blueprint, so icons stay sharp at any slot size. The chassis sheet stays a PNG, `chassis.png`, in the standard paint, with a mask beside it, `chassis-paint.png`, of the same cells and boxes. The mask is drawn flat with no light or outline: red where the body color shows, green for the cab and blue for the gear, black elsewhere. `paintSheetPixels()` in `src/render/palette.ts` recolors the standard sheet with it, scaling each new color by the pixel's shade, so the toon steps and lines carry over and a screen can show any of the palette's paints. The chassis hash covers the standard colors and the mask, so a changed standard paint makes the portraits stale. Both load with the manifest's hash in their URL, so a browser never pairs a cached old sheet with a new manifest.
- `itemTone()` in `src/render/partLooks.ts` gives each item its category tone: weapons, armor, cargo parts and goods, and the rest. `ITEM_TONES` in `src/render/palette.ts` holds the colors: red, graphite, brown and blue. A test keeps the blueprint line at least 4.5:1 against each. Grid boxes, loot and storage chips, standalone item icons and card borders set it as `--tone` through `toneStyle()` in `src/ui/cards.ts` (the HUD weapon panel is the exception: it shows each gun as its red drawing alone, without the tone tile), and the atlases fill their item tiles with it. The tone tells only the category. Shape tells items apart.
- Defs drawn by the same models, like the wheels, transmissions and fuel tanks, draw alike. They need models of their own to look different. Chassis that share a model would rank 1 to 4 by id and draw 1 to 3 thick 45° stripes.
- Each def has one cell, so every screen shows the same picture of it. The manifest records each cell's drawn extent as `box`, and every slot crops to it and fits the drawing inside, upright whatever the item's rotation.
- The truck shop shows each chassis's portrait beside its grid. The portrait is the game's own truck view of the bare chassis at rest, with wheels, suspension, antenna and tow chain, through `bareVehicle()` in `src/sim/factory.ts`. Every portrait gets the same box, and the drawing fits inside it.
- `npm run icons` also writes labeled atlases to `public/icons/atlas/`, with each icon large, at 36 px and at 22 px: `atlas-top.png` and `atlas-diagonal.png` draw every entry in one view, and `atlas-game.png` draws each in the view the game shows. Item tiles sit on their tone. `report.txt` lists the item pairs that differ least at 36 px in each view. They ship in the build, so each deploy serves them at `<build url>icons/atlas/`, but the game never loads them. Look at them after a change.
