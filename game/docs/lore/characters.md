# Characters

Named people of Icarus. Their groups are in [Peoples](peoples.md). J.J. of Waste Of Time Radio is in [Lore](README.md).

Each person gets a sheet, then their story, then their line. The sheet is built on the Fate Core character sheet, without the dice parts, plus the goal, fear and habit of the [Story game](story-game.md). A quest writer reads it to know what the person does in a scene.

- Way: their main way of coping, from [Theme](../theme.md).
- High concept: who they are, in one sentence.
- Trouble: what keeps going wrong for them. It comes from their way.
- Aspects: short phrases that help them in some scenes and hurt them in others. At least one ties them to another person on this page.
- Skills: their best and worst of the player's skills, so a quest can set a check against them.
- Wants, Fears and Habit: what they want, what they fear, and one thing they always do that a player can see.
- Secret: what they hide and from whom. A person's stated reason for a job is often not the real one.
- Their line: how they talk, in one sentence. Full voice sheets for speakers are in [Voices](voices.md).

For example, Agnes Ondra's trouble is that she is afraid to refuse the steward's oil, her habit is reading every meter at dusk, and her fear is being named as the one who talked.

## Alan Graves

- Way: control.
- High concept: The President of Nose, who has led the Central Committee since year 100.
- Trouble: Every rule he adds to save the basin also tightens his own hold, so he cannot let any of it go.
- Aspects: Grey eyes of his grandmother Lena Graves; Buried Mara Kessel's case to keep Pierce quiet; Rations everything, even trust.
- Skills: best social, worst machining.
- Wants: Nose fed, ordered and under his name until he dies.
- Fears: A famine that proves his rationing failed.
- Habit: He reads the ration tally aloud before he lets any meeting talk of anything else.
- Secret: He let the Committee hold back the Old Army's fuel payments so the army would fail and Nose could take its workshops. He hides it from Nose and the Bowl.

The President of Nose has led its Central Committee for 30 years. His grandmother Lena Graves woke in a pod as a girl, and he has her grey eyes. He believes the basin will die without rationing and discipline. He is probably right about that. He can no longer tell the good of Nose from his own hold on it.

## Captain Silver

- Way: control.
- High concept: The Nose Army captain sent as a demotion to run the army post inside the Salvage Yard.
- Trouble: The harder he pushes the Yard toward Nose, the more the Yard crews and his own soldiers turn against him.
- Aspects: Demoted and watched from Nose; Mara Kessel writes everything twice under his roof; Fair in public, cruel in private.
- Skills: best social, worst perception.
- Wants: The Yard under Nose and his old rank back.
- Fears: A life as a demoted captain at the Yard.
- Habit: He walks the Yard gate at dusk where the crews can see him and settles one small dispute in public.
- Secret: He plans to stage a disaster at the Yard to prove it needs Nose. He hides it from the Yard crews, the Administrator and Mara Kessel.

Silver commands the Nose Army post at the Salvage Yard. He was sent there as a demotion, and he means to earn his way back by bringing the Yard under Nose. In public he is a tough and fair officer. In private he is a cruel despot.

He believes the only future for the Yard is to belong to Nose. He is willing to stage a disaster to prove it.

## The Administrator

- Way: fear.
- High concept: Sully Okafor, the old repair foreman who runs the Salvage Yard and keeps the Pierce debtor book shut.
- Trouble: The shut book is his only shield, so he can never use it, and the guns he sells make everyone want the Yard.
- Aspects: The shut Pierce debtor book; Bought the book from Sefa Krol's families; Sells guns and cares for his crews.
- Skills: best machining, worst toughness.
- Wants: The Yard free and the Nose post gone.
- Fears: Someone opens the book or takes the Yard from his crews.
- Habit: He checks the lock of the crane office himself every night.
- Secret: He has a quiet pact with Mara Kessel. He hides it from Captain Silver.

The Administrator is Sully Okafor, once the best repair foreman in the Pierce yard. He led the crews when they took the yard in year 123. He keeps a Pierce debtor book shut in the crane office, and it names what both towns owe Pierce. As long as it stays shut, nobody dares to take the Yard. The Yard is free, and he wants to keep it free. That means getting the Nose post out. He is a crook who sells guns. He also cares for his people as well as he can.

He used to bet on everything. Now he has something to lose, and he does not bet anymore.

His line is "I care for my people as I can."

- Trouble: His crown needs a pack that trusts him, but his ambition turns the pack into an army and costs him the people he leads.
- Aspects: Unacknowledged son of a Nose committee member; Kaplan's doubt at his shoulder; Dagny Brask calls no one king.
- Skills: best social, worst machining.
- Wants: To be king of the camps at any price.
- Fears: That his child's death bought him nothing.
- Habit: He says the price of a raid aloud to his riders before they leave.
- Secret: He hides which Nose committee member is his father, and how his child died, from Kiln Camp.

Ain is the unacknowledged son of a Nose committee member. His child died. After that he fled Nose and started his own gang. Now he wants to be king of the raider camps, whatever it costs. He believes the end justifies the means, and nobody knows the whole of his past.

His line is "I will be king of the camps, and I will pay any price for it."

Absalom Reyne began to preach at the Sunward's fire around year 118, and people call him the Preacher. Mattias Kade made him the order's voice. He wrote their teachings about the thing in the crater, and he talks himself into every word a little more each dawn. He has never been as close to the green glow as he tells his followers.

## Mattias Kade

- Way: control.
- High concept: The head of the Sunward, a Nose-born Given sworn in year 85, who wears the dead Keeper's coat.
- Trouble: The order is his only while it keeps riding, so he can never stop the vigil he does not believe in.
- Aspects: The Keeper's coat, never taken off; Knows what the Apprentice told him; Moved the camp to the Hollow; Made Absalom Reyne the Preacher.
- Skills: best driving, worst perception.
- Wants: The order fed and himself on top.
- Fears: The glow itself, and the fever that comes from sleeping near it.
- Habit: He touches the coat's lining, where an unreadable page is sewn.
- Secret: The Apprentice told him in year 128 that nothing in the crater feeds or calls. He believes it, and keeps the vigil anyway.

Kade put on Oren Vey's coat the night Oren walked into the light, and he has led the order since. He is practical and cold, and he talks like a man counting cars. He moved the order's sleeping camp to a hollow deep in the wastes because the fever was killing his men, and kept the night vigil because the vigil keeps them together.

## The Apprentice

- Way: control.
- High concept: Teo, once the head of the Lunar Society, cast out in year 121, who carries a blue eye and every implant he could find.
- Trouble: Each implant lets him see more and makes him harder for anyone to trust.
- Aspects: A blue eye from the cradle room; Cast out by Ines Abend and the Lunar Society; Sold the last number to the Preacher; Cast out Oren Vey, who founded the Sunward.
- Skills: best perception, worst social.
- Wants: A new start for humanity before the basin dies out.
- Fears: The basin dies before the new start begins.
- Habit: He speaks in sermons to anyone who stands still near him.
- Secret: He read the last marks in the central hull's shadow and knows where the seed vault lies. He hides it from everyone. He told Mattias Kade alone that the glow is a burning engine.

The Apprentice was a member of the Lunar Society until it cast him out. He found implants in the ship's wreck and put a blue eye in his own head. Through it he sees marks on the wreck that nobody else can see. He has filled his body with every implant he could find, and he is over 150 years old.

He believes the people of the basin are slowly dying out and that only a new start can save humanity. He has not told anyone what that new start would be.

His most devoted student, Oren Vey, copied the count around year 58. Teo burned the pages and sent him away, and Oren went to the crater dreamers and founded the Sunward. In year 128 the Apprentice bargained with Mattias Kade: one night in the central hull's shadow, under the order's escort, for the count's last number. He walked away after it and has no part in what the order does.

He speaks in sermons. One of them goes like this:

> Do you fear insanity? The world we know is branches upon branches of an old tree. The tree is reason itself, and the branches are the ways of reasoning. The tree is dead. People are like ants, eating it from the inside. To find a new path we must start from the roots and face insanity if we must.

She fears a famine like the one in year 70, and she would pay raiders forever to avoid one.

Her line is "Every sack that goes to Kiln is written on the gate."

## Agnes Ondra

- Way: fear.
- High concept: The keeper of Dustwell's pump book of her late uncle Wendell.
- Trouble: She writes the truth in her book, but she is afraid to refuse the steward's oil to his face.
- Aspects: Wendell Ondra's pump book under her pillow; Takes Aurel Brede's barrel of oil; Sava Kettering sits on a drum at her gate.
- Skills: best machining, worst social.
- Wants: The sealed copy read in the Bowl and the oil sent back before the frost.
- Fears: Aurel Brede, and being named as the one who talked.
- Habit: She reads every meter at dusk and writes it in the book.
- Secret: She made a sealed copy of the book, and she took the steward's oil. She hides both from Aurel Brede and Sava Kettering.

Agnes keeps the pump at Dustwell and the pump book her uncle Wendell kept before he died at the Green Pit sluice. She sleeps with the book under her pillow. Every week the pump brings up less, and the water trucks load less. The Archon's household sends its own tanker to the pump every week, and a Senate order made her take the meter off its hose, so its loads go in no book. Over the winter of year 129 she made a sealed copy of the book. In autumn of year 130 the household steward Aurel Brede left her an envelope: a barrel of oil a month, as Wendell got. She was afraid to refuse it to his face. She means to send it back before the frost. Every driver who loads reads her the big dial, and she writes the number with the driver's name. She wants a driver who read the dial to carry the copy to Bowl and swear to the number, since a driver has no stake in Bowl's water. A household watchman, Sava Kettering, sits on a drum by her gate.

Her line is "I did not come to take your water. I came to write down how much of it there is."

## Mirela Duarte

- Way: oppose.
- High concept: The keeper of the Canal book at the lowest lock house, the last proud Duarte of the canals.
- Trouble: She speaks first and thinks after, so her words cost her kin and her allies.
- Aspects: Cousin Tomas serves in a Bowl coat at her lock; Counts every barrel the water trucks pour into the canals; Agnes Ondra's copy sits beside her book.
- Skills: best perception, worst social.
- Wants: The proof read on the Senate floor, so the Senate splits and the Canal families win back the lock share.
- Fears: The Canal families fade away unheard.
- Habit: She counts every barrel the water trucks pour in at the lock house and writes it in the open book.
- Secret: None.

Mirela keeps the Canal book for the Canal families, the last proud Duarte of the canals. She speaks first and thinks after. The book lives in a lock house on the lowest canal in Bowl, far below the Archon's terraces. Her cousin Tomas serves ten years in the Bowl patrols for scraping a meter's chalk off a sluice, and he serves them at her own lock in a Bowl coat. The water trucks from Dustwell pour their loads into the canals at her lock house, and she counts every barrel in the Canal book. She does not want the household's tanker metered as much as she wants the proof read on the Senate floor, so the Senate splits and the Canal families win back the lock share they lost in year 90. Her lock crew takes household wages through the dry season of year 130, and she does not.

Tomas saw the household's tanker unload on the top terrace on patrol and wrote it in a report, which the steward filed unread. At the lock he told the tenant drivers about it. The steward then bought his silence with oil for his mother, and the same week Mirela made him say it again on her steps.

Her line is "The water is ours, and we will count every barrel of it."
