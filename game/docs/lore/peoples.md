# Peoples

The groups of Icarus and what they believe. Each one copes with the world in one of the five ways from [Theme](../theme.md). A group leans one way, but its people vary. No group is right, and each one pays for its way.

## Nose

Way: control.

Nose is the town built around a ship's wreck on a mountain. The Central Committee governs it, led by President Alan Graves. The Nose Army keeps the order.

Nose runs the basin's only workshop line that builds whole engines and guns. It built the line from Old Orchard's machine tools, hauled up the mountain once the army was gone. Nose plans its whole economy. It rations food, water and work, so most people there are safe and fed. Its leaders are deeply corrupt, and the army is brutal to anyone who speaks against them.

Its motto is "Unity above all." Nose believes the basin can survive only through careful rationing and strict discipline, and it wants to bring all of Icarus under that order.

The cost is that whatever does not fit the order gets crushed.

In the game, the Nose Army patrols the roads around Nose.

## Bowl

Way: fear.

Bowl is a farm built in terraces down the inside of an impact crater. Its people are citizens and tenants. A person earns citizenship by serving ten years in the Bowl patrols, and only citizens vote. Tenants have fewer rights and less money than the people of Nose, but much more freedom.

The Senate of citizens governs Bowl. It elects Archons to carry out its decisions. Bowl's agents in other towns are called Episkopoi. Old men run the Senate. It often splits on the questions that matter, so it acts slowly. Bowl has more people than Nose.

Bowl does not want to grow. It wants to keep its walls and its fields safe. It would still destroy Nose if it could, and it calls that defense.

The cost is that life shrinks to fit the walls, and the tenants who feed Bowl have no voice in it.

The Bowl road patrol is paid from the Granary count, the north-western convoy's fee and tenant shares. Tenants may serve in it, and ten years in it earn a vote. It keeps only the Bowl's roads.

In the game, the Bowl Farmers patrol the roads around Bowl.

## The Old Army

Way: control.

The Old Army stood between Nose and Bowl after the war between them. It had the best drivers and the best weapons in the basin, and it ran the workshops that made them. It was a military dictatorship. It stayed neutral and fought only when the whole basin was in danger. Its one goal was to stop another war.

Old Orchard was its depot. The army ran short of food, and then it was gone. The trucks still stand where they stopped. Nobody in Nose or Bowl says out loud why it left.

The cost was that it held the peace by holding all the guns, and it could not feed itself.

## Guidestar Guild

Way: embrace.

The Guidestar Guild carries messages across the basin by courier and by radio relay, for a fee. It is owned in shares, held in a trust by both towns so that no single buyer can take it over.
The Guild serves every side and sells news to all of them. It does not like J.J., because she gives news away for free from a mast it cannot find.

The cost is that the Guild treats everything as news for sale, including the secrets that get people hurt.

In the game, the Guild's couriers drive the roads.

## Pierce Corporation

Way: control.

Pierce Corporation insures salvage runs into the wreck fields. It works like a casino. Pierce bets that the truck comes back, and if it does not, Pierce pays the family. Its offices also lend money.

Pierce insures only trucks that carry a gun. More than any law, this rule put a gun on every truck.

Rumor says both Nose and Bowl owe Pierce so much that the Corporation decides more than either town admits.

The cost is that Pierce turns every life on the road into a bet.

## Salvage Yard

Way: embrace.

The Salvage Yard was the Pierce yard until year 123, when its repair crews stopped answering to Pierce and hung a crane over the stacked wrecks. The Yard sells hull pieces, relics and the Old Army's stripped guns. The Administrator runs it. A Nose Army post under Captain Silver sits inside its gate.

The cost is that the Yard lives by selling guns to everyone, and everyone wants to own it.

## The Marshalls

Way: control.

The Marshalls keep the road book at the Roadhouse, a fenced Old World yard around a Guild relay mast on the open plain north of the Nose. They are few: a handful of drivers in fast plated cars, most of them old Lincolns. Their first two cars were Commander Lind's staff cars from the Old Orchard motor pool.

A lawful driver who was attacked or robbed on the open road, or whose kin was killed there, brings the wrong to the Roadhouse, or a driver who saw it does. The book writes the claim, the answer, the witnesses and the price. A Marshall rides out, asks the drivers on the road where they last saw the culprit, and asks him to pay. Only a driver who refuses gets a fight. The Roadhouse takes a cut of every closed case. It answers to no town, and it can buy a driver off a town's post by paying the town its price.

The name comes from a north-western guard's tin badge stamped with an old word nobody here could read. Jonah Feld brought it home from the north-west, and drivers on the plain started calling the people at the mast the marshals.

They take no claim against a raider and no claim from one. Raiders have no town to buy anyone off, and nothing a hearing can hold them to. The book writes such a wrong as unanswered.

Every town lets them. The Roadhouse costs no town anything, pays the towns' price when it buys a driver off a post, and stands on Guild ground that both towns hold in trust.

The cost is that the Roadhouse puts a price on every wrong. A driver with money buys his way out, a wrong with nobody to pay for it goes unanswered, and a claim waits until a car is free to ride for it.

In the game, the Marshalls ride out from the Roadhouse after drivers a witness has reported.

## Raiders

Way: oppose.

Raiders live in two camps, Scrapjaw Camp in the north and Kiln Camp in the south. They refuse the order that Nose and Bowl try to build, and they take what they need from the roads. To a raider, robbery is business. Killing is a last resort, because a dead driver hauls no cargo next week. Kiln Camp takes a fee from haulers who pay and strips trucks that neither pay nor fight.

The cost is that raiders live by taking from people who build things, and they never build anything that lasts.

## The Given

Way: escape.

When the ship fell, hundreds of its escape pods came down across the basin, and people climbed out of them. They were pale, wore grey suits, spoke a tongue nobody knew and remembered almost nothing before waking. Basin people called them the Given, after the cry "Given by the sky".

The Given were never one faction. Most went to the Nose, the wreck that looked like home, and they built it alongside the Garrison's deserters. Others joined the Creed's refugees in the crater that became the Bowl. Today most people in the basin have a Given forebear.

Their descendants carry them in small ways: pale skin and grey eyes, a few words of a lost tongue in songs and names, a grey suit or a pod tag stamped with common digits kept in a family chest. Many of the Sunward and most of the Children of Noah are Given families. They are the ones who dream of the crater, and the ones who wait for the ship to come back.

The cost is that they live as guests in a world they did not choose, still half waiting to be collected.

## Drivers of the road

The other drivers have no town to speak for them.

- Traders embrace the world. They buy where goods are cheap and sell where goods are dear.
- Scavengers embrace it too. They live off the wrecks and ask no questions about where the wrecks came from.
- Roamers escape. They keep moving and never stay anywhere long.
- Vultures oppose. They follow fights and strip the losers.
- Mercs embrace the world as it is. They sell their guns to whoever pays and leave the deciding to others.
- Couriers embrace the road. Guild couriers run the open road in pairs.
- The Burnt Convoy families oppose. Vulture families lived in the burnt trucks of an old convoy until the road crews cleared the wrecks. They live on the north side of the Salvage Yard now and are first to any wreck.

## Faiths

### The Reclaimer's Creed

Way: embrace.

The Creed is the most common faith in the basin. It teaches that Icarus is a test of whether humanity is worthy. If the people of the basin find its true purpose and fulfill it, they will be saved, or the world will be given back as it was.

The cost is that believers can wait for a reward instead of living, and they blame the people they think are unworthy.

### The Sunward

Way: fear.

The Sunward say they serve whatever lies in the Fallen Sun's crater. Their teaching says it feeds on what falls from the sky and will burn the basin unless it is served, and that their founder, the Keeper, went into the light to serve it and will come back. When they find a stranger in the wreck fields, they cry "Given by the sky!"

They began as wreck-fever dreamers camped by the green glow. Oren Vey, a student cast out of the Alpha Tower, made himself their Keeper, and hunger made their law: whatever comes into the crater is theirs. After the Keeper walked into the light and the old singers left, the order kept only its sworn men. [The Sunward](sunward.md) has the order's history.

Today they sleep by day somewhere in the wastes, and at dusk eight cars cross open ground to the crater with their lamps in a line. On the road they answer nobody. In the crater they keep a vigil for the Keeper until shortly before dawn, and they invite strangers to come and watch the light. On some nights three fast cars hunt the roads near the crater and drag a truck back. Drivers keep away from the crater after dark.

Mattias Kade wears the Keeper's coat and leads them. The Preacher writes and chants their teachings. The members are not one kind of person:

- Kade believes nothing, and keeps the order because it is his.
- The Preacher half believes, and believes a little more each dawn.
- Most of the sworn men say the words for food, a place at the fire and the fear their cars bring them on the road. Nobody says aloud what they have done.
- A few young recruits believe every word, and watch the others for doubt.

The cost is that they serve a thing they cannot see, and the fiction is the only thing that keeps them from seeing what they have become.

### The Ephemeralists

Way: escape.

The Ephemeralists believe the basin is a passing shape of a higher reality. They meditate and read dreams to see past it. Many of them stop working fields or driving, because nothing here is real enough to fix.

The cost is that they leave this world to the people who still believe in it.

### The Children of Noah

Way: escape.

The Children of Noah believe another ship will come and take the worthy away.

Their singers came from the crater. In year 110 Yara Sorrel led the Sunward's old singers, the last believers among the dreamers, out to the Children of Noah, and the deserter Joss Ardo sings with them now.

The cost is that they live for an arrival that may never come.

### The Lunar Society

Way: control.

The Lunar Society studied the ship and the sky from the Alpha Tower Observatory. Its astronomers tracked the stars to learn where the ship came from and whether more are coming. The Society is disbanded now, and its last members are scattered.

The Apprentice was once one of them.

The cost is that knowledge became a way to own the unknown, and some members could not stop.
