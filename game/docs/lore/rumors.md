# Rumors

Things people in Icarus say. Some are true, some are half true and some are false. A rumor is evidence, never an answer, as [Theme](../theme.md) describes.
## What the ship was

Nobody agrees, and each guess has its believers.

- The ship was a launch platform, and the basin was built to catch its falling stages.
- The ship is a power plant, and the reactor in the Fallen Sun was its heart, not its engine.
- The ship came from a colony on another world, and its people came home to die.
- The basin is purgatory, or Hell, and the ship is how the damned arrived.
- The ship was built to carry people away from a dying Earth, and it never got out.

## The sky

- Astronomers of the old Alpha Tower Observatory tried to work out where the ship came from by the movement of the stars.
- The Observatory found something else coming toward the basin, and kept it secret.
- One night two moons of different colors stood over the basin.
- A philosopher lives alone in the Alpha Tower. He is the smartest person in Icarus.

## The wreck and its tech

- The ship's control room was destroyed in the war between Nose and Bowl.
- Nose soldiers were locked inside the control room during the war, and nobody opened it again.
- Before the fall, people had machines that moved memories from one person to another.
- Somewhere under the Fallen Sun lies a warhead big enough to end the whole basin.
- Nose has the secret of a weapon that goes through any armor, and it guards the secret closely.
- Some drivers who sleep near the green glow wake up knowing things they never learned.

## Powers and money

- Pierce Corporation controls both Nose and Bowl through what their rulers owe it.
- Pierce insures salvage runs like a casino. It bets that the truck comes back.
- The Old Army wanted to bury the Fallen Sun under a great bomb, so that nobody could fight over it again.
- The Old Army did not run out of food. It was starved out on purpose.
- Pierce sold the Old Army's guns to Scrapjaw, then made every driver buy a gun to be insured.
- Somebody in the Pierce office sold the roads of insured trucks to robbers. He went north-west and never came back.
- The Nose Committee rents its workshop line with Pierce money.
- The Administrator keeps a book in the crane office that could bring down both towns.
- The old Senate families on the top terraces send a tanker to Dustwell that never shows up in any book.

## The road

- A steel truck drives the wreck fields at night. It cannot be stopped, and nobody has survived meeting it.
- The Lady of the Road is a voice on the radio that warns drivers of danger ahead.
- One day the basin will dry out completely, and everyone in it will die of thirst.
- A Nose brake rod is cheap steel, and on a sharp bend it will snap and nothing will slow you.
- In the north-west there are towns whose trucks carry no guns, because a guard paid by every town keeps the roads.
- The glass on the Glass Flats all points the same way, toward the Fallen Sun.
- The number the Sunward chant at the rim is the year the radio count stops.
- Kiln riders let you go if you fight hard enough. They strip you if you don't.
- The marshals can reach any stranded truck on the plain before nightfall.
- The marshals know every truck's route before it leaves.
- Once the clerk at the Roadhouse writes your name down, nobody gets out of paying.
- The Sunward take strangers into the crater, and the strangers do not come back.
- At dusk a line of lamps crosses open ground to the crater, and before dawn it goes back. Call those cars on the radio and nobody answers.
- Cars that stop near the crater at night get dragged into the dark on a chain. Families find the furrows by the road in the morning.
- The Keeper walked into the light singing, and his coat is still warm on the man who wears it.
- The beacon keepers keep their lamps dark at night now, because lamps draw the Sunward's cars.
- Joss Ardo of the Children of Noah got out of the Sunward. His friend did not.
- Some people out past the glass live without towns and without trucks, and nobody knows how.
- A crew once camped in the wreck and their sentry fell asleep. In the morning the sentry was gone, and his boots were still standing by the fire.

## The six words

Every stage of [The end of the Old World](old-world-end.md) has five rumors here, each from a different kind of speaker. No single rumor tells the truth. The truth is what the five share, so a player who hears several of them can work out what the stage was.

Rules for these rumors:

- Each rumor keeps one true piece of its stage and wraps it in the speaker's own faith, trade or fear.
- Some rumors use the wrong name. They tell one stage under another stage's word, or under one of its other names from [The end of the Old World](old-world-end.md), like the Crucible or the Endless Age for the Chaos. The player has to match the story to the stage, not the name. Each other name is marked below.
- A rumor never says how many years ago something happened or how long an era lasted.
- Spread the five rumors of a stage across different places and speakers, so no one place gives the whole picture.

### Spiral

- An illiterate farmer: "My grandmother said the Spiral was a bad harvest that never ended. Each year the fields gave less, so each family took a little from the next field. Then there was less again."
- A tribesman: "The Spiral is the sun. It turned its face on the world and burned it, and it is still turning. That's why you cover your head."
- An old soldier: "The Spiral wasn't one war. Every country robbed the next before the next could rob it, and none of them could afford to stop first."
- A raider, who calls it the Chaos: "The Chaos came before the ships, back when the big men still had their cities. One of them cheated, so the rest had to, and it went round and round till nothing was left to cheat for. Same as the road now."
- A preacher: "The Spiral was the first test, and we failed it. Every people chose its own bread over its neighbor's, and God let them keep turning until they fell."

### Cry

- A learned man: "The Cry was a learned man's sum. A man named Gulke worked out that the earth was running dry and people had one short chance to leave it. Nobody checked the sum, because too many people were paid to believe it."
- A storyteller, who calls him Gulk: "Gulk cried out from the mountain that the world would die before his grandchildren grew old. Every king heard him, and every king was afraid."
- A believer: "The Cry was the call to board. The worthy heard it and made ready."
- A mystic: "The Cry was the moment people first saw that the world was only passing through, and screamed."
- A clerk, who calls it the Race: "The Race started with one man saying the ground was used up. Once the biggest buyer believed him, every other buyer had to."

### Race

- A factory hand: "In the Race a fitter worked from eight in the morning to nine at night, six days of seven, on a ship he would never ride. Then he went home to a house lit by a generator."
- A tribesman: "In the Race the gods rode machines across the sky against each other, and the world was born from their dust."
- An illiterate farmer: "In the Race they burned the orchards to feed the furnaces. Whoever burned fastest would win."
- A preacher, who calls it the Exodus: "In the Exodus every nation built an iron tower to climb to heaven, and fed it everything it had, even its children's bread."
- A raider: "The Race was a race to the bottom of the barrel. Whoever scraped it fastest got the last of it."

### Exodus

- A preacher: "In the Exodus the worthy rose to heaven on pillars of fire. The rest of us were left here to prove ourselves."
- A bandit: "In the Exodus the judges went down to hell in fire, and left the world to us."
- A believer: "The ships went up in the Exodus. One day one of them comes back for whoever kept the lights burning."
- An officer: "The rich left in the Exodus. The ones who stayed were the ones who lost the fight for a seat, and the ones who wanted to be kings of what was left. Ask yourself which kind built your town."
- A grandmother, who calls it the Cry: "The Cry was the sound of the great ships going up. It was so loud the sky tore."

### Chaos

- An old soldier: "The Chaos was every army left without a master. They fought over whatever the rich left behind."
- A preacher, who calls it the Crucible: "The Crucible was the fire that burns the unworthy out of a people. We came out of it, so we must be worth something."
- An elder, who calls it the Endless Age: "My grandmother called it the Endless Age. Nobody remembered it beginning, and everybody thought it would never end."
- A scavenger, who calls it the Crucible: "In the Crucible every town fought every other town, because the masters were gone and nobody would take orders from anybody."
- A mystic: "The Chaos was the world screaming because it was left alone. A child left alone breaks everything in the house."
