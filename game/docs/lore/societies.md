# Societies and craft

How the organized societies of the basin live, dress and make things, and how its artisans work. It answers to [Theme](../theme.md) and to [Design](../DESIGN.md) principles 8 (New World vs Old World contrasts) and 10 (the map is a miniature of a coherent world). Every fact here also answers to the [Sense check](README.md#sense-check).

## The committee's brief

The committee asked for this page in two messages. They are kept as written, spellings included.

> The overall world vibes
> - Organized societies behave like 18-19 century powers. They have complex social structures, family heirlooms, elite vs commons conflicts, political infighting, etc. Society behaves pre-industrialization and pre-globalization. So you have a clothing manufacturing plant, but its a bunch of people working looms, not Zara. 
> - Patch world of overgrown memes. Each society is isolated enough (due to the hostile wastes between them) that they are their own bubble. They are all heavy on LARPing a certain symbolics, making them different like no countries in real world could be. For example, Bowl is an ancient Greece-like republic with togas embroidered with spaceship metal brouches and such, while Nose is a military dictatorship with parade uniforms like early 20th century Russian empire, and combat uniforms your typical 20 century khakis. In real life these would mix and become quite similar, somewhere in the middle. Here they stay separate, which makes for contrasts.
> - A good overall reference is WW1 where you could have mamalukes on horses charging tanks, that kind of weird divides
>

> Also: add to the vibe of the world: artisans. Since we are pre-industrialization, it's very character driven. There can be a most famous mason and his apprentice. Two best wars truck mechanics/constructors, rivaling. That kind of stuff.

The people named in the brief are examples of a kind of person. They are not people to add. The rest of this page turns the brief into rules.

## Old powers, new machines

Daily life runs at the mid-1900s level, as [World facts](world.md#technology) records. Organized society does not. A town is run like an 18th or 19th century power, and the machines are simply newer than the manners. Nothing is mass-made, nothing is global, and a stranger's word counts for little.

- Households matter more than offices. The Archon's [Marr household](timeline.md#hidden-truths) has its own hose at the Dustwell tanks, the Graves father handed Nose to his son, and Agnes Ondra keeps the Dustwell pump book that her uncle kept before her.
- An elite and the commons pull against each other. Bowl's citizens vote and its tenants feed them without a voice. Nose's Committee rations what its people eat and say. See [Bowl](peoples.md#bowl) and [Nose](peoples.md#nose).
- Leaders fight among themselves. The Bowl Senate splits on every question that matters. The Nose Committee buried a proven case because it owes Pierce too much.
- Families keep heirlooms. A Given family keeps a grey suit or a pod tag in a chest, as [The Given](peoples.md#the-given) records. A scene may show another kept thing when it passes the sense check.
- Work is handwork. Bowl's cloth comes from the weavers' houses, rooms of people at looms, not from a mill. Its parts come from machinists at benches. A thing is known by the house or the hands that made it.
- The Nose workshop line is the one place close to a factory. Old Orchard's machine tools stand in a row, each worked by hand, and turn out engines and guns by the crate. It makes more than any master could and makes it worse. The cheap brake rod and the cheap copy of Otto Brann's swivel are the evidence.

## A patchwork of borrowed looks

The wastes between places keep each society apart. Each one took its look, its titles and its ceremony from the Old World leftovers it found, and plays them harder than any real country did. Nothing blends them, so neighbors look nothing alike.

- Bowl plays a republic in the manner of ancient Greece. It already has a Senate, Archons and Episkopoi. Citizens wear draped robes like togas, pinned with brooches cut from ship metal. The robe shows the wearer is a citizen with a vote. Tenants work the terraces in plain work clothes.
- Nose plays a military state. Its officers and Committee wear parade dress like an early 20th century imperial army: braid, epaulettes, polished boots, medals. Its soldiers in the field wear plain 20th century khaki. Nose dresses as the army it began as. The Committee's titles stay as they are, so braid over a Central Committee is one more contrast.
- Across the basin many old cultures mix, as [World facts](world.md#the-old-world) says. Inside one society one borrowed look leads.
- Nobody in the basin knows where its look came from or names the old country it copies. Ancient Greece and the early 20th century Russian Empire are references for writers and artists only. They never appear in game text, since no real nation exists today.
- Smaller groups borrow too. The [Marshalls](peoples.md#the-marshalls) took their name from the old word on Jonah Feld's tin badge. Another group gets a look only when a scene needs one and it passes the sense check. Only Bowl and Nose have a set look for now.

## Old beside new

The committee's picture is the First World War, with cavalry charging tanks. The basin has no horses and none are added. The contrast here is ceremony beside the gun truck: a citizen with a ship-metal brooch climbing into a plated truck with a Brann swivel, parade braid at a gate where Machiners plug into their seats. Show it in one frame, through dress and work, and say nothing about it.

## Artisans

Almost nothing is mass-made, so the basin knows its makers by name.

- A master's name travels the road in drivers' talk. A famous welder, mason, weaver, still boss or truck builder is known in every town that uses the work.
- Masters mark their work, as Otto Brann cuts his mark into every weld. A driver reads a mark like a name. It says who answers for the part. It gives no better stat, since the game has none.
- Apprentices learn at a master's bench, often in the family, as Lenka welds beside her grandfather and signs her own pivot rings. See [Otto Brann](characters.md#otto-brann).
- Masters of one craft are rivals for the same buyers and the same name. Brann's signed swivel against the Nose line's cheap copy is the one rivalry already in the lore.

Rules for a new artisan:

- They make something a town or the road uses, by what `src/data/` says that place makes.
- They run one corner of a place, as [People and scale](world.md#people-and-scale) says.
- They pass the sense check. [Item lore](README.md#item-lore) still names no person, so an item entry may speak of a maker's mark without a name.
