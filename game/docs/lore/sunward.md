# The Sunward

The internal history of the Sunward order, from the founder's schism in year 58 to the night order of year 130. It was generated with the [Story game](story-game.md): four agents played Teo, Oren Vey's circle, the dreamers of the glow and the Bowl council over nine turns of eight years, with Fate dice, compels, consequences, the age death rule and successors. Their choices made this history. The public lines in [Timeline](timeline.md) are what the basin knows of it.

Way: fear, as [Peoples](peoples.md#the-sunward) records. The order fears the glow, and its people fear losing the food, the fire and the cars the order gives them.

## Public history

What people in the basin know or can find out.

- Years 58 to 66. Teo of the Alpha Tower catches his most devoted student, Oren Vey, rubbing the plate's bars onto paper, burns the pages and sends him away. Oren walks to the crater, where wreck-fever dreamers have camped by the green glow since year 35. The dreamer Pell Ardo walks into the light and is not seen again. Oren tells the camp that Pell walked into the answer Teo's bars lead to, and that the tone from the hull is what they dream of.
- Year 70. Mother Neve Sorrel, who led the dreamers, falls sick with fever and dies in year 71. The Bowl's pact of grain for work at the rim goes to Oren. He names the great hull in the crater the Fallen Sun, the camp the Sunward and himself the Keeper of the tone.
- Year 80. Rifles from the Sunward's rim salvage trade arm the Bowl tenants who ambush a Nose grain convoy. The Bowl council makes the Sunward wardens of the rim: no rifle leaves the crater without their mark.
- Year 89. The Truce of the Fallen Sun is signed on the Sunward's rim. Councillor Ivo Marr writes the clause that no weapon may be drawn in sight of the glow, which ends the rim gun trade. Young Given who fled the Grain War swear an oath to Oren, not to the glow. From then on the order has two kinds of member: old singers who believe, and sworn men who came for food and a place.
- The 90s. Hunger brings the law of the given: whatever comes into the crater is the order's, and so is what it carries. A north-western scavenger's truck breaks down in the crater, and its driver is led into the light. "Given by the sky!" becomes the order's call. The singers call it murder at the fire. The Bowl renews its grain only while no stranger is taken from the roads.
- Year 101. Yara Sorrel, Neve's daughter and the singers' voice, dares the Keeper at the fire to answer the glow himself. Oren walks into the light in front of the order and is not seen again. His coat lies on the rim stone. Mattias Kade, a Nose-born Given sworn in year 85, puts it on.
- Year 110. Yara leads the old singers out of the crater to the Children of Noah on the debris trail, and they become the Children of Noah's singers. Only the sworn men stay. The same year a Bowl tenant grain truck vanishes on the rim road at night. The Senate blames the loose Old Army riflemen.
- Around year 118. Absalom Reyne starts to preach at the order's fire, and people call him the Preacher. He teaches that the thing in the crater feeds on what falls from the sky and will burn the basin unless it is served, and that the Keeper went into the light to serve it and will come back.
- Year 119. The deserter Joss Ardo, Pell Ardo's nephew, gets out of the order with the singers' help and tells the Senate what he saw. The Senate stops all grain to the Sunward.
- Year 121. The Lunar Society casts Teo out, and people call him the Apprentice. Yara Sorrel dies.
- Year 128. The Apprentice strikes the last bars in the central hull's shadow, under the order's escort, and tells nobody what else he found. He lets the Preacher hear the last bar ring, and the Preacher sings that tone at the rim at dawn as the glow's word. The Sunward bar the Lunar Society from the rim. The Apprentice walks away and has no part in what the order does after.
- Year 128. Joss Ardo's friend Cobb Navarre is caught leaving the order at night and is never seen again.
- Years 127 to 130. The order no longer sleeps in the crater. Its trucks leave from somewhere in the wastes at dusk, cross open ground to the crater with their lamps in a line, keep the night there and go before dawn. Drivers keep away from the crater after dark.

## The night order today

- The Hollow. The order rests in a walled scrap fort deep in the wastes, far off every road and out of sight of every town. Its one gate faces the crater and stays shut, and the order's cars stand drawn up in the open before it. A shrine of hull plate stands inside. Its lamps burn there at night. Nobody on the roads has found it, or nobody who found it says so.
- The Cavalcade. Late in the day eight cars pull out of their bays before the Hollow's gate one by one and ride to the crater in a column, across the open desert and wide of every road and town, a long ride that brings the last of them to the light as the sun goes. On the road they say nothing and answer nobody: the vigil's rule is silence until the crater. At the crater each car keeps a post in a ring round the light, watching for the Keeper's return, through the night until just before dawn. Then they drive home in a column and are inside the walls soon after sunrise.
- The invitation. The first stranger they find in the crater at night hears a soft voice on the radio: come and watch the light. A stranger who agrees is told to drive in. One who turns back is hunted. One who refuses is hooked and dragged to the breach. The order never says what the light does.
- The hunters. On some nights three fast cars go to the roads near the crater instead, take a lone truck with hooked lines and drag it to the breach.
- The cars. Wreck-built buggies and stripped Old Army cars with hooked line guns, because a dragged truck arrives with its cargo.
- The Preacher stays at the rim and sings one held low tone at dawn before the cars leave. He never goes near the glow.

## Structure

- The coat. Mattias Kade wears the Keeper's coat and never takes it off. The order says the coat leads it until the Keeper comes back.
- The inner circle. Kade, the Preacher and the oldest sworn men. They decide who rides and who is taken.
- The Cavalcade. Eight drivers, the order's best, each with a car of their own.
- The hunters. Three young drivers on the fastest cars.
- The sworn men. Everyone else at the Hollow: cooks, welders, watchers, the sick.
- The young believers. A few recruits who believe every word, mostly children of sworn men who never knew the singers.

## Beliefs

- Professed: the thing in the crater feeds on what falls from the sky, and it will burn the basin unless it is served. Whatever the sky gives belongs to it. The Keeper went into the light to serve it and will come back. The last tone is the glow's word.
- Private: most sworn men do not believe. They say the words for food, a place at the fire and the fear their cars bring them on the road. Nobody says aloud what they have done, because saying it would mean admitting what they are.
- The young believers believe every word and watch the others for doubt.

## Recruitment, food and vehicles

- Recruits came first from the dreamers, then from Given families of the Nose who fled the Grain War, and later from hungry drivers with nothing else.
- Food came from the Bowl's grain for rim work until year 119, then from offerings, from the rim salvage and from what dragged trucks carry.
- Cars came from the crater's wrecks and the Old Army's abandoned trucks, then from the trucks they drag in.

## People

- Oren Vey, the founder and the Keeper. Teo's devoted student, cast out for rubbing the plate's bars onto paper. He wanted the tone for himself.
- Mattias Kade, head of the order since year 101. See [Characters](characters.md#mattias-kade).
- Absalom Reyne, the Preacher. See [Characters](characters.md#the-preacher).
- Yara Sorrel, the singers' voice, who dared Oren and led the singers out. Died year 121.
- Joss Ardo, the deserter. See [Characters](characters.md#joss-ardo).
- Pell Ardo, the dreamer who walked into the light in the 60s. Joss's uncle.
- Cobb Navarre, a sworn man caught leaving in year 128.

## Hidden truths

Only the evidence of these reaches the game.

- Oren never believed the glow called anyone. He kept one page of Teo's bar rubbings sewn into his coat and could not find the last bar alone. He walked into the light in year 101 out of fear of being forgotten, and burned in the breach.
- Oren's men took a cut of the rifles sold to the tenants in year 80. Dagny Brask bought her rifles through Oren's marked trade.
- Teo knew from year 80 that the glow kills by fever. His student Imre lived at the crater two years and found that the dreamers who dream hardest sleep nearest the glow and die first. Teo told only Imre.
- Kade's sworn men took the Bowl tenant truck in year 110 by the law of the given, and walked its driver into the light. Kade ordered it so the order would eat, and because the driver had seen their lamps.
- Kade found the page in the coat's lining. It shows a row of bars and means nothing to him, and nothing to the Preacher. The teaching needed the last tone, which the page does not hold, which is why Kade rode to the Apprentice.
- In year 128 the Apprentice told Kade alone, in plain words, that nothing in the crater feeds or calls: it is a burning engine that sickens whoever sleeps near it. Kade moved the order's sleeping camp to the Hollow because of it, and kept the vigil because the vigil keeps the order.
- The green glow is the ship's reactor core, leaking. The light burns whoever goes into it, and the crater keeps the bones under the breach. Long nights near it bring wreck fever, and the fever brings the dreams.
- Kade believes nothing and keeps the order because it is his. The Preacher half believes, and talks himself into his own words a little more each dawn.
