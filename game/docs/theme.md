# Theme

This page holds the idea behind ROAM. The game never says it in words. Lore, art, sound, characters and content all answer to it. When a choice is unclear, pick the option that serves this page.

## The heart

The world is too big to understand or control, and people live in it anyway.

Living means more than enduring the harsh conditions. People see beauty, try to make sense of what happened, and try to find their place. The wasteland is alive.

## What it stands for

The wasteland is our own uncertain times. It has checks and balances and no good or bad guys.

- The fallen ship and the other wonders are technology and forces we do not understand and cannot control.
- The Old World is the shoulders of giants we stand on. People use what it left without knowing how it was made.
- The basin is a small piece of a universe too large for any one person to grasp.

## The player's question

The player arrives in this world with no meaning given to their journey. The game asks what they will do about that, and never answers for them.

They can take a role in society and help others. They can become a killer. They can drive into the wasteland until they starve, refusing to join the play. Every one of these paths is playable, and the game judges none of them.

## How it shows

- Never state the theme. No line, item or screen explains what the game is about.
- Show life in small things. A farm town of terraced fields grows inside an impact crater. A radio host reads crop reports with no crops left. Someone hangs a banner or parks to watch the sunset.
- Every mystery has a true answer, written plainly in the lore docs. The game shows it only as evidence: a wreck's position, a log entry, a part that should not exist, two people who remember it differently. No single piece states the conclusion. A careful player can put the pieces together and make a good guess, but the last step is always theirs.
- Show the Old World as giants' shoulders. Its canals still carry water, and nobody knows who dug them.
- No faction is right. Each one is a way of coping, and each way costs something.

## Ways of coping

Every character and faction copes with the world in one of five ways. Use these ways to write them.

- Embrace. They accept the world as it is and find meaning or humor in it. The cost is that they rarely try to change anything. J.J. embraces it.
- Escape. They keep moving, or wait for a way out. The cost is that they never build anything. Roamers escape it.
- Fear. They wall themselves in and keep to what they know. The cost is that their life shrinks to fit the walls.
- Control. They try to own the unknown and put order on it. The cost is that they crush whatever does not fit the order. The Nose Army controls.
- Oppose. They refuse the order others build. The cost is that they live by taking from those who build. Raiders oppose.

Rules for using them:

- Give each character one main way. A faction leans one way, but its people vary.
- Show the way through actions and speech. Never name it in the game.
- A character can change their way over time, and that change is a story.
