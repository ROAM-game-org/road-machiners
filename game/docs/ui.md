# UI design system

The HTML UI and the in-world overlays share one look: warm ink on dark metal, Barlow Semi Condensed for words and IBM Plex Mono for numbers. The 3D world has its own look in [Visual style](art/visual-style.md), and neither borrows from the other.

A design system has three layers. Principles say what the UI is for and how to judge it. Patterns say how a kind of problem is solved every time, like a shop row, a disabled button or an alert. Tokens and shared pieces say what it is built from. A person or an agent who builds a new screen follows all three, so it makes the same choices as the screens before it.

Every change a player sees in the HTML UI or an overlay goes through this document: a new screen, panel, tooltip, notice, button, label or line of UI text. Start with section 13, "How to build a screen". A change that breaks a principle names it and says why. A new or changed principle needs the user's approval. A new pattern or shared piece is added here in the same change, so the next screen can reuse it.

Open <http://localhost:5173/ui.html> with `npm run dev` to see every token and shared piece drawn with the real CSS.

## 1. What the UI is for

The source is DESIGN.md: the game shows what the player needs to decide and hides what they should discover. Every rule below comes from that sentence or from another line of DESIGN.md.

The UI serves decisions, not data. A screen exists because the player makes a choice there. A fact is on screen because that choice needs it.

## 2. Principles

Each principle has a reason from the game and a test you can run on a screenshot.

P1. Start from the decision. Before a screen gets a layout, write the question the player answers there and the facts that answer it. Test: every element on the screen answers that question or helps act on it.

P2. Key facts are never one step away. A fact the player needs to choose now is visible without a hover, a click or a scroll. Test: cover every tooltip and closed panel, and the choice can still be made.

P3. The world is the main screen. Most choices happen with the cursor on the map: where to drive, who that is, whether to fight. So the card under the cursor is the most important panel in the game, and the HUD stays at the edges. Test: the center of the screen is free during planning.

P4. State on the HUD, events in the log. A state stays visible while it is true. An event is told once, in order. Test: nothing the player must track lives only in the log, and the log holds no mechanical state.

P5. Every number can explain itself. DESIGN.md says chances are shown with their causes. The cause lives on the number: hovering the number opens it in the shared tooltip, and a dotted underline marks a number that has one. The cause is reference, so it is not repeated in the glance view. The same holds for a price the player cannot pay, a button that does not work and a stat that will change. Test: hovering any chance shows its cause, and no disabled control lacks a reason.

P6. Show what the truck knows, no more. Information is a resource. The UI shows a fact with the certainty the player has. A vague contact looks vague and an unknown driver looks unknown. A fact a perk reveals is absent without the perk. It is not a locked slot, because DESIGN.md says no hand-holding and a slot is a hint. Test: no screen shows a value the player's truck could not know.

P7. Show it, don't say it. A drawing, an icon or a position carries meaning on its own. A word never repeats it, and a detail never repeats its row. Test: delete each label in turn and ask whether anything was lost.

P8. One thing, one look. The same fact kind prints the same way on every screen. The same state looks the same on every control. One icon means one concept. Test: money, condition and selection look identical in the shop, the inventory and the HUD. One deliberate exception: on both truck drawings, the condition panel and the truck card, a healthy part keeps its own color and only damage gets a tier color. The drawings show no green for a healthy part, because a green wash hides the look of a rendered truck and only damage is news.

P9. Planned and committed look different. Planning is free and a turn is a commitment. The player must always see which marks are still a plan. Test: a screenshot taken mid-plan and one taken mid-turn are easy to tell apart.

P10. Two voices, never mixed. Radio talk, dialogue and story lines are in character and never talk about game systems such as the turn, tiers or quests. The words keep their everyday meaning, like "the road turns". Labels, buttons, tooltips and tips are game chrome and may. Test: every text channel has one voice.

P11. Restraint. Color, size and weight carry meaning, so each is used for one job. One emphasis at a time: a word is bright or bold, never both. Decoration never uses a meaning color. A fact is said in color once: a hostile tag and a red relation line are two reds for one fact. Each surface has its own small set of inks, listed in 4.6, and selection on a screen is inverse print, so the player's choice never rests on hue. Emphasis follows the value, not the slot: values of one kind share one size, a high value is brighter and a zero is faint. A panel uses at most four text roles: title, body, caption and value. Test: on a grayscale screenshot the hierarchy and the selection still read, and in color every colored thing means something.

P12. Space follows structure. A fact gets room in proportion to its importance. Related things sit tight, and groups are separated by space, not by lines or headings. A label that a unit, an icon or a position already makes clear is dropped, and a phrase that every row shares is said once. A panel that cannot scroll grows sideways before it grows down. A fact sits next to the thing it describes. Groups are split by space. Inside one list, the faint `.row` hairline between rows is fine. Test: no line splits one group from another, and no empty block is larger than the facts beside it.

P13. Everything sits on a grid. A panel has a head row and one or two columns below it. A working screen has three (see section 13). A column that holds a drawing has a fixed width. The column edges never depend on content, so the same panel has the same edges for every truck, part or place. Every left edge sits on a column line, and a heading is never centered over its column. Every number and every tag ends on the right edge, or the cost of an action sits right after its button. Side by side columns start on the same row. Vertical space has two steps only: tight within a group and wide between groups. Test: draw the column lines over a screenshot, and every element starts or ends on one.

P14. One kind of panel, one size. A panel's width, height and drawing box are set by its kind, never by its content. The size is set for the largest content it must hold, and smaller content leaves space at the end of a group, not between groups. A working screen may shrink its width to its columns, and its outline then follows the truck, never the selection. A control follows the content it acts on, directly after it. It never sits at the far end of a tall column, and it never sits between two facts that belong together. Test: two instances of the same panel with different content have the same outline, and an action's button is in view next to the thing it changes.

P15. Separate lists that mean different things. Two lists the player must not confuse each sit in their own well. The well edge and the value ink carry whose list it is, such as a danger edge for the guns aimed at the player. Test: at a glance, without reading the heads, the player can tell which list is which.

P16. A tooltip is a small panel. It follows every rule above: text roles, grid, one name per concept and value-driven emphasis. It opens on the thing it explains, so it never repeats that value or restates what the panel around it already says. Its body is label and value rows, with labels in the UI face and values right aligned in mono. A breakdown speaks in the unit of the number it explains. It starts with Base and then adds one cause per row, so the rows add up to the number on screen. An internal unit, such as degrees of scatter, never reaches the player. The sim gives the steps, as `chanceSteps()` does for hit chances, and the UI only prints them. Base is muted ink, a step that lowers the number is in danger ink and a step that raises it is in good ink. A tooltip is as wide as its content, and it opens over the part of its panel that is not needed while it is open, never over a value the player is comparing. Words match the panel it opens from, so a cause called far on the card is never called range in the tooltip. Raw engine terms and debug strings never appear. Test: every number in the tooltip is in a unit the player already sees elsewhere, and the tooltip uses the same word as the card for each cause.

P17. Never narrate the screen. Text is allowed only when it adds a fact the screen does not already show. A word never restates a number, whose number it is, or which way it points, when position, color, grouping or the element the cursor is on already says it. A heading never names what its content plainly is. This is DESIGN.md's no hand-holding, applied to every label. Test: delete the text. If the player can still answer the same question from the screen, the text was hand-holding and stays deleted.

P18. The world is the most precious space. Every pixel a panel covers hides the desert, the trucks and the road. A panel takes the smallest area its content needs. It sits at the screen edge, never in the middle of play, and it opens only while it is needed. Transient overlays such as tooltips fit their content and never cover a value the player is comparing. A wider or taller panel needs a reason in the decision it serves. Test: in a field screenshot with every always-on panel shown, the center of the screen is clear, and no panel has an empty block wider than its content column.

P19. Controls are physical things. Every control on every screen looks like hardware in a cab: toggle switches, knobs, keys and LCD displays like an old Casio. A setting that stays on is a key that stays lit, a level is a knob, and a readout of the truck is a display. A one-time action of the window, like Back or End turn, is a key. What the game changes, such as values, lists and prose, shows on a screen set into the metal, and an action on one line of a list is a button on that screen, by the grammar of 4.6. The start menu, the shops and the menus use the same keys, so the whole game reads as one machine. A new control picks the nearest existing piece in `drawings.css` or the kit pieces of 4.5 before it gets a new look. Test: every control on every screen looks like something you could touch in a cab.

P20. A control acts on the thing named on its row. Whatever shares a row with a button reads as its object, so Close beside a part's name reads as closing the part. Window controls share a row only with the window title. Test: read each row left to right as a sentence, and the button's object is on that row.

P21. One subject per column. Each column of a screen answers one question about one thing, under one name. The top row holds only the screen's title and its way out. Test: name each column in one word. A fact that belongs to another column's word moves there.

P22. One row form. A fact on a card or a stat list prints as icon, label, then value at the right edge, with any bar under it. A status with no number gets the same form. Test: cover the values, and every label starts on one line.

P23. A bar must read at full length. The track of a meter contrasts with the surface it sits on, so an empty bar shows how long it would be. Test: on a grayscale screenshot, a bar at 10% still shows its whole length.

P24. An effect with numbers prints as stat rows. A perk or ability gets its name and one row per number, with the sentence in the tooltip. A sentence is for what no number can say. Test: the player can compare two abilities by reading down the value edge.

P25. A working screen fits the window. On desktop it never scrolls: every fact and control of every state is in view on the smallest supported window, on the largest truck. It reflows before it shrinks, and it shrinks before it hides. Fitting wins over the click minimum of section 6. Test: open every pick on the largest truck at 1280 by 720, and nothing scrolls or clips.

P26. One title per screen. The screen title is `--text-heading`. A subject name, like the truck's or a part's, is one level below it, at `--text-body` bold. Two names of one size read as two screens. Test: on a grayscale screenshot, the eye lands on the title first and on the subjects next.

## 3. Information tiers

Every fact on a screen belongs to one tier for the decision that screen serves. The tier decides where the fact goes, how big it is and when it shows. The same fact can be key on one screen and supporting on another. Price is key in the shop and absent on the map.

| Tier | What it is | When it shows | How it looks |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Key | Needed to choose now | Always, at every density | Value role, full ink, first after the name |
| Supporting | Helps compare or confirm | In the same view, at row and card density | Body or caption role, muted ink, after the key facts |
| Reference | Explains a rule, a cause in depth or the lore | On hover, on open or in help | Tooltip, open detail or the help panel |
| Ambient | Mood with no decision | Any time | The radio, flavor text, the world itself |

- A key fact never lives only in a tooltip, only in color or only in the log.
- A screen shows at most a few key facts. When everything looks key, nothing is. If a screen has more, split the decision or move facts down a tier.
- Supporting facts sit after the key facts in reading order and are visibly quieter.
- Reference text is never needed to act. If the player has to read a tooltip to choose, that fact is key and moves up.
- Ambient elements never carry a fact the player must act on.
- Moving down a tier is how a screen gets simpler. Deleting a fact is better still.

## 4. Where things go

### 4.1 Placement rules

- A thing in the world is labeled in the world. A place is the exception. Its walls, gate and pads show it, and its name lives on the map, the minimap and the town page, never as a plate over it in the world.
- The state of my own truck lives in the HUD.
- A comparison between my truck and another lives on the cursor card.
- An explanation sits next to the number it explains, inside the same block.
- An action sits at the end of the line it changes. A Buy button is inside its row. The turn commits at the bottom right.
- A choice the game waits for opens a notice at the top center and stops nothing else on screen.
- A task that takes the whole attention, like a shop or a save, opens a modal in the center.
- A confirm of the player's own command that throws away progress opens the confirm dialog in the center over every screen, and nothing else takes a key until it is answered. New game, Restart run, End run and Load save ask this way, through `askConfirm()` in `src/ui/confirm-dialog.ts`, and no code opens a browser `alert`, `confirm` or `prompt`, since a native box leaves fullscreen. This bends the choice level of 7, a notice, because a notice waits on the game and stops nothing, while a confirm guards a command the player just gave. New game asks only when an Autosave or Day start save exists, since only those are deleted. Escape and Cancel are one path, and Escape closes only the dialog.
- The quest page and the `.ink-page` take the layer above the instruments and the log, and those hide while one is open, so nothing of the HUD lies over a page. The call notice still shows over it, since a call waits for an answer.
- A look screen over a scene: while a talk window or page scene is on view, I, C and J open the inventory, character screen and journal over it in a look-only state. The screen lifts to `--z-over-scene`, with its own scrim, and its world controls are off with one reason, "Not during a scene". Escape closes it and the scene stays at the same paragraph. The scene lets the keys through while a Menu panel is open.
- The game menu may hold a rare world action that is a way out, not a truck control, like Get unstuck. It is a flat menu button, and when the sim would refuse it the entry is off with that reason in the shared tooltip, read from `unstuckBlock()`.
- The Menu button stays on view above every modal (`--z-feedback`, above `--z-modal`, `--z-log-open` and `--z-over-scene`), and its save, load, options, help and report panels open over the screen. Every center screen starts at `--modal-top`, just below the system row, so the Menu never covers its Leave button or heading, and a later modal gets the rule for free. While a Menu panel is open it takes every key: the town page's digits, the inventory's rotate, the radio call's digits and C, M, J and I do nothing, and Escape closes only the panel, so the player is back on the same screen and page. With no screen, pin or utility aim left for Escape to close, Escape opens the Menu list, as a click on the button does. `GameMenu.isOpen()` is the one query, read through `UiHost.menuOpen()`.
- Reporting a problem belongs to the page, not to one screen. The Menu holds "Report a bug or request a feature" on every layout, so it works under a scene the player cannot close. Over every full-page surface (the boot screen, the start menu, the save rescue, the defeat screen, the crash screen and its notices) the report desk shows the same Feedback corner in the top left, above the surface, and opens the same form above it. `src/ui/feedback-form.ts` owns the form, the send and the corner, and the desk is `#report`. Its corner shows only while one of those surfaces is on the page, so it never doubles the HUD corner. A new full-page surface joins the one `:has()` rule in `styles/report-desk.css`.
- A modal that is the only thing the player can do at that place keeps no Leave button, and its one way out is its action button. The outpost screen behind shut gates is the case: Drive on closes it, and neither Escape nor the game's close-all does. The Menu button stays over it, as over every modal, so saving, options, help and the report form stay reachable.

### 4.2 Zones

The screen has fixed zones. Each zone has one job, and an element never moves between zones. A new element goes in the zone whose job it shares. If none fits, the element is probably not always-on and belongs in a panel the player opens.

| Zone | Job | Lives there |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Top left | Stats, then the minimap | Money, supplies and health in one tray, then the minimap |
| Top center | Transient and waiting | Action bar at the edge, or the place's status when no action is offered, toast below it, then notices and tips |
| Top right | System keys, then the cursor card | Map, Journal, Car, Driver and Menu in one tray, the hover card below it |
| Right side | Ambient and waiting on | Contracts and wanted, above the radio |
| Bottom right | Events and commit | The radio deck: the radio talk screen, the event log, the clock, the speed knob and the End turn key, one piece |
| Bottom left | My truck | Truck diagram above the instrument panel and the weapons panel |
| Center | Task | Modals, dialogue, save and new game |

Two zones may never cover each other. A new element that needs room takes it from its own zone.

The boot screen draws the sign of the six words from [The end of the Old World](lore/old-world-end.md) above the game title. `src/render/sign.ts` owns its shapes, and `index.html` holds a copy that a test keeps equal. The sign builds up with the load: nothing before a fifth of it, then Spiral, Race, Exodus and Chaos at each next fifth. Each stage fades in and holds for half a second before the next one, so a fast load still shows every stage. The whole sign holds for a second before the boot screen fades out. With reduced motion, the boot screen leaves at once. The sign carries no text and is hidden from screen readers, since the progress bar already says how far the load is.

Before boot builds a world, the start menu fills the screen alone, with no zones. It shows the game title in the boot screen's title style over one column of large buttons: Continue, Load, Import save, New game and Options. Import save opens a file picker. A good file starts the game from the slot it landed in, and a refused one shows its reason in danger ink under the buttons. The Save panel holds the same Import save button in its row beside Export save and Export run log, with the refusal under the row. A caption under Continue names the save it resumes with the Load row's pieces: slot, mode and game clock, or "Old or damaged save" in danger ink, with the real date in its tooltip. Continue and Load are off with no save, and focus starts on the first button that is on. Load opens the save list over the menu, and a pick there loads at once with no confirm. The Save panel's slot rows show the same slot and mode, and a click on a slot that holds the other mode asks first, naming the slot and both modes. New game opens the New game screen over it.
### 4.3 The always-on budget

The always-on HUD answers a short list of questions without a click: can I keep going, is my truck okay, what is around me, what will this turn do, what just happened and what am I waiting on. A new always-on element names the question it answers. If it answers none, it goes in a panel.

### 4.4 Phones

A phone plays only in landscape. It gets the desktop layout of 4.2 at a smaller UI scale, chosen so the UI layer is at least 1024 by 600 (see "The UI layer" below). A portrait phone shows a prompt to turn the phone sideways instead of the game.

### 4.5 The HUD skin

The in-game HUD is one set of 3D hardware, baked to pictures. Baked art carries the material, and live DOM carries every word, number and state, so no text is baked into art and Russian and long text work. Every HUD picture comes from `npm run hud-bake`. [HUD bake](art/hud-bake.md) says how to add or change a part.

- Baked parts. The stats tray, the system keys, the instrument panel, the targeting computer, the radio, the rail and the truck card are baked parts. Each has a housing picture, sprite frames for its moving pieces and named zones in `src/ui/dash/baked/<part>.<view>.json`. `BakedPart` in `src/ui/dash/baked.ts` shows the housing and puts a live node on a zone with a matrix3d warp, so text lies on the tilted face it belongs to. A key's label or icon follows the key down on press, `BakedPart.follow()`.
- One view. Every part is modeled in three.js and seen by one camera from a vanishing point above the middle of a 1280 by 720 screen, so top and side faces, recesses and shadows are real 3D. A layer narrower than `--hud-bake-desktop-aspect` uses the flat view, baked with each part seen straight on, since the desktop angles look wrong once parts move.
- Print color. Print on an olive panel, such as the menu, a panel title or the Auto fire label, is dark coal with a light lower edge, `--hud-print-coal`. Print on the radio and on key caps is cream, `--hud-print`. Light print never sits on a light panel.
- Bottom row. The instrument panel, the targeting computer and the radio always share the bottom edge in one row, and the speedometer never lifts off its panel. When the row does not fit, the gaps between the three close first, then all HUD parts shrink together through `--hud-zoom` until the row fits the layer width and the radio and top tray fit its height. `dashFit()` in `src/ui/dash/panels.ts` computes both.
- Center tip. When the camera leaves the truck, the tip "[F] Center on truck" shows above the bottom-left instruments, on the tooltip frame and screen. It is not a key. A click or tap on it recenters, so touch can use it.
- Real depth. Keys sit sunk into cut holes, with no painted rings. Panels are thin bevelled slabs. Screens sit behind a brass lip. Lamps are flat domes lying on their panel. Toggles are long brass fluted stems in a small nickel ring, so the lever reads up or down at a glance.
- Leather. Only the slate piece is leather. The rail along the bottom edge is the same olive painted metal as the devices, with the faint ink hatch and no stitches.
- Salvaged devices. The instrument panel, the two top trays, the targeting computer and the radio are painted metal in the olive housing. The radio's lower strip is gray and is held to the body by one bent steel bracket. The metal carries the faint ink hatch of the window frame, never grain, scratches or blotches. The instrument panel and the two trays carry small hex bolt heads in their corners. The instrument panel has no visor: its top edge is plain olive bevel, so it reads as one panel.
- Device shadows. The targeting computer and the radio each cast one flat shadow back and to the right onto the rail top, `placeDeviceCasts()` in `src/ui/dash/skin.ts`.
- Kit pieces. A control type has one baked picture, used everywhere, from the `pieces` bake: one key cap in plain, lit and red, each up and pressed, one close key, one lamp, one tape label, one toggle in two positions (the drive toggle's model, leaning up on and down off, drawn in a box of its own aspect), the olive `frame`, the `screen` recess and the `input` well. A new control picks a piece before it gets a new look.
- Panels. Every box that is not a baked part is a panel built from the panel kit in `src/ui/dash/skin.ts`: `panelHead()`, `screen()`, `screenLine()`, `controls()`, `textKey()` and `closeKey()`. Its housing is the baked olive frame, drawn as a 9-slice border image, so a panel can take any size. The 9-slice cuts the frame just past its bevel, `--hud-frame-slice`, and draws that edge at `--hud-frame-bevel`, close to its baked size. A wider cut squeezes the bevel into a thin double line at some UI sizes. Windows such as the journal and the inventory are built the same way, by the grammar of 4.6.
- Panel layout. The title sits at the top left, left aligned, in engraved print. A panel that closes has one close key, the same small key with the X icon, at the right of the title row. Text that changes sits on a screen. One control is centered, and two or more sit at the left and right edges. A destructive key is red, so every confirm dialog's yes key is red.
- Panel sizes. A HUD panel takes its width from six steps, each about 1.4 times the last, anchored on the cursor card: `--hud-panel-xs` 140px, `--hud-panel-s` 200px, `--hud-panel-m` 282px, `--hud-panel-l` 400px, `--hud-panel-xl` 560px and `--hud-panel-xxl` 800px. Medium narrows to 246px on a compact layer and to 200px on a narrow one. The knocked-out mark is extra small. The cursor card, contracts, wanted, the menu list, stranded and rescue are medium. The confirm dialog and the death box are large. The tip and the dialogue are extra large. The toast and the tooltips are as wide as their text, the toast at most large and a tooltip at most medium. A panel keeps its size across its states, so stranded holds one box with the beacon switch and with End run. A new panel picks a step and never sets its own width.
- Screens. A screen is the baked screen lip, a dark edge and a brass rim with rounded corners like the targeting computer's window, drawn as a 9-slice with `--hud-screen-slice` and `--hud-screen-edge`. Every screen, in the HUD and in the windows, takes the same glass behind its content: the scanlines, vignette and sheen of `--hud-screen-glass` and the inner shade of `--hud-screen-shade`. Anything drawn on the glass, such as a screen button, a tag, a selection, a gauge segment or a diagram cell, glows like the print. A map is a picture on a screen. The slice holds only the lip, so the lip shows at full width on every screen, like the targeting computer. A screen draws no CSS shadow or ring of its own, since the baked lip is its only edge. Its inks, fonts and states are in 4.6. A truck picture on a screen carries the glass's scan rows faintly through `.crt-picture`, so it reads as drawn on the screen. The rows are a mask on the picture's own pixels, so the empty glass around it is unchanged. Gun marks, the repair meter and tips over the picture stay clean.
- Windows. Every `.panel` and `.dialog` that is not a HUD kit panel is a window, built by the grammar of 4.6: olive metal from `--hud-frame-art`, one screen per block of content, keys on the metal. The crash screen keeps a plain look with its own `--crash-*` tokens, since it reports files that did not arrive and must draw without pictures.
- Keys. Every button on every screen is a physical key, never a box with text. A key's look is its art, so a screen never draws a key's state with `background`, `border` or `border-color`, since the cap paints over them. A state on a key sets `--key-art` or `--key-print` instead: `.on`, `.selected`, `aria-pressed` and `aria-checked` take the lit cap pressed down, so a toggle that is on reads as latched, and `:active` takes the pressed cap. A screen that sets a key's padding subtracts `var(--key-edge)` from each side, so the key keeps the size of its box. A plain `button` outside the drawn hardware is the baked keycap as a 9-slice, with its label in the engraved face and `--hud-print` ink. A disabled key keeps its cap and dims its print to `--hud-print-dim`, and `.btn-danger` takes the red cap, pressed or not. An action on one line of a list is a screen button, not a key, by 4.6. On the HUD `button.hud-key` is the key cap with an engraved label, an icon or both. A key with no icon prints its label on the cap. The key grows to fit its label and prints it on one line. Keys in one row share one height. A lit or danger key with text uses the red cap. A list of actions, such as the menu, is a column of icon keys with printed labels beside them. A pressed key shows its baked pressed frame. Every button also has a sound, named in `--press-sound` in the rule that draws its look (`components.css`, "Press sounds"): `key`, `screen`, `switch`, `latch`, `latch-lit`, `knob` or `none`. `src/ui/control-sound.ts` reads it on a press and throws on any other value. A new control sets it where it sets its look, and a control that does not move on a press sets `none`.
- Targeting computer. The screen always shows six gun cards, three rows of two, with a static amber CRT look from the `--hud-crt-*` tokens. A card shows the gun number, its picture, its name, one cell per round and one status: the aimed part and the hit chance, no target, reloading, error or got 'em. The number and the name are always dim. A click on a card selects that gun alone, All selects every gun, and a second press clears the selection. Reload and Hold act on the selected guns and say No gun selected when none is. The spec is [targeting-computer.md](concepts/targeting-computer.md). Its glass lies over all its content at full `--hud-crt-scan` strength, the strong case of the rule a truck picture follows.
- Three text sizes: `--hud-text-s`, `--hud-text-m` and `--hud-text-l`, plus `--hud-lcd-size` for LCD digits. Labels on hardware use the engraved face `--font-engraved`. Latin letters come from Routed Gothic Narrow, and Cyrillic letters from OpenGost Type A, the Soviet drafting lettering of GOST 2.304. OpenGost is thinner, so Russian labels draw with a heavier stroke, `--engraved-stroke`. LCD digits use `--font-lcd-digits` (TC Calculator Digits) over unlit segments in `--hud-lcd-ghost`.
- Displays share one screen color, `--hud-screen`, and one amber, `--hud-amber`, with a soft glow.
- No effect without a job. There is no hover look, no fade and no gray-out on the HUD. A key moves only by its baked press, and a switch changes between two baked positions. Other moving parts are the needles, the knob, the wheel and the heat lamp, which blinks red while the engine runs hot, from 75% heat up, and holds steady under reduced motion. During a turn the speed needle follows the truck's real speed from the playback frames.
- Tooltips. A hover tooltip is drawn by the game above all UI, never by the browser (`installHoverTips()` in `src/ui/touch-tip.ts`). A key comes first in brackets, then the name, and more facts go on further lines: `[Space] End turn` then `Hold to fast-forward`. The speed tooltip lists what adds speed in green and what takes it away in red.


### 4.6 Hardware grammar

Every screen in the game is a salvaged device bolted into the cab: olive painted metal, with screens set into it behind brass lips, keys and switches mounted on it, and tape stuck on it where a driver wrote a name. The radio and the targeting computer are the model. This section decides which surface each kind of content sits on, which ink and font it uses, and how each hard case is solved. A screen that follows it reads as one more device of the same truck.

The test for every choice is the player's suspension of disbelief. A piece that could not exist as hardware in the cab is wrong, even when it reads well. A screen does not grow, a key does not change its print, and a gauge is a row of lamps, because real devices work that way.

The one rule: what the game changes lives on a screen, and what the player presses lives on the metal.

#### Content to surface

- Window body. The olive `frame` piece, 9-sliced over the whole window. Olive metal shows in the title row, in the gutters between screens and in the key strip. The piece has a thin ink edge and a mitred bevel, light on the top and left and dark on the bottom and right, with concentric round corners. Its face carries an even field of short diagonal ink hatch strokes that tiles with no seam, in the drawn style of the game and never grain or scratches, and the 9-slice repeats the face with `round` instead of stretching it, so the texture keeps its size on any window. Every panel's padding clears the bevel, so no screen touches the rim. A notice like the death box or a confirm has one fixed size per kind, wide enough for its text in every language.
- Fixed words of a window, such as its title or the label of a group of keys. Engraved print on metal, `--font-engraved`, uppercase, ink `--hud-print-coal` with `--hud-print-coal-edge`. Only short fixed words, never data.
- A name from the game used as a heading, such as the truck, a person or a shop. Tape on metal: the `tape` piece as a 9-slice, so its torn ends keep their size and its paper fibres repeat along the strip instead of stretching. The print is `--font-hud` bold in `--hud-print-ink`. A driver wrote it, so tape is the one place a game value sits off a screen. Hard case, the other truck in the inventory: its driver's name is tape stuck on the top lip of that truck's own screen, never a title inside the glass and never in the budget well. The downed state (Gave up or Knocked out) is the first line of the glass, and the drawing is fitted so no part covers the tape or that line or leaves the glass, since a long gun reaches past the grid. `scripts/layout-check.mjs` checks it in the `look-over` and `downed` states.
- Anything the game changes: values, lists, prose, prices, descriptions, quest text, the inventory drawing. A screen: the baked `screen` piece, 9-sliced, set into the metal. A window has one screen per block of content, never one screen for the whole window.
- A fixed action of the window, such as Close, Back, Rotate, Strip, Fill or End run. A key on the metal.
- A persistent choice, such as a tab, a filter, a mode or a setting that stays. A key that stays lit while it is on, or a rocker for on and off. A level is a knob: the baked `knob` cap with its turning `knobPointer`, built by `knobControl()` in `src/ui/sound.ts`, with its name engraved under it. Where the exact value matters, as in the world settings, it prints on a readout beside the knob, and the default is an engraved mark at the knob's edge. A slider is never used, since a fader cannot sit on glass.
- An action that belongs to one line of a list, such as Buy, Sell or Accept on a row that scrolls. A screen button: an outlined word on the screen beside its line. A key cannot sit on glass or follow a scrolling row.
- A choice among options the game wrote, such as quest choices or save slots. Screen lines, each with its number, on the screen.
- A status that is on or off, such as a warning. A lamp on the metal. A lamp for a state that damages the truck, or is about to, blinks red, hard on and hard off, the one blinking lamp pattern, so a later warning reuses it.
- A text field or a picker. The baked `input` well on the metal.
- A screen that scrolls. A rocker wheel on the metal at the screen's right edge, centred on its height, from the radio's baked wheel, through `mountScrollWheels()` in `src/ui/scroll-wheel.ts`. The screen shows no scrollbar. The wheel turns with the scroll, a drag or the mouse wheel over it scrolls the screen, a click pages, and it shows only while the screen can scroll. A screen with no gutter beside it keeps room for the wheel.
- A picture, such as an item icon, the art band or the truck drawing. On a screen, or the art band across the top of the window, in its own colors.
- A small value, such as money, load, a price or a cost. It prints on the nearest screen that already holds related content, never on a screen of its own. The chips over the art band share one screen, a fuel or supplies row puts its level and gauge on one screen and prints the prices on its keys, a repair row has no screen, and the cost of Rotate or Strip prints on the details screen.
- A level or an amount, such as fuel, load, condition, XP or work done. A gauge on a screen: a row of segments, like the ammo count on the targeting computer.
- A short message, such as a tip or a passing message. A small HUD panel: the olive frame around one screen, one fixed width per kind. A panel is never drawn as paper or as any other flat shape.

#### Inks per surface

- Metal: `--hud-print-coal` only. No amber, no cream, no accent, no good or bad color on metal. Coal on olive measures 3.8 to 1, so metal print is at least `--text-lead` and short.
- Tape: `--hud-print-ink` only.
- Screen: `--hud-amber` for main text, `--hud-amber-soft` for secondary text, `--hud-amber-faint` for an option that is off or done, `--hud-lamp-green` for good and `--hud-hot` for bad. Nothing else. No cream, no coal, no white, no gray.
- Plain cap: `--hud-print`, and `--hud-print-dim` when the key is disabled.
- Lit cap and red cap: `--hud-print`. A key's print never changes color with its state. Only the cap changes.
- A color that carries game information keeps its own colors on a screen. The cell kinds of the chassis map, which tell the player where guns, deck and engine go, use the `--map-*` colors, never shades of amber.

#### Screens are fixed

- A screen is a fixed part of its device. Its size and its place come from the window's grid, never from its content. The content changes and the screen stays.
- A screen is never one line high. A single fixed sentence of the window, such as a town's flavor line, a confirm question or a toast, prints on the metal under the title in `--hud-print-coal` and the engraved font. A list screen keeps room for at least two rows even when it holds one. Number readouts, such as the money chips, and inputs are instruments and keep one line.
- Every screen keeps one padding from its brass rim to its content, `--hud-screen-pad`. A screen file never sets its own. `scripts/hardware-check.mjs` reports content closer than 12px to the rim, and a screen under two body lines high whose text sits on one row.
- A window keeps the same screens on every tab and in every state. A screen with nothing to show stays in place and prints one faint idle line, such as NO PART PICKED. The one exception is the other truck in the inventory: its screen exists only while a truck is shown, since it is a second subject that replaces the budget well's room and is not a state of the first.
- Content longer than its screen scrolls inside it with the rocker wheel. The screen never grows to fit.
- A list with a fixed most, such as the three held contracts, gets a screen sized for the most, and its empty slots print faint.
- The window itself has a fixed size per kind. A talk window does not grow with the number of replies, and a shop does not change size between tabs.
- These rules and the grid hold on a desktop layer. On a narrow layer and on a tight phone layer the columns stack and the window scrolls, so only the rules for keys, gauges and inks apply there. `npm run layout` checks them in `scripts/hardware-check.mjs`.

#### The grid

- The screens of one window sit on one grid of columns and rows. Screens side by side share their top and bottom edges. Screens stacked in one column share their left and right edges, and a screen across several columns starts and ends on column edges.
- The gutter between two screens is one size per window: `--space-9` in a window and `--hud-panel-gap` in a HUD panel.
- Keys sit in strips on the metal along the grid: the title row, a foot row under the screens or a key column beside them. A strip starts on a column edge.
- The rows of a list on a screen use one column grid. Every row puts each field in the same column, so names, values and screen buttons line up down the list, and the screen buttons of one column have one width.

#### Keys carry no state

- A key's print and face never change with the game. Counts, levels, XP, progress and pictures of the player's things are game state, and they go on a screen beside the key.
- One exception: a price on a key is allowed, as in Fill and a coin with 29. A key may also print the step it buys, as in Buy 5 L and a coin with 1. The price part sits in the `.price` element of the key, and `scripts/hardware-check.mjs` ignores it. Any other digit on a key is a fault.
- The only change a key shows is its cap: lit while it is on, pressed while held, dim print while it cannot be used, and red when it destroys.
- A key prints only its action. Its keyboard shortcut shows in its tooltip, as in Esc and Back, and never on the cap. A control with a shortcut is named to `keyPress()` where its handler runs, so the key sounds like the click.

#### Gauges

- A gauge is a row of segments on a screen, like the ammo count on the targeting computer, built by `segmentGauge()` in `src/ui/cards.ts`. Full segments print in `--hud-amber`, empty segments in `--hud-lcd-ghost` like spent rounds, and a load or draw over capacity prints every segment in `--hud-hot`.
- A planned change shows in the same gauge. Segments a plan will fill print in `--hud-amber-faint`, like rounds being reloaded, and the share of one picked part prints lit while the rest of the load prints in `--hud-amber-soft`.
- A gauge has a fixed number of segments, so it keeps its length for every value. The exact value prints beside it in `--font-mono`.
- A flat filled bar is never used.

#### Fonts per role

- Engraved print on metal and on caps: `--font-engraved`, uppercase, on every key in every window. Latin is Routed Gothic Narrow and Cyrillic is OpenGost Type A with `--engraved-stroke`.
- Screen heading, the first line of a screen that names its content, like WOT RADIO or OFFERS: `--font-hud`, uppercase. Chakra Petch has no Cyrillic, so Russian draws in Fira Sans Condensed.
- Screen words: `--font-ui`. Barlow Semi Condensed in Latin, Fira Sans Condensed in Russian.
- Screen numbers, such as prices, weights, chances and counts: `--font-mono`, IBM Plex Mono in both languages.
- Tape: `--font-hud` bold. Russian draws in Fira Sans Condensed bold.
- LCD digits: `--font-lcd` and `--font-lcd-digits`, only on the HUD trays and clock.
- Sizes come from section 11. Screen words in a window are `--text-body`, dense data is `--text-dense`, and quest prose is `--text-lead`. A window title on metal is `--text-heading`.

#### States

- Selection on a screen is inverse: the line or the screen button fills with `--hud-amber` and its print turns `--hud-screen`. Selection never relies on hue, since amber ink and the accent are close.
- Hover on a screen line or a screen button fills it with `--hud-amber-dim`. A key has no hover look. It moves only by its baked press.
- A key that is on takes the lit cap pressed down, so a toggle shows that it is latched. A pressed key takes its pressed frame. A disabled key keeps its cap and dims its print. A destructive key takes the red cap.
- A choice already taken, such as a quest line the player has read, prints in `--hud-amber-faint`.
- A screen has no dividers. Space separates its lines, and a block that needs a frame of its own becomes a second screen. The outline of a screen button and of a tag are the only lines drawn on a screen.

#### Hard cases

- A place's status with no action, such as a picked clean wreck. The knocked-out mark's piece in the action bar's slot, never a disabled key.
- Window title. Engraved on metal, top left. The close key is at the right of the title row.
- A shop or town heading like Machiners' yard. Tape, since the name comes from the game. A fixed screen name like Inventory is engraved.
- The truck name over the inventory drawing. Tape stuck across the top lip of the drawing's screen, so the three column screens start level.
- Stats beside the drawing. One screen per column.
- Rotate and Strip under the part details. Keys in a key strip on metal, right against the details screen and starting on its left edge. The cost lines print on that screen at the edge that touches the strip, over the strip on the bench and under it in a town's truck column. They never get a screen of their own.
- Shop row Buy with its price. A screen button on the row's screen. The price is part of the button's label.
- Zoom of a picture on a screen, like the truck card's. The − and + screen buttons side by side in the screen's bottom right corner, on the glass, each square at `--btn-h-s` with its icon. This widens the screen button from a list row's action to the action of the screen's own content.
- Contract offers with Accept. One screen holds the list. Accept is a screen button on each row.
- Tabs such as Buy parts, Sell parts and Trucks, and the filter row. Keys on metal, the current one lit. A key prints only its fixed word or icon, never a count.
- Part filters of a shop. Icon keys in the shop band, as a second row under the tabs. Tabs without filters hold an empty row there, so the band keeps its height on every tab and the screens keep their place.
- The truck switch of a shop. Keys that print TRUCK and HAULER. The drawing of the truck is on the truck's screen, not on the key.
- Skills on the character screen. A key per skill prints only its name and icon. The skill's XP gauge, rank and price are on the screen beside the keys.
- Goods rows on a trade screen. One column grid down the list: icon, name, stock and held, then the Buy and Sell screen buttons, each column of buttons one width.
- Held contracts on the board. A screen sized for the three a truck can hold, beside the offers screen and as tall as it.
- A talk window. One fixed window per call: the line screen sized for three lines and the replies screen sized for six replies.
- A tip and a passing message. A small HUD panel: the olive frame, the engraved title and close key on the metal, the words on one screen.
- Chips over the art band, such as money and load. Small screens set into the band's lower edge.
- Prose such as a town or shop description. A screen under the title row.
- Quest page. The art in the left column, the tape and one screen of full column height in the middle with the prose and the numbered choices, the way out as a key on the metal under it, and the stats and facts on a second screen of full height at the right. The town's ink page keeps the art band on top.
- Confirm dialog. No title. The question on a screen, Cancel as a plain key and the yes as a red key, both on metal.
- Tooltip. A thin olive frame around one screen, like the center tip.
- A gun's number on its arc icon over the world. On the icon's own small screen, left of the gun picture, as on the gun card. A number never hangs off a chip's corner.
- Text field and picker. The input well on metal, with amber screen ink inside.
- The crash screen keeps its plain `--crash-*` look, and the boot screen keeps its `--boot-*` look, since both draw before the baked pictures load.

#### Useless if

A screen built with this grammar fails review if any of these hold:

- It is mostly one dark screen with a thin rim, the look of a slab.
- Any amber or cream text sits on metal.
- A screen sits inside a screen.
- Fixed labels and titles sit on a screen instead of the metal.
- Selection is shown by hue alone.
- Its layout moves against the screen before the HUD work.
- A material or a part looks painted in flat: a flat fill, a bare outline or a shape with no depth where the hardware would show a lip, a bevel, glass or a shadow. Every material comes from a baked piece, and every screen has the same glass.
- A screen changes its size or its place with its content, its tab or its state.
- Two screens side by side have different tops or bottoms, or two stacked screens of one column have different edges.
- A key prints a count, a price, a level, XP, progress or a picture of the player's things.
- A flat filled bar shows a level.
- The rows of a list do not line up in columns.

### 4.7 The UI layer

Options holds the device settings: language, UI size, the minimap, the damage numbers, the log view, the camera and sound. Sound is the four volume knobs, for music, wind, effects and interface, and the Sound key, drawn as the radio's hardware, since a level is a knob (P19) and the same piece looks the same everywhere (P8). The Sound key stays lit while sound is on, and pressing it mutes. It lives only here. The radio keeps only the next key. `SoundSettings` in `src/ui/sound.ts` owns the levels and the mute, and every `OptionsPanel` is built with it. The minimap is Off or On (see `.map-view`). Damage numbers is On or Off, On by default, and `src/ui/damage-numbers-setting.ts` keeps it in local storage, never in the save. Off stops the hit labels over trucks and nothing else. The panel hangs from the top inset rather than the centre, so it clears the weapons keys beneath it. The log is Brief or Verbose (see section 7), and `src/ui/log-setting.ts` keeps it in local storage, never in the save. The camera is Auto or Centered, and `src/ui/camera-setting.ts` keeps it in local storage, never in the save. V flips it, and an open row follows. UI size is 80%, 90%, 100%, 115% or 130%. It is kept in local storage, never in the save, and applies at once. `src/ui/ui-size.ts` owns it. At a size other than 100%, `#ui` and `#overlay` are laid out in a box of the window divided by the size and scaled up or down from the top left corner, so every pixel value, the drawn hardware and the map labels scale together. At 100% no transform is set. The boot screen, the crash screens and the report desk stay at 100%.

- The layer, not the window, is the screen. The layer must stay at least 640 by 360 (long side by short side), and at least 1024 by 600 on a touch device. A size that would make it smaller is off in Options with the reason "Too big for this screen", and the game applies the largest step that fits, at most the stored pick.
- `input-mode.ts` sets `slim` (480px wide or less), `narrow` (720px or less) and `compact` (1100px or less) on `#ui` from the layer size. CSS breakpoints are these classes, never a width media query. Orientation queries are fine.
- CSS never uses `vw`, `vh`, `dvh`, `svh` or `vmax`. It uses `--layer-w` and `--layer-h`, which are the window divided by `--ui-scale`. `--safe-*` divide the device inset by the same scale.
- `--touch-target` is `max(32px, 32px / --ui-scale)`, so a touch target is never under 32 real pixels.
- Code that sets `left`, `top`, `width` or `height` from a screen rect or a pointer converts through `uiSize()`: `layerRect()`, `layerPoint()`, `toLayer()` and `layerSize()`. Comparing a screen rect with a screen rect or a pointer needs no conversion. A new UI calculation never reads `window.innerWidth` or `innerHeight` for placement.

## 5. Entity anatomy

Parts, trucks, drivers, goods, contracts and places appear on many screens. Each has three densities with fixed slots in a fixed order: identity, then state, then key numbers, then cost, then action.

| Density | Use | Shows |
| --- | --- | --- |
| chip | Inside a sentence, a list of many, a slot | Identity only: icon and name |
| row | A list the player compares | Identity, key facts, cost, action |
| card | One thing the player studies | Everything key and supporting, reference on hover |

- A slot keeps its place at every density. A density drops slots from the end, never reorders them.
- A missing or unknown value keeps its slot empty or marked unknown. The layout never shifts.
- Numbers right align in fixed width columns in the mono face, so a list reads down a column.
- The open detail of a row adds facts. It never repeats one.

## 6. Interaction states

One grammar for every control and every selectable thing.

| State | Meaning | Look |
| --- | --- | --- |
| rest | Can be used | Surface and line tokens |
| hover | Under the cursor | A screen line or screen button fills with `--hud-amber-dim`. A key has no hover look. |
| focus | Keyboard focus | One accent outline on screen pieces and fields. A key shows no outline, since a key shows only its baked art. |
| selected | The player's current choice | Inverse print on a screen, the lit cap on a key |
| planned | A choice that is not committed yet | Dashed accent outline. A planned key takes the pressed cap |
| working | Timed work in progress | Accent progress meter |
| disabled | Cannot be used now | Dimmed, with its reason |
| spent | The row's own action is done | Dimmed row, past-tense tag in the control's slot, no hover |
| seen | A radio option or talk choice with nothing new behind it | Faint screen ink. Hover and focus still apply |
| unaffordable | Needs more money | Price in danger ink |
| locked | Needs progress, like a rank | Lock icon |

- A control uses only the states in this table.
- A key has no hover look, as 4.5 says, so its hover is only its tooltip. A clickable row or tile on a window keeps its hover wash.
- A row in a trade list keeps its slot until the player leaves the list. A row whose action removes it from the list stays as a spent row, drawn from what was on screen, so a second click lands on nothing and the next row never slides under the pointer. `RowSlots` in `src/ui/row-slots.ts` owns this. A refused action changes nothing.
- A screen that is only for looking, like the inventory over a scene or over a truck being looked over, gives every control that changes the world the disabled state with one reason read from one function (`lookReason()`), before any reason of its own. The look-over screen adds the reason Not during a look-over to that function and locks the player's own grid too; items can still be picked to read their card.
- While the driver is knocked out, every control that gives the truck an order is off with one reason, Knocked out, from `orderLock()` in `src/ui/weapons.ts`. It comes before the control's own reason, so it stays steady across the auto turns, and the control's key does nothing. Look controls (Driver, Map, Journal, Menu, the log and the auto turn button) stay on. The inventory is a look screen then, through `lookReason()`.
- A disabled control still takes hover and focus, so its reason can show. It uses `aria-disabled` and the shared tooltip, and the reason is one short fact like Need 12 M more.
- Unaffordable and locked look different, because the player fixes one by earning and the other by progressing. A locked key puts the lock icon before its word and gives the rank it needs as its reason.
- Touch has no hover, so it fills the gap. The first tap shows what hover would show: a tap on a truck only pins its card, and a tap on a point utility only sets its marker. A tap on the thing already shown does what a click does. A long press of 500ms on a control opens what hover opens, its title or its reason, and a tap on a disabled control opens its reason. A mouse click on a disabled control opens the same reason in the shared tooltip at the cursor, and the hover reason hides while it is open. The ground needs no preview tap, since a plan is free and the drawn path is its preview. In the inventory, a tap on an item selects it, a tap on the grid shows a placement preview there, Rotate on its card turns the preview, and a tap on the preview places the item. A long press on the ground is Shift-click. `#ui.touch` is the primary pointer being coarse, and it only decides what the screen shows: no keycaps in labels or titles (`keyLabel()` in `src/ui/input-mode.ts` adds one off touch), the Horn, Rotate and close buttons, and touch wording in tips and help. Under it the small button is 32px and a click target is at least `--touch-target`.
- A gamepad has no hover and no Shift, like touch, and it fills the gap the same way. `#ui.pad` is set while a pad drives and cleared by any real mouse, finger or key. The button press or stick tilt that starts pad mode only starts it, so a press never lands on a control the hidden reticle rests on. Leaving pad mode with RT held lets go of the turn key. When the stick lets go in the world, the reticle snaps to a truck, place or loot in reach, but to a control only when it is over that control, so ground next to a panel stays reachable. The pad's press and drag pointers are not real browser pointers, so `emulatePadCapture()` in `pad-dom.ts` stands in for pointer capture on them, and every screen that captures a pointer works under a held A. The left stick moves one reticle over every screen. It slows over a target and snaps to the nearest one in reach when the stick lets go: a control, a truck, the player's truck, a place or loot. In the world, A is a tap and holding A is the long press, so the touch rules above hold: A on a truck pins it, A again aims, and holding A on the ground sets a stop. LB and RB step through the trucks in sight, and RT is the turn key. On a screen, the D-pad jumps the reticle to the next control in that direction and scrolls a list at its end, LB and RB step the tabs, and B is Esc. A reticle resting on a control shows its tooltip, as hover does. Key caps show the pad button that sends the key (`padGlyphFor()` in `src/ui/pad/pad-model.ts`), and a key no button sends shows no cap. Holding Y opens the radial dash, a ring of the HUD switches and screens with no key of their own on the pad, and letting go runs the picked one.
- A click target is at least the small button height, 24px, except that a working screen's grid cell may go under it to fit the window (P25). The aim target of a part on the truck hover card follows this rule too, with `--touch-target` on a touch device, The card keeps one size, so the cells of a long rig like the semi may fall under it, which bends this rule. A part's hit area is its own drawn pixels, plus the body and deck pixels over its grid cells. Armor also takes the box it is drawn in, since plates sit outside the cells. A drawn part wins over the cells under it, so the part under the pointer is the part you see. The cell map is rendered once per truck build. Other drawn hardware is exempt and keeps the size of its drawing.

## 7. Feedback and alerts

Every event gets a level before it gets a channel. A level fires on its channels and on no others.

| Level | Meaning | Channels |
| --- | --- | --- |
| info | Worth a record | Log line |
| notable | Worth a glance now | Log line and toast |
| attention | A state the player must watch | A mark on the HUD that stays while true, and one log line when it starts |
| choice | The game waits for the player | A notice with its buttons |
| critical | The run changes | Full screen |
| refusal | A command the rules turned down | The toast in danger ink, with no log line. A modal shows it on its own status line. |

- A state alert lives on the HUD for as long as it is true. The log only records that it started.
- The log shows info lines only in its Verbose view. Brief, the default, shows notable, attention and critical lines. `src/ui/log-levels.ts` owns the level of each event in `EVENT_LEVELS`.
- Brief shows results, and Verbose shows how they happened. Ask of each line: after it, is something about the player's run different? That means what the player owns, the truck's health, who is friend or foe, contracts, places known, skills or standing. If yes, or the line is an alert to act on or a story beat, it goes in Brief. A change the screen already shows gets no line, like a driver turning hostile, which its hover panel shows. Everything else is Verbose: shots, rolls, driving, radio talk, the steps of a deal and world events that do not touch the player.
- Some examples. "Engine disabled", "+120 M: contract", "Truce with Fenn Marsh" and "Fuel low" are Brief. A shot for 5 damage, a ram, every radio line, a storm far away and a tow starting to hitch are Verbose.
- A new event type starts at info. It moves to Brief only when it passes the test above, and "the player is part of it" is not enough.
- Brief adds no line of its own: it only hides. Spam is cut by moving lines to Verbose, not by rewording them. A radio deal leaves one result line in both views, like a truce, a stand-down, cargo handed over or a fee paid, so the talk itself can stay in Verbose. One deal gives one Brief line, not one from the talk and one from the result.
- A notice always has the controls to answer it. A notice with nothing to press is not a choice and drops a level.
- Sound follows the level, and every sound follows `docs/sound.md`.
- A call that a turn raises opens `CONFIG.callBeatMs` after that turn's playback ends, so the hit shows before the words about it. Its notice, `radio` sound, antenna cue and opening log lines open in the same frame. A call the player starts opens at once.

## 8. Words and numbers

- A unit is written once per value and in one style. Money is whole M's. Fuel is L. Mass is kg. Speed is km/h. Work and cooldowns are in turns. Deadlines are game hours.
- `src/ui/units.ts` owns money. On a screen, money is `moneyEl()`: the M's coin, then the number. In a sentence, a log line or a title it is `moneyText()`, as "1 M" or "15 M's". A button with a price uses `pricedEl()`: the action, then the coin and the number from `priceTag()`, in danger ink when the player cannot pay. A button never spells a price in words, and a price never stands outside its button, where it could read as the neighbor's. Debt is a negative amount with a real minus sign, in danger ink. A change in money prints through `moneyDelta()` as "+15 M's" or "−15 M's". A test fails when a screen builds money text by hand.
- Time left to a deadline prints through `timeSpanText()` as "2:30 left" or "1 d 2:30 left", counted from the HUD clock, with the deadline's clock time in its title. A log line keeps the clock time, since it is read later. The first turn a contract fails comes from `dueTurn()` and `bountyTurn()` in `src/sim/market.ts`.
- A count of turns in a sentence prints through `turnsText()` as "2 turns". Value and unit are plain text, never "2t".
- A button is one verb and never holds its cost. The cost sits right beside it as icon and number: the clock for turns, the parts icon with a real sign for parts spent or gained, the coin for money. The words stay in the tooltip of the cost. An action that is off shows its reason in that same place.
- Each fact kind has one formatter in `src/ui/format.ts` or `src/ui/units.ts`, and every screen calls it.
- A number that the player's choice will change shows its after value before the choice, like the truck's mass before a buy.
- A label is a noun. A button is a verb. A reason is a short fact.
- Every UI word costs. Use the fewest words that stay clear, such as You and Them for the two gun lists.
- No arrow characters as connectors in UI text, such as You → it. Name the thing in words. An arrow is allowed only as the name of an arrow key.
- A tip says one thing to do in one sentence.
- Check tips, help text and reasons against the [Humanizer](writing/humanizer.md). A label stays a noun and a button a verb.

## 9. Visual language

| Channel | One job |
| --- | --- |
| Accent mustard | The lit key cap, the lamp and the gun's fire arc. On a screen, selection is inverse print |
| Danger | Loss, hostility and failure |
| Good | Gain, health and success |
| Ink levels | Text hierarchy only, never a state. Each surface has its own inks, in 4.6 |
| Size | Tier: key facts larger than supporting ones |
| Weight | Names and titles only |
| Dashed line | Provisional: a plan, or knowledge that is not exact |
| Flood fill | The thing under the cursor or selected, never a whole diagram |

The own truck's condition panel and the truck card show one picture, the real truck rendered from above. The camera is tilted 25 degrees and the front of the truck is at the bottom. The picture is rendered once per truck build, never per frame, and it uses the same wheel positions and armor as the truck in the world. It draws only the parts that have a condition: the core parts, the engine, the guns, the armor and the accessories like rams and spikes. Cargo, spare parts and other gear stay off it, so loading cargo never renders it again. A browser that refuses pixel readback, which `PixelReadback` finds out once, gets no render: both drawings show a plain plan of the truck's outline with the part tiles tinted by damage tier, and hover tips, aiming and the repair meter work on it as on the render. The body and the cab are drawn clearly darker than the parts, in their own hue, and the parts show their true paint, so the parts read first.

The picture is built in the background while the truck is in sight, so a card finds it ready. A card whose picture is not built yet shows everything else at once and keeps the picture's box, sized from the chassis, empty with no word or spinner. It takes no click on a part until the picture lands. A rebuilt picture, after a refit, keeps the old one on screen until the new one is ready.

A healthy part keeps its own color. Guns, tires and armor get a thin dark edge, so each reads as its own shape over the body, and a gun painted like the body still shows. Only damage gets a tier color on both drawings: yellow under 70%, orange under 40% and red when broken. A damaged part glows: it is tinted in its tier color and gets a soft halo of that color, so a thin gun still reads. A broken part also gets a light hatch. This is a deliberate break of P8, which asks that one state looks the same on every control. A green wash hides the look of a rendered truck, and only damage is news.

Every inventory grid, on the road, in a town garage, at an outpost, in truck trade, the full shop and the loot of a downed truck, draws the same truck a third way: straight down, nose up, at its true proportions, so a cell has the size of the truck's own grid cell in meters. The front and back armor rows lie just outside the truck's nose and tail, and the left and right ring cells on its sides, on every chassis. The grid has padding, so the truck never touches the edge of its panel, and the fit to the panel's height counts that padding.

At rest the garage is quiet. The bare truck and its built-in parts are drawn together and dimmed, darker and less saturated (`--garage-body-filter`). Each mounted part is drawn on its cells from its own render at that exact spot, in full color, with a soft shadow so it reads as placed on the truck (`--garage-part-lift`). A gun is drawn above every other part, armor included, so a gun on the other side faces out and stands on its own mount. A part below deck is drawn under the gear on its cells: a press takes the top part, a second press on the same cell selects the part beneath, and a third clears the selection. There are no cell washes and no grid lines at rest. The built-in parts have no drawing of their own until they are damaged. A part the body hides, like the transmission and tank on a chassis that does not show its cores, shines through as a faint light fill in its own outline with a soft glow (`--garage-hidden-*`), and when damaged it takes the damage halo and tint instead. Goods and loose parts in the semi's cargo bay glow over their cells, since they are packed crates.

The grid shows near the pointer. Cell lines and the armor edge marks fade in within about one cell of the pointer and fade out farther away (`--cell-grid-line`). While a part is held or dragged, every cell line shows (`--cell-grid-line-drag`).

A picked part marks the cells it can mount on with a light green wash (`--garage-mount-wash`), for as long as it is picked, whether it sits on the truck, in storage or in the loot, and for as long as a shop row is under the pointer. The picked part gets no box. Its mark is an edge that follows its own drawing, not its cell box. A hovered part gets the same kind of edge in a lighter line (`--garage-edge-hover`). The picked edge is bright white (`--garage-edge-pick`, `--ink-bright`), not accent. This is a deliberate break of the accent rule, which gives selection to the accent: the garage drawing marks a picked part white because the gun's fire arc owns yellow.

A picked or hovered mounted gun shows its fire arc as a yellow fan (`--accent-wash`, `--accent`). The fan starts at the gun's real pivot, the point the head turns on in the 3D truck, and not at the middle of its cells. It lies above every part except guns, and guns lie above it. A part that blocks the gun's fire gets a danger edge that follows its own drawing (`--garage-edge-danger`), never a box on its cells. A built-in part that blocks the gun, like the cab, is drawn for that time so the edge has a shape to follow.

Damage uses the same tier tint, halo and hatch as the condition panel on every part, and a healthy part keeps its own color, without the HUD's thin edge. Spare rows sit under the truck behind a small gap, in square cells the size of the truck's, or beside the truck, level with its nose, when the fit moves them there. Beside the truck they take their own square cells: the largest that fill the room next to the truck, up to 42px and never smaller than the truck's cell (`GridMetrics.spareCell`). A tile therefore changes size as it crosses between the truck and cargo. This bends P8, since the truck is height-bound and cargo is not, and one shared scale would leave the room beside the truck empty. A truck is always drawn whole and one vehicle, so a fit never cuts it at a hitch. Spare rows keep the icon tiles, as do cargo, spares and a part stowed off its mount, since those are not on the truck. A mounted part with no pixels of its own from above, like the buggy's engine under its body, keeps its tile on its cells too, so it can still be found and dragged. A picked tile has a white border, like the drawn parts. A drag snapped to a legal mount spot shows that spot's drawing. The grid never shows a part without its drawing: the drawings are built in the background, and a grid that would show one not built yet builds it before it draws.

The truck card's picture can be magnified, so a small part is big enough to read and aim at. The mouse wheel zooms around the cursor, a two-finger pinch zooms around the fingers, a drag pans, and the minus and plus screen buttons in the picture screen's bottom right corner step around the screen's center. A press on them never pans or aims. The closest view is a fixed 150px per meter (`CARD_ZOOM`), and the picture softens a little past its own sharpness. The keys give their reason when off: Whole truck shown at the fit, Closest view at the closest. The card keeps its box at every zoom and the magnified drawing is clipped to the picture screen, so Radio, Look over and the lists stay where they are. A drag that panned never aims. The zoom stays across turns and redraws, and resets when the card shows another truck or opens again. The math is in `src/ui/card-zoom.ts` and the pointer gestures in `src/ui/card-gesture.ts`. The own truck's HUD panel keeps its fixed fit. At the fit the drawing keeps clear of the buttons (`fitClearOf()`: a truck that would reach the corner shrinks until it misses it), and when magnified a part under them is reached by panning.


Hover shows the part name and its condition in a tip, on both drawings. The tip and the gun marks keep their UI size at any zoom of the drawing, so a truck zoomed down to fit still reads at tip size, and the tip may reach past the drawing's column. A gun's fan stays on the truck and never crosses cargo beside it. The part under the pointer gets an outline and a light fill over its cells. On a truck that can be aimed at, a click aims at it. On touch, the first tap shows the tip and a tap on the part already shown aims. The gun marks and the repair meter sit at the middle of their part. A part that has no pixels in the picture, like the transmission and the tank on a chassis that does not show its cores, shows on the body over its cells. The panel is black at 30% opacity with a 6px blur of the game behind it, with no border and no shadow. The truck picture of the diagram takes the same scan rows as every truck picture, on the truck and not on the panel.

World colors keep their meaning in the 3D view. The UI does not reuse the cold cyan of dead ship tech or the green glow of the reactor.

## 10. Where styles live

- `src/ui/tokens.css` holds every raw value: colors, fonts, type sizes, spacing, radii, shadows, layers and UI sizes. It is the only file that writes one.
- `src/ui/style.css` imports the tokens, then the files under `src/ui/styles/`. Its import order is the cascade.
- `src/ui/styles/components.css` holds the shared pieces below. Each other file under `styles/` holds one screen area and adds only its own layout.
- `src/ui/styles/windows.css` holds the window surfaces of 4.6: the screen look of `.well` and `.screen-block`, the screen button `.screen-btn`, the tape heading `.tape` and the engraved title. It loads last, so a screen's own file never repaints a window surface.
- `src/ui/styles/dash.css` holds the HUD skin of 4.5. The window housing, the key cap of a plain button and the input well live in `base.css` and `components.css`, since every screen shares them. `src/ui/dash/` holds its panels, and `dash.html` shows them with sliders and the housing picker.
- `src/ui/styles/drawings.css` holds drawn hardware: the compass gauge, the volume knobs of Options and the radio keys. Its shapes keep their own sizes, and its colors still come from tokens.
- `src/ui/tokens.ts` reads `tokens.css` for Three.js. `tokenColor("--path-plan")` gives a color number and throws on an unknown name.
- `src/ui/style-guard.test.ts` fails on a raw value outside `tokens.css`. It reads every file under `styles/`, `truck-condition.css`, the HTML page and the inline styles of the UI and overlay scripts.

## 11. Tokens

Name a token by its role, like `--ink-muted` or `--panel-l`, never by its value. Use the nearest token. When a value falls halfway between two steps, take the smaller one. Add a token only when two or more places need a value no token gives.

- Type: `--font-ui` for words and `--font-mono` for numbers and readouts. `--font-lcd` is the slanted fourteen-segment Casio face for the HUD clock and the LCD readouts beside the dial. It has Latin letters only, so the day label DAY stays the same in every language, like WOT RADIO. The time has faint unlit segments behind it. Six sizes only: `--text-caption` 10px, `--text-dense` 12px, `--text-body` 14px, `--text-lead` 16px, `--text-heading` 24px and `--text-hero` 40px. HUD panels and data use `--text-dense`. Modals and dialogs use `--text-body`. Quest text and its choices, in the talk window and the page view, use `--text-lead` at line height 1.55, since they are read as prose. Quest choices wrap and align left. A new size needs the user's approval. The boot gate holds the first paint of text until the faces of the language load, capped at 2.5 s, so text never swaps its face; after the cap the fallback shows.
- Surfaces from dark to light, for the pieces that are not baked, such as tracks, cells and overlays: `--surface-sunk` for tracks, `--surface-well` for cells, `--surface-dock` for the right dock, `--surface-panel` for a box drawn without art, `--surface-highlight` and `--surface-raised` for exempt controls, `--surface-hover` for a hovered row of an exempt control. Windows, screens and keys take their look from the baked art. Overlays over the 3D view use `--surface-overlay`.
- Lines from faint to bright: `--line-faint` between rows, `--line-soft` around chips and tiles, `--line` around small controls, `--line-strong` and `--line-bright` for the edges of exempt controls and notices. `--line-hover` edges a hovered tile. The garage drawing marks its picked and hovered parts with `--edge-pick` and `--edge-hover`, which stay near white so they never read as the accent.
- Text: on windows the inks of 4.6 apply, and `--hud-amber`, `--hud-amber-soft` and `--hud-amber-faint` are the screen inks. `--ink`, `--ink-bright`, `--ink-title`, `--ink-label`, `--ink-muted` and `--ink-faint` are aliases of those screen inks, kept so a screen rule reads by role. `--ink-overlay` is the text over the 3D view. `--ink-on-accent` is text on an accent fill.
- Accent marks the lit cap, the lamp and drawn marks like the fire arc: `--accent`, `--accent-bright`, `--accent-warm`, `--accent-dim` for utilities, `--accent-deep`, and `--accent-wash` for a light fill. Screen print never uses the accent.
- Danger marks loss, hostility and failure: `--danger`, `--danger-bright`, `--danger-ink` for text, `--danger-deep`, `--danger-dark` and the `--danger-well` fills. `--alarm` is the part that is about to break.
- Good marks gain, health and success: `--good`, `--good-bright`, `--good-deep` and `--good-well` for a meter track.
- The condition ramp `--cond-pristine` to `--cond-junk` colors part condition and must fall in brightness. A test checks it.
- `--cell-*` colors the truck grid in the inventory and `--map-*` the small grid on truck cards. Over the drawn truck the cells are only lines, `--cell-grid-line` near the pointer and `--cell-grid-line-drag` while a part is held or dragged, above every part (`--z-grid-lines`), and only spare rows keep their cell color. `--garage-body-filter` dims the bare truck and its built-in parts, `--garage-part-lift` raises a mounted part, `--garage-mount-*` mark the cells a picked part fits, `--garage-edge-*` are the hover, pick and blocker edges of a drawn part, and `--garage-tint-*` are the damage tints of a drawn part.
- `--path-*` colors the path preview on the ground through `tokenColor()`.
- `--steel-0` to `--steel-6`, `--lcd-*` and `--dial-*` color the drawn hardware only.
- Spacing steps run `--space-1` 2px to `--space-10` 32px. Padding, margin and gap use them. A 1px hairline and 0 stay raw.
- `--radius-s` and `--radius-m` round corners. A circle stays `50%`.
- `--icon-xs` 12px to `--icon-hero` 56px size icon boxes. `--icon-sign` 112px sizes the sign on the boot screen. `--meter-s`, `--meter-m` and `--meter-l` set bar heights, and `--pip` sets a rank pip or a footprint cell.
- Full screen panel widths are `--panel-s` 260px, `--panel-m` 300px, `--panel-l` 420px and `--panel-xl` 520px. HUD panels use the `--hud-panel-*` steps in section 4.5, and the right dock is `--hud-panel-m` wide. `--modal-width` and `--modal-height` cap a modal, and `--modal-top` is the top edge every center screen starts at, below the Menu.
- `--safe-top`, `--safe-right`, `--safe-bottom` and `--safe-left` are `--hud-inset` or the device inset divided by the UI scale, whichever is larger, and place every HUD zone. `--touch-target` is the smallest touch target, 32 real pixels. `--ui-scale` is the applied UI size, and `--layer-w` and `--layer-h` are the window divided by it.
- `--lcd-cell-w` is the minimum width of an LCD readout cell beside the dial, 88px, and 0 in the narrow layer.
- `--compass-w` is the side of the compass gauge beside the map, 60px. `--map-side-w` is the width of the Map tab's instrument column, 88px, wide enough for the heat chip on one line.
- HUD placement: `--hud-inset` from the window edge, `--hud-gap` between HUD panels, `--hud-hover-top` for the hover panel and `--hud-notice-top` for notices.
- Layers run `--z-hud` to `--z-report`, with `--z-confirm` above `--z-new-game` and below `--z-tip`. The report desk sits above the crash screen. A new layer goes between two others in `tokens.css`, never as a raw number.

These pairs must stay apart at 16px: fuel and store, supplies and cargo, supplies and salt, health and hp, heat and engineHeat, armor and blast, damage and blast, speed and accel, reload, turning and turn, cooldown and clock, mass and load, cargo, rows and cells, truck and cab, parts and tools, tools and wrench. `ui.html` draws every icon at each real size on each real background. On the LCD only, the money coin is a solid disc in LCD ink with the M cut out.
- `.chip` is a small boxed value, built only by `chipEl()` in `src/ui/chip.ts`: an optional glyph, then the value. A word value stays in the UI face and a readout value goes in `.num`, so a chip never prints its numbers and words in one face. Every glyph, the M's coin included, has one `--icon-s` box, and the padding is the same left and right. Mass prints as `1,680 / 3,840 kg` with the `mass` icon, and power as `1.5 / 6.6` with the `power` icon, followed by the `speed` icon and the speed cost when guns slow the truck. The words for the overload and the cost live in the chip's tooltip. Chips are for a head row of a shop screen. A count of free cells is not a chip on the player's truck, since the grid shows it, and the working screen uses stat rows instead. `.tag` is an outlined word in the color of its text.
- A contract's raider danger is one `.tag` after its summary: `dim` for safe, plain for risky and `bad` for dangerous. An escort offer words it as the road and a survey as the ground, on the board, in the town tab and in the journal.
- A contract is one row form in three places. A held row (`.job.held`) is the title over a meta row (`.job-meta`: clock and pay) and the action. An offer row on the board (`.job.offer`) has the same areas, with the truck's need (`.need`: cells, spare goods or win chance, `bad` when unmet) in the meta row. Each Accept shows its refusal reason beside it. The board puts offers beside the held well (`--board-held-width`), and neither scrolls in the standard states.
- `.contract-clue` is one told clue per survey place still open, on its own line under its contract. It starts with the place kind, then the map grid square the place lies in, like "square C4" (`gridSquareText()` in `src/ui/format.ts`, the one way a square prints), and ends with a `.tag` marked "found" when the place has been spotted but not surveyed. The HUD contracts dock prints it as the short compass clue, then what the place looks like (`surveyClueLines()`). Board and held rows keep the same clue as the row tooltip (`surveyClueTip()`), and the journal tells the way as `.way-steps`. The HUD dock also prints a held claim in short form (part, wreck and square, from `dockSummary()`), while the journal and the board keep the full clue.
- `.way-steps` is a numbered list of directions, built from `directionsSteps()` in `src/ui/directions-text.ts`: one `li` per step, the number in caption ink on a fixed column and the text wrapping under its own text. The radio keeps the same steps as one run of sentences through `directionsText()`.
- `.info-actions` is the action row of the truck card. It is the foot row of the card: the last row, right-aligned, wrapping right-aligned on the narrow dock. This row's place is the truck card's own bend, not the pattern for actions: an action on a selected thing uses `.action-rows` after its facts. It holds Call, Look over and Mark as `.btn-s`, in that order, each shown by its own unlock: Call for a driven truck in a mode with the radio, Look over with the Perception basic, Mark with the Spotter perk. A refused button stays, dimmed, with its reason from the sim query (`callError()`, `lookOverError()`, `markError()`). Off touch each label ends in a `.key` cap from `keyCap()`, T, B and N, and on touch there are none. A pinned card has its close key at the right of the title row, with Esc in its tooltip off touch, and a hover-only card has none. A click does what the key does. The row sits at the card's foot, so controls keep a fixed place (P14). The Marked countdown stays a quiet state line.
- `.pad-reticle` is the gamepad pointer and `.pad-radial` the ring of the radial dash, both in `src/ui/pad/pad-dom.ts`. They sit outside `#ui`, at real screen size, above every layer.
- `.key` is a keyboard key or a mouse button, drawn as the baked keycap at the small key edge, as in the help panel. A shortcut listens for the physical key (`KeyboardEvent.code`), never the typed character, so it works in every keyboard layout. The keycap text is only a label.
- `.amount .coin` is money from `moneyEl()`: the coin, sized in em by the text around it, then the number. The coin colors are the `--coin-*` tokens.
- The HUD's truck diagram (`TruckConditionView` with `CONDITION_LENGTH_M` in `src/ui/hud.ts`) draws every truck at one scale, 30 px for 0.65 m, and shows a truck up to 7.8 m long at full size. A longer chassis, like the 11 m sorokonoga, zooms the whole diagram down until it fits, so the bottom left zone keeps its height and the parts keep their icons and colors. Only the HUD diagram shrinks, and its tip and marks keep their size. `chooseLayout()` in `src/ui/inventory-draw.ts` fits every desktop inventory grid with no scroll. It takes the room and the truck, and returns the columns, the cargo's place and the cell size. Cells run up to 42px with no floor, and a long truck gets smaller cells, not squashed ones. In each room, for three columns and then for two, it scores cargo below and cargo beside by the truck's cell and keeps the larger, and a tie keeps below. The first room whose winner reaches 24px wins, and otherwise the largest. A stacked screen, on a narrow layer, keeps a 28px floor and scrolls instead, through `stackedLayout()`. The fit holds across redraws and changes only when the truck, its row count, the UI size or the room changes, so a click, a purchase or a comparison never resizes the grid unless it adds cargo rows.
- An inventory edge cell can hold two items, an armor part and an accessory over it. `itemBox()` in `src/ui/inventory-draw.ts` draws a mounted accessory as an `.inv-item.outer` box on the outer half of its cells, above the armor box, so both stay visible and each takes its own click and drag.
- `.hauler-gauge` is the hauler's chassis portrait beside a `.meter` of its condition, in the bottom left zone under the truck diagram, shown only in a run. Its tooltip reads "Hauler, 82% in one piece". It answers whether the hauler is okay, and the grid and the world answer the rest.
- `.compass-dial` is a flat round compass gauge on the metal beside the Map tab's map screen, the only compass in the game, built on each render from the truck's heading. Its cream face holds the four points as the letters N, E, S and W from the catalog (`map.north`, `map.east`, `map.south`, `map.west`), north in `--danger`. They stay Latin in every language, as compass letters do by convention, like the D of the clock. The card is turned by the fixed camera's bearing (`compassAngle()` in `src/ui/cards.ts`), so N sits up and right like the world's north and never moves, and E, S and W follow round it. A dark needle points the way the truck faces against the same card. It is `--compass-w` square, 60px.
- `.truck-switch` is a `.tabs` row of two buttons with a chassis portrait each, the player's truck and the hauler, above the grid on the outpost screen. `.on` marks the truck the grid, the repair bar, the supplies, the Buy parts tab and the Sell parts tab act on. The hauler button is disabled with the reason in its tooltip while the hauler is not parked on the pad.
- A queued job: the job chip over the truck shows the step in progress by name with its work meter and "then N more", since the grid is closed while the truck waits. In the inventory the step in progress takes the working meter, and the items and loot parts of queued steps take the planned dashed look (`.inv-item.refitting`, `.inv-chip.refitting`), like an open plan, since turns have not made either yet. The job block at the bottom of the truck column holds the work in progress on one row with its turns and Cancel, its bar under it, and each queued step dashed with its own turns. A number prints once, so the whole queue's turns live in the job row's tooltip.
- `.well` is a screen in a thin side column of a working screen, holding `.well-group` blocks: tight rows inside a group and a wide gap between groups. The middle column is its own screen.
- `.stat-row` is the one row form (P22): icon, label, value at the right edge, built by `statRow()` in `src/ui/cards.ts`. A value that has a cause takes `.has-cause` and its dotted underline (P5). `.meter-row` is a stat row with its bar under it.
- `.gauge` is a bar on a well, with a track lighter than the well (P23). The fill is `--ink`, or `--good` for a part's health. An accent span marks the share of the picked part, and a dashed span the share a choice would add (P9).
- `.action-rows` lists what the player can do to the selection, right after its facts (P14). Each row is one `.btn-s` verb, all of one width, then `.cost` with `.cost-item` icons and numbers, or the reason it is off. The cost lines print on the details screen at the edge by the key strip, one line for each key that has a cost or a reason, and none for a key with nothing to say, like Rotate in a garage, Dump, or Repair, whose price is on its key. Order: Patch, Repair or Rebuild, Weld, Rotate, Strip, Dump, so destructive verbs come last. A verb with no use on this part, like Rotate with no pose that fits, is absent. Dump is also the drop target of a drag, and the card shows the dragged item while it is held.
- `.job-block` is the work in progress and an open plan at the bottom of the truck column. `.job-row.queued` is a queued step, dashed (P9).
- `.meter` is a bar that fills from the left. `.s` and `.l` change its height. `.broken` turns the track to danger. `.progress` fills it with the accent for timed work and recharge.
- `.meter.notched` is the work meter of timed work, built only by `workMeterBar()` in `src/ui/work-meter.ts`: one cell per turn of the job, the first cells filled with the accent for turns done, up to `WORK_CELLS_MAX` (12) turns. A longer job, like an odd-jobs shift, keeps the smooth `.progress` fill. A cell is at least `--pip` wide, so the meter is at least as wide as its cells. The count ("4 of 8 turns left") is in the meter's `title` and `aria-valuetext`. The job chip takes the pointer, so hover and the long press open the title. The job chip over every truck, the player's own included, uses it. The working meter on an inventory tile stays smooth, since a tile is too narrow for cells.
- `.selected` is the one look of a chosen item. On a screen it is inverse print: a `--hud-amber` fill with `--hud-screen` print. A key that is on takes the lit cap pressed down instead. No screen draws its own selected look. Drawn hardware, like a switch or a truck instrument, keeps its metal and takes the accent edge only.
- `[aria-disabled="true"]` dims a control that cannot be used now. A key keeps its cap and dims its print. It keeps hover and focus, so its tooltip can say why. A control is never locked because a turn is playing, except the inventory grid's edits, which name cells the turn is changing and stay locked with no reason, since the lock ends by itself. A press while a turn plays waits through `Game.whenTurnEnds()`, then runs once, in the order pressed, against the world as it is then, or is refused as a fresh press would be. The control changes state when it runs. `spokenReason()` in `src/ui/dom.ts` owns this, and `showControl`, `createSwitch`, `disabledWith` and `markDisabled` all use it.
- `.tooltip` is a note that opens over its anchor. Words use the UI face and numbers use `.num`. It also gives the reason for a disabled control, as one short fact like "Need 12 M more".
- A held item a shop does not take keeps its row on the Trading post, after the shop's goods, and a stored crate keeps its sell cell. Both show the sale off with its reason (`notTraded`), with no price, stock or profit, and never a missing row.
- Item tip: the picture or box of a part, good or parcel on the inventory, shop, market, trade and outpost screens opens `itemTip()` from `src/ui/cards.ts`, the name and below it the item's lore. A shop row's open detail ends with the lore as a `<p class="dim">`. The selection card of a working screen leaves it out, since the same tip opens from the part on the grid. The HUD weapons bar, bare symbol icons and a card's own head icon keep the plain name. The name line repeats a visible name on rows, against P16, because the same tip opens from icon-only grid cells and ellipsized names. [Item lore](lore/README.md#item-lore) has the writing rules.
- `.chassis-feature` is the one dim line on a truck card in the shop: the ability name in bold, then its effect in one sentence with its numbers filled in from the chassis data. The outpost head and the truck trade head show the name as a chip with the effect as its title. The town band never does. The inventory's truck column prints the ability as its name and one stat row per number (`abilityRows()` in `src/ui/truck-panel.ts`), with the sentence as its tooltip (P24).
- `.qt-term` is a word in quest text with a term tip: a dotted underline, and the `.term-tip` tooltip on hover and focus. A term tip is story text, so it keeps the in-character voice of P10, not the chrome voice of other tooltips. It is reference, never needed to choose. [Term tips](writing/quests.md#term-tips) has the writing rules.
- The quest page view fills most of the screen, up to `--quest-page-width` by `--quest-page-height`, in three columns: the picture, the text and the quest's numbers and facts. The picture column is about as wide as the window is tall, and it shrinks first on small screens. The text column stays at least `--quest-text-min` wide, and its text is centered and capped at `--quest-text-width`, so lines stay readable. The right column keeps `--quest-side-width`. Where the picture column would fall under `--quest-art-min`, the page stacks like a narrow page: a picture band on top, then the text, then the side column. `questPageStacks()` sets `.stacked` from the window width. The picture keeps a shape between 3:4 and 3:1 (`--quest-frame-column`, `--quest-frame-band`) on every window. `questFrameFits()` checks it for `npm run layout`.
- `.art-band` is a wide picture across the top of a page, `--art-band-height` high (`--art-band-narrow-height` on a narrow layer), loaded with `loadArt()` from the session picture store (`src/ui/picture-store.ts`), which downloads the town pictures in the background after the sounds and keeps every picture for the session, and held by `ArtBand` so redraws keep its zoom running. A picture the store holds is on the image in the first frame. It zooms like `.quest-frame` and shows a plain striped band while the picture is still on its way or when a place has none. The place name and its chips sit over its lower edge. On the town front page of a desktop layer the band grows into the height the heading, prose and choices do not need, with `--ink-band-height`, the section band, as its floor, and the text sits at the bottom of the page (P12, P18). Section pages keep the fixed band, so the Garage keeps its height for panels. This bends P14: the front page band follows the window, since an empty block under the choices would hold no fact.
- The journal sits beside the live world as a side column (`.journal-side`, `--journal-side-width`) under the Menu row and above the log, with a tab row (Contracts, Wrecks, Notes, Past) that is absent while only one tab has content, and a body. The body is one fixed screen, so a long open way scrolls inside it with the rocker wheel. Under a scene or on a narrow layer it is a modal instead. The held contract's title prints at `--text-body` weight 700, the loudest line of the card. A held contract's way is numbered steps (`.way-steps`). A hover card hides the column while it shows.
- A survey contract's places are one grid (`.journal-detail.survey`): kind, square and state tag, with each row a subgrid, so every square sits in one column and every tag at the right edge. A closed place is a quiet regular-weight row at the column's size. The open place takes the screen-line selection look instead of bold, then its picture beside the lay ("Lies on flat ground.", never the square again) and the shop's question, then one `.place-way` group: the muted "From X" caption `--space-2` above the numbered steps, or, when the road has no way, one line with the distance and direction from a landmark. Inside a group the gap is `--space-2`, between groups `--space-6`. A surveyed place is a plain `div.place-head.done` in faint ink with a plain "surveyed" tag, not a disabled button. The open note head in the Notes tab stays bold.
- The journal's Wrecks tab, "Wrecks you heard of", sits between Work and Notes. It has one `.journal-place` row per map grid square that holds a wreck a driver told of and the player has not searched, newest first: "Square C4", or "Square C4, 2 wrecks" when the square holds more. A row says only the square, nothing more exact than the driver said. `toldWrecks()` in `src/sim/rumors.ts` gives the wrecks.
- `.survey-picture` is the 4:3 picture of one survey place, `--survey-picture-w` wide, at the left of its journal place block (`.journal-place.survey`), and stacked above the text on a slim layer. It adds no words and is hidden from screen readers. It comes from the same picture store and shows the striped surface of `.art-band` while the picture loads or when it never arrives.
- The Market goods row under Acumen puts each tint on the number it describes. The stock count takes danger ink when the shop is short and good ink when it is flooded, and the buy price takes good ink when cheap and danger ink when dear. Each tint names its fact in its title, and a stock fact never sits on the price.
- `.ink-page` is the frame of the town page and of a quest that opens while the town page is open, so a quest in town reads as the next page of the town. It has the quest page's box and layer, the `.art-band` across the top with the truck chips over its lower edge, then `.ink-body`: a centered text column of `--quest-text-width` with the heading and prose (`.ink-head`), an optional panel (`.ink-panel`, `.wide` for the full width), the numbered choices as `.screen-line`s on one screen (`.ink-choices`) and the status line. The heading of a named place is `.tape`, the prose is a `.screen-block`, and the way out is a key on the metal. The town page wraps the heading, panel and choices in `.ink-scroll`, which scrolls when the page is short, and pins `.ink-foot` under it: the way out (Back or Drive on) with the status line to its right, on one line cut with an ellipsis and the full text in its title. The foot keeps its place on every page, and the band and heading keep theirs on every section page. On a desktop layer the front page's band grows over the heading (see `.art-band`), so its heading sits lower. A quest's stats and facts sit in `.ink-side` at the right. A choice that is off keeps its slot, is `aria-disabled` and shows its reason at its right end, where a quest choice shows its skill check. On a narrow layer the band is `--art-band-narrow-height`, the chips flow under it and the columns stack.
- The town page is an `.ink-page`. Its front page names the place, gives a short paragraph, shows the place's fuel and supplies rows with their +1 and Fill buttons, and offers its places in a fixed order: Machiners' yard, Trading post, Contract depot, the site's canteen, Guidestar post where the site sells maps, Odd jobs and Drive on. A section page has its heading, one line, its panel and Back. The Machiners' yard and the Trading post set the truck column and a shop column into a wide panel. The Contract depot, canteen and Guidestar post sit in the text column, and on the canteen and the Guidestar post the locals are the choices, so their panel is `.fit`: it takes only its content's height and leaves the room to the choices. Every shop column (`.town-shop` of the Machiners' yard, Trading post, truck trade, outpost and full shop) pins its tab rows, filter chips and error line over one `.shop-body`, the only part of the column that scrolls. The truck column holds a `.truck-body` that scrolls only when the grid at its 28px cell floor is taller than the room, and the wide panel fills the room between the heading and the foot, so the page never scrolls as a whole on a desktop layer. In the truck column the side column `.inv-side` is the one scroll area, and each box in it (storage, crates, the picked part's card) keeps its content height, so a long storage list scrolls the column and never draws over the card. `npm run layout` fails on a scroll area inside another one. The Machiners' yard puts its service strip (`.town-services`) on the heading line, at its right: the repair buttons, then the fuel row and the supplies row with their +1 and Fill buttons. The strip wraps under the title when the line is too short, and takes the full width under it on a narrow layer. The outpost keeps its repair bar over the grid, tied to the truck switch above it. It builds the Buy parts chips and list, the Sell parts list, the Paint tab and the Market's sell cells from `src/ui/garage-tabs.ts`, the same module the Machiners' yard uses, with its own tab row of Market, Buy parts, Sell parts, Trucks and Paint under the truck switch. The yard also holds a `.tabs.sub` row of Buy parts, Sell parts, Paint and, at garages, Trucks, with the part filter chips as a second pinned row on Buy parts. The canteen lists the drivers in sight on the place's pads, each with a Radio button that is the map's hail. The Guidestar post holds the map of squares for sale, and picking a square shows its price on the Buy button. Digits pick the choices and Escape goes back one page, then drives on.
- `.quest-frame` holds a quest page's picture, cropped around its focus point. In the column it fills the column's width, starts level with the text's first line and is at most 4/3 of its width tall, so on a narrow window plain column surface lies below it (P12 bends there, since a taller frame would give back a thin strip). In the stacked band it is the band's full height, at most 3 times that wide, centered. The picture zooms in by 8% over 40 seconds and holds there. Reduced motion turns the zoom off.

Without the perk it is absent, and it uses no danger color, since a bounty is not hostility.

A filing makes no log line or toast. The player learns of a case from a Marshall's demand or terms or at the Roadhouse desk, so the well shows only what the truck knows (P6). Learning a case adds no log line, since the radio or the desk already shows it. Hover or focus opens one row per case above the total, in the flow of the well, since the dock scrolls and clips a tooltip. Its layout is in `styles/marshals.css`.

A case price is its fine plus restitution. When it holds restitution it has a dotted underline, and hovering Pay opens a `.tooltip-row` breakdown to the left of the button: Base, the fine, in muted ink, and Restitution in danger ink, since it raises a cost the player pays and danger means loss (section 9). - `.screen-block` is a screen of 4.6 set into a window: the baked `screen` piece as a 9-slice, with the screen inks. A new block of changing content takes this class, never its own screen look. It never holds another `.screen-block`.

## 13. How to build a screen

1. Write the decision as one question.
2. List the facts that answer it. Give each a tier.
3. List every state before any layout: nothing picked, each kind of pick, a refusal, work running, a queue, an open plan, the longest content, the largest truck, the smallest window and Russian. The layout must hold all of them.
4. Pick the zone or the surface: in the world, HUD zone, cursor card, notice or modal. Then place each piece of content on its surface by the grammar of 4.6: fixed words engraved on metal, game names on tape, changing content on screens, fixed actions and persistent choices as keys.
5. Pick the density for each entity and fill its slots in order.
6. Use the shared formatters, states and pieces. Put the screen's rules in one file under `src/ui/styles/` and import it in `style.css` after `components.css`. Write only layout there. A new shared piece goes in `components.css`, section 12 of this guide and `src/ui/guide/pieces.ts`, which draws it in `ui.html`. `src/ui/guide/guide.test.ts` fails when one of the three is missing.
7. Take every value from a token. Run `npx vitest run src/ui/style-guard.test.ts`.
8. Pick a level for every event the screen raises.
9. Take a screenshot next to a neighboring screen and check it against the principle tests. The [screens gallery](screens/README.md) holds the neighbors. Rewrite the image of every screen the change alters with `npm run screens -- <name>`, and add a new screen to it. [The inventory before and after](ui-redesign-example.md) shows a redesign and its reasons.

A large or disputed screen starts as a mockup that renders the real pieces with data captured from the real game, in a gitignored `tmp/` folder. It offers each state and window size on a floating control, it uses the real window so nothing is cut off, and a script presses every button and measures the states of step 3. Iterate with the user until settled, write down what was settled and why, and only then build it.

### The working screen pattern

A screen where the player works on a truck has three columns: the subject on the left, the open workspace in the middle and the selection on the right. The side columns are thin, `--inv-side-width`, on `.well` surfaces. The middle has no surface, since it is the interesting one. The title and the way out sit on the top row over the wells. The subject's own name sits in the middle column, level with the well tops, left aligned. When three columns would push the cells under the click minimum, the screen drops to two: the workspace and one panel that always shows the budget, then the pick or, with nothing picked, the other facts.

### Worked example: the inventory

1. Decision: what do I do with this part or crate, and what does my truck look like after it.
2. Key facts: load and power, since every change moves them, and the picked item's condition, mass and stats. Supporting: top speed with its causes on hover, acceleration, turning, fuel and supplies, the ability. Reference: the speed causes, the ability's sentence, an action's cost in words. Cut: free cells, money, mounted or spare (the drawing shows it), a mounted gun's arc (the fan shows it) and the lore (the grid's tip has it).
3. States: nothing picked, a part with and without damage, a mounted and a spare gun, a crate, a refused action, a job, a refit queue, an open plan, loot, a downed truck, the scout and the sorokonoga, from 1512 by 860 down to 1024 by 600.
4. Surface: a modal from `--modal-top` down to the bottom edge. The instruments and the log hide under it, and an opening tip sits in the free middle of the top row.
5. Left well: Load and Power as `.meter-row`, then Top speed, Acceleration and Turning, then Fuel and Supplies, then the ability's name and rows, with the job block at the bottom. Loot takes the place of everything under Load and Power. Another truck, looked over or downed, gets its own screen under the budget well: its name on tape on the lip, the downed state as the first line, then the drawing. The budget well shrinks to its content to give it room.
6. Middle: the truck's name, then the truck, with cargo below or beside it.
7. Right well: icon, name and wear on one row, then Condition with the wrench icon, its value and a green bar, then stats with Mass first, then action rows. Mass is first because every part spends the same budget. The wrench is a tool, since a heart reads as a living thing and a part is not one.
8. Foot: under the left well, the Auto Patch key on the player's own truck, lit while auto patch is on, or the loot keys while loot or a downed truck is open. Under the truck, Rotate and Strip.
9. Type: `--text-heading` for Inventory, bold `--text-body` for the truck's and the part's names, `--text-dense` for rows.

### Worked example: the character screen

1. Decision: which rank to buy or which perk to pick next, and what it gets me.
2. Key facts: each skill's rank, the cost of its next rank against the XP to spend, and the totals each skill gives now. Supporting: what the next rank adds, the perks at each rank. Reference: where a skill earns XP, a standing score's causes. Cut: knockouts, column heads, a gain row on every rank (each rank adds the same), "Taken" (the raised card shows it) and any note that a pair is one pick (two cards side by side show it).
3. States: a new driver, a pick waiting, a skill one rank from the top, every rank bought, a cost the player cannot pay, a scene or knockout that blocks buying, Chaos Highway with no Standing or Map, from 1280 by 720 down to 1024 by 600.
4. Surface: a modal from `--modal-top` down to the bottom edge, `--char-width` wide, so the instruments, the log and an opening tip hide under it. The Map tab widens it to the modal width.
5. Top row: Character, the Skills, Standing and Map switches, Close. Nothing else.
6. Skill tabs: one per skill in a row, each with icon, name and a Pick tag on its first line, rank pips and the next rank's cost on its second. A cost the player cannot pay is in danger ink. The XP to spend and the health close the row at the right, beside the costs they are read against.
7. The skill's page: the effect totals across the top in fixed columns, then a ladder with one row per rank. A rank row holds its node, number and cost in the left column and its cards on the right. Only the rank to buy shows Buy and its gains, one per effect in the totals' columns, so the change sits under the number it moves. Perks are cards: open ones edged in the accent with Pick, the taken one raised, the passed one faint. A rank with no perk is a short row, so a perk added at any rank takes a card and moves nothing else.
8. Words and numbers: an effect label names the thing that moves, like Repair time and price, and the sign says which way. A share with a base, like the field repair cap or the price gap, prints its real total and gains in points.
9. Type: `--text-heading` for Character, `--text-body` for skill names and totals, `--text-dense` for labels, costs and card text.

### Worked example: the truck hover card

1. Decision: fight, talk or leave, and if I fight, where do I aim.
2. Key facts: who it is and its stance, my chance on it, its chance on me, and the drawing for aiming. Supporting facts: truck type, faction, motion, activity and states. Reference: the causes of each chance, in the tooltip on the chance. Distance is left out, because it changes no choice here and range already shows in the chances and their causes.
3. Surface: the cursor card, in the right column, at the medium panel width and the radio's right edge, one HUD gap under the system row. The card has one size for every truck, `--hud-info-h` high, so the enemy card and the semi card match. The picture screen takes the height that the rows leave, and the gun table scrolls once it fills half the card. The floor is the log's top edge, or the top of the instruments when their buttons reach under the card. When the floor is higher than the card's bottom, the card stops at the floor. The dock panels and the log hide while the card covers them.
4. Grid: the head row holds the name, and the close key at the right when the card is pinned. Below it, the truck type and faction in muted ink, and the stance tag at the right edge. The state line under it starts with Parked or Moving and the speed, then the activity and the states. Below, the drawing sits in its picture screen, glass with the recess, and the truck is scaled to fit it on its tight axis, up to the zoom where one picture pixel meets one screen pixel (never under 1.5x). The player magnifies it from there with the − and + screen buttons in the screen's bottom right corner (see Hover shows the part name). Under it You and Them sit side by side in a strip that hugs their rows (wells of at least `--info-well-min`, stacked on the narrow dock). Call, Look over and Mark sit in a foot row (`.info-actions`) with no keycaps on their faces. The lists never scroll at 1280x720, even with nine guns. Only on a window under 720px tall, do they scroll inside the card, and the drawing keeps a minimum height. The card keeps its box while a turn plays, so the drawing does not refit.
5. Lists: the drawing is the aiming surface, so it gets the most room. The chances are what the player reads, so they sit right under it. With many guns the lists take height and the drawing's cells may fall under 24px, which bends section 6's click minimum. You sits in a well with a neutral edge, and Them in a well with a danger edge. One line per gun: your key for the gun, the gun's name in caption size, then the chance. A long name wraps to two lines, and a blocked gun says why on a second line, out of range, out of arc or shut down.
6. Color: hostility only in the tag, in danger ink with no box. Chances by value: 50% and up brighter, 0% faint, the enemy's in danger ink. Parts show the same picture as the player's own truck drawing: their own color when healthy and a tier color only for damage.
7. Type: four roles. Title for the name, body for words, caption for list heads, keys and gun names, mono for chances.
8. Tooltip: hovering a chance opens Base, then one row per cause in percent, so the rows add up to the chance. It opens over the drawing, never over the lists.

### Worked example: the help panel

1. Decision: which key or click does the thing I want.
2. Key facts: the key and the action. Supporting: where the click lands, like ground or your truck. Reference: the version and the world settings.
3. Surface: a dialog in the center, since reading it takes the player's attention and a top-left panel collided with the tips.
4. Grid: four groups side by side, Drive, Places and talk, Fight and View. Each line is the key as a `.key` keycap, then a short action. The panel grows sideways, not down.
5. Words: no sentences. "Space" then "Drive or pause" replaces "Space: drive on or pause." The notes are one quiet line at the bottom.

### Worked example: the town page

1. Decision: where in town do I go next, and once there, what do I buy, sell, take, fix or ask.
2. Key facts: on the front page, which place this is (the band and the heading), the money chip, the fuel and supplies rows where the place sells them, and which ways are open. On a section page, the facts its panel shows: prices, held counts, rewards and repair costs. Supporting: truck type, mass, power and free cells in the chips (the town band leaves out the chassis feature), a parked driver's truck and faction, a local's role. Reference: the reason a choice is off, in its tooltip. Ambient: the picture, its zoom and the prose.
3. Surface: an `.ink-page` modal of the quest page's size, so a quest that starts in town reads as the next page. It is larger than `--modal-width` because the Machiners' yard puts the truck grid beside the part rows (P18).
4. Grid: the band, then a centered text column with the heading, one paragraph or line, and the way out pinned to its bottom in the foot. The Machiners' yard and the Trading post set a full-width panel between them, and the other places a text-column panel. This bends P13 and P14: the outline, band, heading and choices stay fixed across section pages, and only the panel width follows the section. The front page's band is the one part that follows the window, since only those two need the grid.
5. Choices: quest choice buttons in the same order on every site, Machiners' yard, Trading post, Contract depot, the canteen, Guidestar post, Odd jobs and Drive on. A site with no maps has no Guidestar post, so the digits after it move up by one there. A place appears only if the player can do something in it. Odd jobs stays in its slot when off and shows its reason at its right end. It shows its pay at that end when on, through `moneyEl()`. Locals are the choices of the canteen and the Guidestar post. Back ends every section page.
6. Words: choices and prose are in story voice, reasons, chips and panel labels in the chrome voice. The prose names no price, no person and nothing the band or the choices show.
