# Economy

Values, prices, shops, contracts, the effort model, hidden salvage, fuel and supplies. The principles behind them are in [DESIGN.md](../../DESIGN.md).

Prices are in M's, the basin's currency ([lore](../../lore/README.md#ms)). One M is the price of 5 L of fuel at any town or stall, and every price reads against it. The sim and data keep money as whole cents, 100 to an M, so every price, fee and balance is exact to a cent. The UI shows whole M and rounds any part of an M away from zero, so a price, fee or debt never reads smaller than it is: 1.50 M reads 2, and a debt of 12.50 M reads 13. A balance rounds up too, so a balance and a price can read the same whole M while the balance is a few cents short.

- Fair price: every town garage and stall sells 5 L of fuel for 1 M with no spread. The scrap patch charges the same 1 M per 5 L it spares, and aid is priced the same.
- Roadside: a truck selling fuel adds the spread and the road spread, so 5 L costs 1.50 M at Trade 0.
- Petrol is a trade good, priced by distance and stock like any good. It is cargo, not tank fuel, so the anchor does not hold for it. A shop's fuel service draws on its own petrol, though, and its supplies service on its rations (see Stock and production below).
- Fuel is bought in whole 5 L units. The smallest purchase is 5 L for 1 M. Nothing rounds a fuel amount up for free or a fuel price down to zero.

Before the anchor, 5 L of fuel cost 3 money, so every money number grew by 100/3. The start money and NPC budgets are rounded to whole M.

| Thing | Old money | M |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 5 L fuel at a town | 3 | 1 |
| 5 L fuel from a truck, Trade 0 | 5 | 1.50 |
| Supplies, one unit | 5 | 1.67 |
| Scout full tank, 200 L | 120 | 40 |
| Standard start cash | 1000 | 333 |
| Tier 1 part | 100 to 250 | 33.33 to 83.33 |
| Chassis by tier | about 2000, 3000, 4500 | about 667, 1000, 1500 |
| Tier 1 wage | 0.37 a turn, about 110 a day | 0.1233 a turn, about 36.7 a day |
| Tow | 18.5 base, at most 166.5 | 6.17 base, at most 55.50 |
| Water, electronics base value | 18, 155 | 6, 51.67 |

1 M buys 5 L of fuel. 100 M buys 500 L, two and a half scout tanks, or one or two tier 1 parts, and is about 2.7 days of tier 1 salvage.

Every good, part and chassis has one value and a tier from 1 to 3. A part or chassis value is a hand-set base plus a bonus from the stats its kind is bought for, so better stats cost more. Chassis start at about 667, 1000 and 1500 M per tier. Tier 1 parts cost about 33 to 83 M. A good's value and its prices are per crate, and a crate weighs 50 kg whatever the good, so a dear good is dear by the cell, not by the kilogram. The town goods table says so above its rows. Every price is a formula from the value. Buying adds a spread and selling cuts it. Social narrows the spread to at most half. A truck on the road trades with a much wider spread than a town, since its driver answers to no market. At Trade 0 it sells at 1.5 times the value and buys at half of it. A part's value falls with each wear step, most at the first one, so pristine parts carry a premium. Its buy and sell prices also scale with its HP, but never drop below its scrap value from mass. Repair and rebuild cost a little more than the value they restore, so repairing a part to sell it never pays. A chassis trade-in pays the chassis sell price, scaled by the health and wear of its built-in parts.

Each town garage sells only its own lineup of trucks, see [content](content.md). Ship Metal is a tier 3 good found only in the Fallen Sun's caches and field spots. The Salvage Yard sells them and Nose buys them dear, and no other shop takes them. Shops trade in Bowl and Nose garages and in stalls at the Salvage Yard, the Granary, the Pump Station and the two outposts, Dustwell and Green Pit. Each shop trades only the goods its site makes or uses. A good is cheap where it is made. Elsewhere its price rises with the distance to the nearest shop that makes it, so a long haul pays for the miles. On top of that gradient the price reads the shop's own stock: below its target stock the good is dear, above it cheap, by up to 60% either way at an empty or a doubled stock. A lot is priced crate by crate through the stock, so a big sale pays less per crate. A shop sells only what it holds, so an empty good shows no buy. Garages keep a target of 200 crates of each good and absorb a full hauler load, and stalls keep 50 and move after a few units. Every shop sells fuel and supplies at the same anchor prices and repairs parts at the same prices. NPC traders trade through the same prices, so they move them too. An NPC takes a trade run only when its load earns more than the fuel for the trip at the pump price, so traders keep their working capital instead of burning it on long runs for small loads. NPCs sell cargo at whichever shop pays most, stall or town. Raiders sell only at their camps and the Salvage Yard. Every town and stall repairs NPC trucks, and only a camp repairs raiders. Profit comes from knowing routes, as in Dustland Delivery.

## Stock and production

Every site runs small recipes on a production clock of one game hour. A batch takes its inputs from the shop's stock and adds its outputs, all at once, and a missing input skips the batch. Bowl's sorgut needs Dustwell or Green Pit water, and falls to a dry-farm trickle of a third without it. A recipe whose upkeep good is out, like the Pump Station without tools or batteries, runs at its slower worn cadence. Production stops at a site's full stock, 1.6 times its target. Towns and stalls also use up goods on their own: Nose eats rations and drinks water, and every site burns drums and rations through its fuel and supplies service. The `recipes` and `site-stock` tables in the [economy wiki](../economy.md) list every rate.

- Dustwell pumps much water and Green Pit little. Both feed Bowl, Nose and the Granary.
- Bowl grows sorgut from water, and makes textiles, meds, parts from scrap and electronics from batteries and scrap.
- The Granary makes rations from sorgut, salt and water. Rations are the preserved food that refills a truck's supplies, worth their inputs plus the work.
- Nose makes salt, tools from scrap and drums, and batteries from parts and drums.
- The Pump Station makes petrol, and the Salvage Yard scrap and parts.

The fuel service draws one drum per 9 L units sold and the supplies service one crate of rations per 7 units, the crate's value over the anchor price. An opened crate serves the next sale. A shop out of drums or rations is rationing: it still sells at the anchor price, but only up to 40% of the tank or of full stores, so nobody is stranded and the shortage shows as a shorter range.

An essential good (water, sorgut, rations and petrol) that a site uses and holds none of starts a slow import after a day and a half: one crate every four hours, until about a seventh of the target. Prices stay near their peak there, so it is never a bargain. The market does not show it. The shop's price and its people do.

Once a day a site can surge or break down: the Pump Station and Bowl's harvest can run at twice their pace for a day, and the pump, Dustwell's well and the Salvage Yard's crews can break down to a quarter for two days. A site that holds tools spends one crate and repairs the breakdown in about five hours. The market tab shows no recipe, surge or breakdown. A local at every economic site tells what it makes, what it brings in, which of those it runs short of today and whether its works are down.

## Shipments and road news

NPC traders and supply convoys carry every shipment. Traders and convoys start with no goods. A trader buys where a run pays best after fuel. A convoy runs supply runs: it buys a good where it is made and sells it where it is used, weighted by how short the buyer is. Both buy and sell through the shop's stock like the player. While a truck carries a shipment its goal line reads "Haul sorgut to the Granary". A robbed, knocked-out or destroyed shipment never arrives, and its driver radios it in: the loss stays on the road news at every shop for a day and a half. The Market tab lists it under a Road news caption, with the lost crate count on the good icon, and the row's hover says why traders keep off that road. A run on a leg with news counts a quarter of its cargo value per report as risk, so a robbed Dustwell to Bowl road pushes water buyers to Green Pit until the news fades.

Traders and supply convoys carry working capital, so their trade loads are worth taking. Keeping named cargo from a robber costs its road price, one and a half times its value. Robbing them is a mid-game route beside hauling and salvage. It costs feuds, lawmen near towns and fights with escorts, and the player can demand cargo or M's from each driver only once. A driver that pays in M's gives up a cut of half its money above one tank and one supply load, capped at 88 M. The cap is the mean value of a cargo hold-up in a recorder batch, so robbing for cash pays about what robbing for cargo does. A driver hands over its cargo less often when its escort watches, so a guarded convoy mostly means a fight with its guard. The cues are what the player sees: a cargo box or rack, the goal line under the driver, its guns and its escort. `npm run income` measures the route against hauling and salvage.

Each shop holds a random, finite part stock with rolled wear, and restocks on a timer. Garages hold more and fresher parts, and stalls hold a few worn ones. Bowl and Nose always hold at least one stock engine after every roll. Spare parts found in the field are mostly worn too. A part sold to a shop joins its stock. Every shop, town garage or stall, keeps the player's garage storage and mounts parts at once. A bought part that does not fit the grid goes to storage, and stored parts sell at any shop. Storage is one shared store reachable at any shop. Rebuilds and chassis need a town garage. So does a paint job: it repaints the player's truck at once, with a body, a cab and a gear color each picked from one palette of nine muted paints, or all three at once from one of six ready-made schemes, for 5% of the value of the chassis it drives. The colors belong to the driver, so a chassis bought later wears them. Paint changes no number, and NPCs keep their role paint.

Shops post contracts, and each offer shows how long it gives. The clock starts when the player takes it, so a stale offer on the board never shortens the window. A held contract shows the game time it is due. The journal keeps the last 30 contracts that ended, with how each ended and the money it moved. The log warns once when a held contract is two game hours from its deadline. A haul takes goods from the posting shop's stock to a shop short of them, along a route the production graph uses, and delivers them into that shop's stock by a deadline. Taking a haul costs nothing, and its crates are owed to the shop while it is held: the player cannot sell, dump or use that many crates of the good. Past the deadline the haul is late and pays nothing, but the player can still hand in the crates that remain at the destination until as long again as its window has passed, for no pay and no bounty. If the crates are gone at the deadline, robbed, looted or spilled, the haul simply fails and costs no money. If crates are still aboard when the late limit passes, the posting shop files an embezzlement bounty on the player for their value. The Marshalls hunt it like any other bounty, see [crime](crime.md#reports-and-bounties). The kept crates stay with the player. A haul pays its travel at the salvage wage whatever the good, plus a cut of the goods' value. It is priced so a standard haul nets about what scavenging and trading earn per hour on a mid-game truck, counting the empty drive back and fuel. A standard window is twice and a half the estimated drive plus about a quarter day, so a loaded truck has room for a stop. Some hauls are rush jobs: they give a window of one and a half times the estimated drive, which the slowest measured loaded truck still makes, and pay 1.75 times the standard reward. A player in debt cannot take a haul. A supply order asks for 3 to 12 crates of a good the posting shop trades but does not make, that another shop makes and that the shop holds at least 3 crates under its target of, never more crates than that shortfall. The offer names the good, the crate count, the pay and the window, and never where the good is sold. The player gets the crates any way they like, bought, salvaged, looted or robbed, and hands the whole lot in at the posting shop before the deadline. The crates join the shop's stock. It pays each crate at the shop's price at posting with no spread, plus half the rush travel pay of a round trip to the nearest shop that makes the good, so the travel part matches a rush haul on that road one way. The window is the rush window of that round trip plus two buying stops. A shop holds one order per good, and while the order is on its board or with the player, it sells none of that good to anyone, player or NPC, and for as long as the order's window after the player hands it in, so the handed-in crates cannot be bought back for the next order. Its buy buttons say Held for an order. It still buys the good and uses it in its recipes and services. Taking an order needs no free cell or money, and a missed deadline fails with no charge, the crates staying with the player. A search names one driver who did business at the posting shop within the last quarter day and has since left, so is more than a sight radius (20 tiles) away. The offer leaves the board once that visit is more than a quarter day old, and the shop posts another at its next restock. Taking it loads a parcel for that driver into a free cargo cell, so it needs one free cell, and the player holds one search at a time. The parcel weighs 5 kg and is worth nothing to anyone else: it cannot be sold, dumped or stored, robbers and looters leave it, and a new chassis carries it over. The search starts with a lead at the shop. Any non-raider driver in sight, except a suspicious one, can be asked where it last saw the target and answers only from its own memory of a sighting, never from the target's live place. A newer sighting moves the lead on the map. The player finds the target, radios it about the parcel, and it pulls over beside the truck. Handing the parcel over meets the search, and the player claims the pay at the posting shop. A call alone meets nothing. A search lapses when its target is wrecked before the handover and fails with no penalty at its deadline, and either way the parcel is void and leaves the truck. A delivered search never fails. The pay and the window are set from measurement, see Search pay below. A salvage claim names one spare part lying in a remote off-road wreck: an old hulk, or a wreck in the Fallen Sun, Old Orchard or Glass Flats, far from every town and shop. The shop only posts a claim on a part that already lies in that wreck, so a claim never creates a part. The offer gives a clue: the kind of wreck (an army truck, a dead truck, a dead tank, a torn hull compartment, a split cargo pod or a fallen engine section), the square of the map grid the wreck lies in, the compass point from the shop, the distance rounded to 250 m and the territory. The map grid splits Icarus into 8 by 8 squares of about 390 m, lettered A to H toward the south-east and numbered 1 to 8 toward the south-west, the map\x27s top corner. A clue is fair when no other claim wreck fits it unless it stands within 40 m of the true one. When the shop's words alone are not fair, the clue adds the named piece of the territory the wreck lies by, like "inside the small arch shell" or "beside the hub". When that is not fair either, it gives the compass point and the distance, rounded to 25 m, from that piece. A name shared by several pieces, like a barn or a ring drum, fits every piece of that name. A wreck with no fair clue from a shop is never posted there. Nothing marks the wreck in the world or on the map. The player finds it by eye, by asking drivers near it over the radio, and by searching wrecks of that kind near the clue. A full search of the wreck that holds the claimed part always turns it up, and the loot window marks it with the contract's parts icon, so a full search without the mark means the wrong wreck. The shop sends a rival crew after the same part once the offer's head start runs out: the estimated trip plus one search, counted from posting, not from taking. The rival is the nearest free driver with the scavenger, vulture, roamer or trader trait. It drives to the wrecks that fit the clue, in the named square, nearest the clue point first, searches each, takes the part and hands it in at the shop for the same reward. The board and the contracts panel name the rival while its run lasts, and the shop tells a player holding the claim when it sends one. The player can race it, warn it off the wreck, rob it of the part or lose to it. A rival that gives way to a warning ends its run. Trucks that are not on the claim may search the wreck and take everything else there, but never the claimed part. A scavenger that finds only the claimed part leaves with the reason Claimed wreck. The player may take the part at any time, taken or not. The shop pays the part's full value plus half the travel pay a haul gives for the trip, so delivering beats selling, and the part joins the shop's stock. A mounted part must come off the truck first. When the rival delivers first, a held claim lapses with no penalty and the log says who brought the part. A held claim whose part is sold, dumped, robbed or taken off the rival by another truck lapses with no reward and no penalty, and a posted offer whose part is gone leaves the board. A claim cannot be dropped, so one the player gives up runs to its deadline and fails with no penalty, which frees the part; a rival that still carries it keeps it as loot. A rescue names one real stranded driver near the shop, who is no raider and has no helper yet. It shows the help the driver needs (fuel, repair or tow), a ring on the ground and the pay for the drive out. It is met when the player's own tow reaches a site, or the player's aid or patch leaves the driver able to move, and the player claims it at the shop that posted it. Fuel for a dead engine, supplies alone and a tow dropped halfway do not meet it. It lapses at no cost when the driver is wrecked, knocked out, towed by another truck, gone or back on the road without the player, and it fails at no cost when the player turns on the driver or the window runs out. The driver does not wander off while a board offer or a held rescue names it. Chaos Highway posts none. A survey names one to three old-world places far from every road and site, like a homestead, a hamlet, a lookout, a field of tank wrecks or a group of rusted boats. Every place can be reached from a road over ground a loaded light truck climbs, so the committed map offers no lookout. Nothing marks the place itself, on the ground or on the map, and no screen measures from the truck to it. The shop tells a clue for each place instead: the square of the map grid the place lies in, a named town or location it lies off, the compass word from there, a distance rounded to 100 m and whether it sits on high ground, in a hollow or on flat ground. The clue names the posting shop when that is fair, else the nearest named place that is. A clue is fair when no other old place of the same kind lies in the same compass eighth from the named place at about the stated distance, unless it stands close enough to the true place to be seen from the same spot, and a place with no fair clue is never offered. The board and held line show one clue line per place not yet surveyed, with its kind, what it looks like and a found mark when it has been spotted, and a hint line on how to find and survey it while a place is unfound. A held survey in the town tab and the journal shows instead the posting shop's way to each place, told from its gate in the road-and-landmark speech of the radio, with the square it lies in, what the place looks like, its lay and a found mark; a place whose way cannot be told in full keeps the compass clue. The journal adds a picture of each place as it looks from the game camera and what the shop wants to learn of each place. Until every place is surveyed, the Report button names the places left. The place is found the first time the truck sees one of its buildings or hulks with its own sight while it holds the survey, which the log and a toast tell. Gray vision and a sighting before the contract was taken do not count. Parked by a found place, the player surveys it as a timed job, which moving cancels. Only a survey finished while the contract is held counts. Every stock of salvage near a surveyed place that still holds salvage and was not searched joins the rumors, so it shows its label. When every place is done the survey is met: it no longer runs against its deadline and the player reports at the shop that posted it, which pays it once. A survey not met by its deadline fails with no charge, and a part-done survey pays nothing. A failed or late contract of any kind (a missed deadline, an escort left behind, a rescue the player turned on) lowers standing with the posting shop's faction where it has one. Contracts pay money, and a finished one trains Social from the work, not from a part's or a crate's price. An escort contract sits on the board while a real trader waits for a guard at a shop, and it goes when the trader leaves, which the offer shows as its departure time. The offer also names the road safe, risky or dangerous. The shop pays it, not the trader. It pays the travel wage for the road times 0.6, 1 or 1.5 for a safe, risky or dangerous road, where danger counts the raider posts near the straight line between the two towns. The player can take it only with a truck that could beat the trader in a fight, by a margin that grows with the danger, and with no other escort held. An escort has no deadline of its own. The trader drives its own trip and waits for a guard that falls behind, whatever the gap. The job fails and pays nothing if the trucks stay more than 40 tiles apart for 40 turns, which does not count while either truck is in a fight, while the player's truck is stranded, on a rope or knocked out, or while the player holds the trader with the radio order. A stranded player gets 120 turns of the trader's patience. The shop pays the share of the road covered, never more than the full reward, when the player is knocked out or stuck too long, when the trader arrives while they are apart, or when the trader is lost. It pays in full when the trader arrives with the player near. The player holds a few at once.

The unit of effort is one turn of play. The wage is the net money per turn a player earns at a tier. The tier 1 wage is what salvage earns, about 36.7 M a day. Loot spots in the Fallen Sun, Old Orchard and Glass Flats add many small stocks of salvage, so more of the map pays at tier 1. Glass Flats has two tables of its own: engine caches roll `engineScrap` (scrap, batteries, engine and armor parts) and ruin compounds roll `cityStores` (cloth, water, meds and scrap at farm-store scale). Old-world loot spots off the roads add a few more. They start with about a ninth of the value of the rest of the map's salvage, and now and then one holds a good engine. An item's effort is its value divided by its tier's wage, and data keeps each item inside a target band. A haul's travel pay is estimated turns of work times the tier 1 wage times a reward factor, set from the measured hourly money of scavenging and trading. `npm run progression:record` plays bots through the real turn pipeline, and `progression:report` prints wages and the day each upgrade is reached.

Salvage lies hidden until a search turns it up. Territory loot spots and road wrecks roll their stock into hidden loot. Each turn of a search, every hidden unit turns up with a 35% chance, or 55% with a working scraper's knife. Fuel and supplies each count as one unit and turn up whole. So a full stock gives a lot each turn and an emptying one gives less. What turns up stays in the stock, revealed, until somebody takes it. A search lasts one turn per two hidden units, or per two revealed units when nothing is hidden, and Machining shortens it. A loose pile takes one turn. A search never changes how much loot a stock holds. Search rolls use their own random stream, so they shift no other randomness.

- The player can search a stock while it hides units, or until they have searched it once. The Search action shows only then.
- One finished search opens the stock for looting. From then on every revealed item there can be taken, including what other trucks turned up. The first search of a stock teaches Machining, once.
- NPCs search with the same job and the same chance. When the job ends they take the revealed loot that fits, and the rest stays for others. They start a search only where something is hidden or revealed loot fits their truck.
- Two trucks searching one site roll on one pool, so nothing doubles.
- The daily restock adds to the hidden loot only, and a table's highs cap hidden and revealed together.
- Wrecks of destroyed trucks and ground piles lie in the open, so their loot starts revealed.
- A road wreck counts as looted once nothing is hidden or revealed.
- An object that holds loot, hidden or revealed, shows its colors clearly brighter and richer, and it goes plain once emptied.
- Saves keep the hidden loot, the revealed loot and a search in progress, so nothing rerolls.

Fuel and supplies limit range. Fuel burns per tile by chassis, times heat. Below 20% of tank capacity, the truck's top speed is halved. The truck crawls when fuel runs out. Supplies burn per turn, times heat. A full load lasts about 920 daytime turns. Without supplies the character loses health down to 30. Nothing refills supplies for free: every shop sells fuel and supplies at the same prices.

## Charts

Bowl and Nose sell charts of unexplored land. The map is cut into six by six square sheets, 130 tiles a side on Icarus, and each sheet is sold in the chart town nearest its centre, so every sheet has exactly one seller. A sheet is on sale while it still holds an unexplored tile.

- A sheet costs the tier-1 wage of the turns a truck at the reference speed would spend sweeping its unexplored tiles with its sight: `wage * unexplored / (2 * sight radius * reference speed)`. A half-explored sheet costs half, and a fully known sheet is not sold. An unexplored sheet costs about 13 M.
- A chart marks every tile of the sheet explored and discovers every town and location inside it, with a log line each, and brings up the mark of every place whose ground the sheet touches. It gives no XP, since a bought chart is not exploring, and it shows no trucks, wrecks or loot.
- The Map tab of the character screen [M] and the minimap, when Options turns it on, show explored and charted land. Nothing is saved besides the explored tiles and discovered places that exploring already writes.

## Search pay

A search pays `CONTRACTS.search.rewardTurns` turns of the tier 1 wage, 750 turns or about 92 M, and gives a window of 600 to 900 turns from the moment the player takes it. The pay covers the truck's running costs over a typical search and adds one tier 1 wage a turn:

rewardTurns = effort × (running cost a turn + tier 1 wage) / tier 1 wage, rounded to 10 turns.

- Effort is the median turns from taking the job to the claim. A design-time model of a player who follows leads and then tours the shops gave 246 turns on seed 2 (56 searches, 37 delivered within 600 turns).
- The running cost is fuel, supplies and repairs a turn. Two 900-turn trader runs gave 0.232 and 0.274 M a turn, 0.253 M on average.
- So rewardTurns is 246 × (0.253 + 0.123) / 0.123, rounded to 750. That is about 0.37 M a turn of searching: a quarter of what a haul pays a turn.

The `searcher` bot re-measures both inputs with `npm run search:effort`, but this host reached only 250 and 175 turns per 30-minute run: one search claimed after 117 turns, two still open after 84 and 154 turns, and a running cost of 0.33 to 0.37 M a turn that still holds the first fuel and supply top-up. That is too few searches to replace the design numbers, so the pay stays at 750 turns until a longer run measures it.
