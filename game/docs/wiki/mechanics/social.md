# Social

Radio calls, topics, the horn and Waste Of Time Radio. The principles behind them are in [DESIGN.md](../../DESIGN.md).

Every truck has a radio, as in Space Rangers 2. A call reaches only a truck in sight. The player calls the inspected truck with T: the one whose card is pinned, else the one under the cursor. An NPC calls the player when it has something to say. Turns wait while a call is open, and neither truck on the line shoots the other. A driver in combat with another truck answers a call with a short refusal in the call panel, and the player can only hang up. It does not honk back. Other drivers do not rob it, tow it or hire it until that combat ends. A driver that only flees, with nobody attacking or hunting it, takes calls. A hostile driver is part of the player's fight, so it takes calls even while it fights another truck. While the player is in combat, only the foe calls, with a cargo demand, a claim on spilled cargo, a truce or a plea for mercy. Tow offers and patch requests wait until the fight ends.

A truck on a call flashes the red antenna light twice, glows while the call stays open and flashes once when it ends. Drivers that just talked over the radio play the same cue in short, and so does the player's truck when its beacon switches on.

Talk is built from topics. A topic is lines and replies in data, and its logic is named conditions and effects in code. The player's call opens on a menu of the topics that truck can take up. A driver's traits decide its voice and its topics. A topic can be once per driver: that driver remembers how it ended.

- Personality: each driver is drawn one of eight personalities at spawn, and it never changes. It changes how the driver speaks and which information it shares, never what it does. Directions, market news, site tips, trading tips and rumors are open, closed, held for a small payment, or held until the player has helped that driver with aid, a tow or a patch, or until the driver's faction is friendly with the player. A wary driver answers with a cold line and keeps its trade and news to itself, but still answers a rescue. [Factions and relations](factions.md) has the bands. A gossip tells two rumors and a braggart tells tall tales. Each personality passes on its own kinds of rumor.
- Directions: traders and scavengers name the nearest town they know and tell the way by roads, turns and landmarks, never a bearing or a distance in meters. Every driver knows the roads, the sites, the bridges and the tall landmark props, so the route names them: "At Dustwell, turn left onto the road to Nose." A turn with no landmark is told by counting forks, only up to the third fork and only when the forks are at least 25 tiles apart and from the start of the leg: "At the second fork, turn left." A turn that cannot be told that way, or a sixth turn, ends the answer with "Ask again on the way." The place is named once, in the first words. A span ("a fair way") is said only off the road, and a long road leg gets "It's a long haul." The last road is not named again: "The road takes you right in." A driver far from any road, or a map with no road to the town, says it knows no road there. A way to a spot, like a wreck, leaves the road within 100 tiles of it. The leave heading is an octant, with a half-step lean when the true bearing is more than 11.25° off it: "head south and a touch east." A shop tells its way from its own gate, and a town with two gates opens with the one it uses: "Leave by the east gate." The town counts as found, but it shows on the map only once its ground is explored. The first road names the way to drive along it, as a compass point: "Follow the road west." A road named by its heading is not given it twice.
- Anything interesting: traders, scavengers, couriers, roamers, vultures and suppliers tell one trading tip from the shops they did business at in the last day. They name a good they saw at least a fifth over or under its value, the furthest off first, or say they have nothing worth telling. The tip is as old as the visit, so prices may have moved since.
- Any tips for the road: every voiced driver that is at peace with the player answers, whatever its personality, and tells one short story once per driver, then gives its repeat line. The driver's trade picks the story: haulers (traders, suppliers), salvagers (scavengers, vultures, roamers), raiders, lawmen (lawmen, guards) or gun hands (mercs, couriers). A driver with no voice, like a scumbag, tells its main trade's stories. If the player's truck shows a weakness, the story is about one of them, picked by the driver: no working gun, a side with armor mounts and no working armor, an engine, gearbox or tank worn enough for a roadside patch, or mass above the chassis's rated mass. Otherwise it is one of the trade's three general stories. The pick uses the world seed and the driver's id and rolls nothing. A story pays nothing and marks nothing. It gives the usual once-per-driver Social practice of any call.
- Tow: see [Defeat and recovery](defeat.md).
- Spares: traders and scavengers sell their spare parts to the player. The player cannot sell over the radio.
- Patch: a patch gets a broken engine or gearbox going again, and seals a holed tank that has run dry, at a quarter of its HP. The same patch lifts a badly worn engine, gearbox or tank (below a quarter of its HP) on a truck that still drives, up to a quarter. A stranded player asks a trader or scavenger. A stranded NPC asks a player with Machining 1 once, unless it carries the parts to fix its own truck. The NPC's traits roll the terms: paid, bring your own parts, or free. Only terms the payer can cover come up. The work runs while both trucks stay parked side by side, and the payment moves once when it ends. The parts are fixed when you shake on it. Whatever happens to the truck before the work ends, the patch lifts those parts. A deal nobody works on lapses for free. A deal that breaks, for lack of parts or pay or an attack, shows in the log.
- Demand: a raider or robber that has looked the player's truck over and is about to attack radios first, once, and asks only for the cargo it can load, the most valuable first, and names it on the radio. About two in five open fire without a call. The line names the haul and its price, the road price a truck asks for that cargo: one and a half times its value, rounded up to whole M's. A robber names the same price to every driver, whatever their Social. The player drops exactly that haul beside the truck, pays the price in M's and keeps the cargo, or refuses. The pay answer shows only while the player has the money. A robber with no room for the victim's cargo, or a victim with only mounted loot, asks a cash cut instead and names only the price. The cut is half of the victim's M's above one tank of fuel and one load of supplies, rounded down to whole M's and capped at the mean value of a cargo hold-up. A victim at or below that reserve is not held up for cash. Either call settles the one demand, so a robber demands once. Paying or dropping ends the fight and the grudge, and the attacker and its mates nearby then keep a truce for a while. Shots break the truce. Refusing keeps the fight. A stranded driver makes no demand. A log line shows an M's payment the player makes or takes.
- Spilled cargo: a robber that claims cargo spilled from the player's broken cargo part radios once, even mid-fight, and offers a truce for it. Leaving it makes peace and keeps the rest of the cargo, and the robber drives over to take the pile. Refusing or hanging up keeps the fight.
- Truce and mercy: the player can call a hostile truck and ask for a truce or give up. Mercy costs the cargo the driver can load or its price in M's, and the cash cut when it can load none. It costs nothing when the player has no cargo and nothing above the reserve. The driver's answer is rolled like an NPC plea. A robber after the player's cargo that has looked the truck over, not weak or outgunned, grants no free truce. It answers a truce with its demand, and the player hands the cargo over or pays the price, which ends the fight with a truce, or refuses, which keeps the fight. Either answer settles the robber's radio demand, so it does not make it again. After a refusal, the player cannot ask the same driver again for a while. A driver in a feud takes up only these topics. A hurt NPC calls the player with its own truce or plea for mercy. Sparing a beggar leaves its cargo on the ground, all of it, since the player picks up by hand. The player can instead demand that the beggar stand down. It answers as to a demand to give up, and a refusal refuses the plea. The player can also call a badly broken foe and demand that it give up. The driver answers as a stranded driver answers a surrender offer, once while it keeps the player in sight. A driver that accepts stops where it is and lies as a knocked-out truck the player can strip, then later retreats home. A stranded NPC that accepts a robber's offer lies down the same way, and the robber strips it.
- Aid: one truck gives the other fuel, supplies or both. A truck is low at or below 20% of its tank or supply cap, where its speed halves. The player can radio a peaceful driver that is low and offer some, paid at the town price up to the driver's money or free. A free gift gives Social XP as much as earning its town value in trade profit, less for the same driver again. A low driver asks for a little: at most a quarter of its tank and no more than it needs to reach a pump, and no fuel while its tank leaks. A truck on a tow rope gets no aid or patch offer. A low player can ask a trader, scavenger, lawman, courier, roamer or supplier once for a little, about one driver in ten agrees, more often a trader, scavenger or roamer. The gift is free, a quarter of the player's cap per low supply, and only from stock above the driver's reserve. Rarely, about 3% of sightings, such a driver offers unprompted to a player that looks poor (truck below half condition and worth at most 1,333.33 M) and is low. Raiders never help. The trucks park side by side, and nothing moves until the player presses [E] (or the action button). The handover is then one turn of shared work with a progress bar on both trucks, and the goods move at its end. A truck that moves pauses it. A feud or combat of either truck breaks the deal, so no deal is struck while either truck is in combat, and a deal nobody meets lapses. The [E] handover shows once the agreed driver is in reach, and a deal with a driver further away shows only in that driver's hover panel.
- Towing an NPC: the player can offer a tow to a stranded driver at peace parked within reach. The driver names its nearest known town and pays the tow fee, up to the money it holds, when the player reaches that town. The towed truck trails the player, and the player drives slower. The player can let it off the rope by radio for free.
- Patching an NPC: with Machining 1, the player can offer a patch to a driver stranded by a broken engine, gearbox or holed tank, unless it is on a tow rope. The driver names its terms as it does when it asks. The player can also offer one to a driver that still drives with a badly worn engine, gearbox or tank and cannot fix its own truck, while the player is not stranded. The driver pulls over and waits, then goes back to what it was doing.
- Surrender: a driver that fights the stranded player, with no other foe in sight, radios once. A raider or robber that is not itself stranded offers to strip the truck: accepting stands the player down: the truck lies as a knocked-out truck that gave up, and the robber strips it by the knockout looting rules, with no cap on what it takes (a robber with room for nothing makes the plain offer that takes nothing). Every other driver, like a lawman, guard, trader or merc, offers a plain surrender: accepting ends the fight with a truce and takes nothing. Refusing or hanging up makes every gun of the driver aim at the cab until the player is knocked out. A robber then loots the truck.
- Warning off: anyone can warn anyone off a wreck, a pile, a loot spot or a knocked-out truck. The player parked beside loot that a peaceful driver is looting can radio it once to back off. The driver leaves the loot, refuses and keeps looting, or fights. Raiders and lawmen fight more, traders and cowards give way more. It pays no XP. A driver that reaches loot the player holds may warn the player off in its own voice. Rolling on leaves the loot to it, and the player may not take from it for a while without a fight if the driver sees. Refusing, or hanging up, makes the driver back off or fight, about one time in four without traits. Daring it starts a fight. A driver waits about five turns for the answer and then leaves. Two drivers argue the same way, and the log shows the argument when the player sees or hears either truck.
- Help in a fight: while the player is in combat, a driver in sight that is not in the fight, not hostile to the player, not in its company and not a free merc can be asked "Need a hand here, I'm under fire!". The driver asks who is on the player, and answers that it comes, that it will not, or that the foe is too much for it. The player's foes are every awake truck in combat with the player. The driver refuses with no roll when it is knocked out, stranded, has no working gun, is already fighting or fleeing, belongs to an outlaw faction, is bound to or in a truce with a foe, likes a foe at least as much as the player (a lawman skips that test), is a coward that is only neutral toward the player, or gave its word to someone else. A lawman also refuses when the player attacked or robbed a lawful foe. A driver wary of the player does not offer the topic. Otherwise it weighs its own side plus the player's group against the foes' groups with the usual misjudgment, and says the foe is too much when that is not manageable for its boldness. Otherwise it rolls: a lawman or an allied driver agrees about 98 times in 100, a friendly one about 3 in 4, a neutral one about 1 in 10, a brave neutral one about 1 in 5 and a vulture less. A driver that agrees declares a feud on every foe, shows "joins the fight" in the log once, and drives to the nearest foe to fight it by its own sight, danger and guns, so it may still break off, flee or be knocked out. A refusal, or the answer that the foe is too much, blocks asking that driver again for 20 turns, and its hover card says it turned down the call. Help is free and pays only the XP every call pays. A free merc is hired with Ride guard for me for a day instead.
- Look warning: a driver the player looks over may radio "What are you looking at?", brave drivers, raiders and scumbags most often. "Nothing. Moving on." ends the call in peace. "Your load." is a threat: the driver answers it as it answers the Robbery demand below, and it counts as that driver's robbery. Only a driver at peace and out of combat calls, once while the player stays in its sight.
- Hired guard: the player can hire a free merc in sight as a guard for one game day, and dismiss it early with no refund. See [Hired guards](npcs.md#hired-guards).
- Robbery: the player can demand the cargo of a truck at peace, or its M's, once per driver for both. The driver gives it up, fights or runs. A driver that gives up M's pays the same cash cut a robber asks, so it keeps the money for a tank and a load, and a driver with nothing above that reserve says its pockets are empty and still holds the truce. NPC drivers settle a hold-up the same way: they pay the price in M's when their money above the reserve covers it and drop the cargo otherwise, with no roll. Traders and cowards give up more, raiders fight more, and every driver gives up to a much stronger player. A driver whose escort is in sight gives up a tenth as often. Giving up drops the cargo beside the truck and holds a truce with the player. Fighting or running starts a feud.

- Roadside encounters: on Icarus a driver that sees the player, at peace and not in combat, may open a call with something small to say. Each sighting rolls once, only when the radio is on, the player is active and out of combat, no call is open and no encounter waits. A raised call holds off the next roll for a third of a day, so at most about three come in a game day. Every pick runs on the usual rules: aid, threats, feuds, rumors and money transfers. Chaos Highway has none. The four kinds are once per driver.
  - A tale: a trader, scavenger, courier, roamer or supplier with an open personality offers a story from the road. The driver tells a short story in the voice of its trade, haulers for traders and suppliers, salvagers for scavengers and roamers, couriers for couriers. Asked "And?", it ends with a site it knows and the way there, which the journal marks, or its freshest trading tip. Hanging up before that marks nothing and tells nothing.
  - A salvage tip: a scavenger, vulture or roamer sells the place of a wreck that still holds salvage, for a tenth of the mean loot of a road wreck. Paying gets the wreck's map square and the way to it, and the journal keeps the square. A scumbag sells the place of a wreck it stripped itself, so the player pays for an empty trip. The Read the driver perk shows the scumbag trait.
  - A challenge: an armed driver that finds the player manageable calls the player out. Brave drivers do it most, scumbags next. Lawmen, guards and convoy drivers never do. The player can say they are not looking for trouble, which backs the driver off, show the guns again, which the driver answers as it answers a threat, or accept, which starts a fight. A driver that complies cedes nothing and the player earns Social XP.
  - The courier: once per run a courier that is low on fuel or supplies, or on its way to a pump for them, asks the player for a quarter of its tank or supply cap, free or at the town price, through the aid deal. When the courier next sees the player, at least two days after the deal ended, the call follows from how the deal ended. Free help gets thanks and the nearest rumor it can give, and two days on, the courier pays twice the aid's town value, capped by its money above a small reserve. Paid help gets a trading tip, and the story ends. Refused or lapsed help gets a cold call, and the courier never gives the player aid again. A courier that remembers the player attacking or robbing it gives the grudge call first, whatever the deal. The player asks for peace, which the courier answers as it answers a truce, or accepts the fight, which makes a feud and revenge. If the courier is wrecked before the story ends, a later courier can start it again.
- Moments: three quiet talks, each heard once in a world. At world creation each one goes to one driver who fits it, by the world seed: a kind, talkative or pious roamer for From the west, such a trader or scavenger for Eight to nine, and a kind or pious driver who is no raider and no scumbag for The Crucible. That driver lingers for its moment's reason more often than others. It calls the player itself when it is parked for that reason and the player parks within 40 m of it. From the west plays at a sunset watch, Eight to nine in the noon shade and The Crucible by a looted road wreck in the morning. Each tells a piece of the Old World as one family remembers it: the second moon falling from the west, the Spiral as the sun and a work song of the Race, and the Crucible. The player can ask or say nothing, and every path ends the talk. A moment pays nothing. Once the talk ends, or the player hangs up, it never comes again in that world, and the driver drives on. If its driver is wrecked or gone before the player hears it, the moment passes to another driver who fits, or to the next one that spawns. Chaos Highway has none.
- Claimed wreck: while the player holds a salvage claim whose part still lies in its wreck, a driver that talks rumors and is within 240 m of that wreck can tell where it lies, as a compass point and a distance from the player rounded to 50 m. A tight-lipped driver wants a small payment first, a suspicious one wants help first, and a grim one does not say. The rival crew on that claim never answers. Each driver tells once, and the journal keeps the answer under the claimed part, with the driver's name. Asking whether anyone else asked says whether the rival crew is near the wreck. It marks nothing.
- The Sunward: a car of the order answers every call with an open channel and nothing else, and offers no topic. During its vigil at the Fallen Sun it calls the player itself, up to three times a night, as [World](world.md#the-sunward) describes. The player agrees, refuses or hangs up. Refusing or hanging up on the third call starts a fight.
- The crater at night: traders, scavengers, couriers, roamers, vultures and suppliers answer a question about the crater at night, gated by personality like market news. While the order stands they tell of the lamps and the dragged trucks, and after it falls of the quiet.
- Dead Army wagon: once the player has heard at Bowl of a lost Army wagon, a driver that talks rumors and passed near the wagon can tell where it lies. It needs no perk and marks nothing. Its words go into the journal. Asking what else it saw ends the call and goes on in the talk window, about the raiders' tracks, the crew and what is left on the wreck.

## Settlement talk

Bowl, Nose, the Salvage Yard and Dustwell have people. Their town page has a People section, which the other places lack. On its page the locals are the choices, by name and role. Picking one opens their talk in the same page, under the same picture: a greeting, then the questions that local takes up, then the answer and the questions again. Answers appear word by word, and a word may stand out in bold or color. A click, Space or Enter shows the whole answer at once. Digits pick a question. Talk takes no time and teaches nothing. Only a job pays. Goodbye or Escape ends it, and a save keeps it open. Locals never drive or show in the world.

The town page reads like a quest page: the place's picture, its name, a short paragraph and numbered choices that turn to its five sections, Garage, Market, Board, People and Bar, or drive on. Each section page has a line of its own, the section's panel and Back. Digits pick the choices, and Escape goes back one page, then drives on. The Bar lists the drivers parked on the place's pads in sight, and its Radio button is the same hail as T on the map. A few scenes open once a game as the player enters or leaves a section, like the weigher at the Bowl Market and Lena at the Nose parts counter.

Each local has a voice and their own questions. Bowl talks warm and slow, of crops, canals and seed. Nose talks short, like the Army post it is. Each town is biased about the other. Two locals may answer the same question their own way and contradict each other. Some questions open only after something happened: finding a place, hearing a rumor or searching a wreck.

Asking after someone: while the player holds a search, any driver in sight except a suspicious one can be asked where it last saw the target. It answers from its own memory of a sighting, with how long ago, the direction and distance from the player and the way the target was heading, or says it never crossed paths. It forgets a sighting after a day. The player calls the target and says they have a parcel for it. The target asks the player to pull over, then drives up and parks beside the truck, as for a trade. With both trucks parked in reach, the action bar offers to hand the parcel over, and only that delivers it. A fight or a feud breaks the meeting off, and the player can call again.

Rumors are not always true. Some are sound, some outdated, some tall tales. A rumor never marks a place: the driver tells the way to a rumored site or wreck as roads, turns and landmarks. A driver telling a wreck, from a rumor, a courier's thanks or a bought tip, names the map grid square it lies in, like "out in square C4", then the way. Nothing marks the wreck on the map, the minimap or in the world: the player drives into the square and finds it by sight. The wreck counts as told once the driver tells the way, so hanging up afterwards keeps it. The Farmers patrol at Bowl and the dispatcher at Nose answer "Any work?" with the best-paying offer the town's own board has that the truck can take, and taking it is the same as taking it from the Board section. With the board empty, with nothing on it the truck can take, or with the player holding the most contracts, they say so.

Kovac at Nose has a job off the books: fuel is leaving the Nose depot, and a shipment rolls soon. It plays as pages: a picture, the scene and its choices, and a column with the watches left, the money, how much the depot talks and the facts found so far. The player picks leads, such as ledgers, the gate log, the clerk, the trader at the gate, a bunk and a night watch. Each lead costs one of a few watches. Leads show what the player sees, like a ledger page or a price board, and never say who did it. Too much noise warns the thief. Kovac weighs the facts behind a name. Naming the right man with enough of them pays, naming the wrong one or taking a bribe has its own end, and the outcome changes what Kovac and Ibo say after.

The journal [J] has four groups. Work lists every contract the player holds, with the same row as the town Contracts tab: what it asks, the pay and when it is due. Under a survey sit its places, and under a salvage claim the radio leads on its wreck. Wrecks you heard of lists the map squares of the wrecks drivers told of that the player has not searched, newest first, with a count when one square holds more than one. Notes hold rumors and clues, newest first, with the day, who told them and what they said. The log notes each new entry. Plain lore stays out of the journal. A journal note is information, never an order or a place on the screen. Past lists the last 30 contracts that ended, newest first: what each asked as it was posted, the day it ended, the shop that posted it, how it ended (done, failed, lapsed, or ended early for an escort cut short) and the money it paid or charged. A group with nothing in it is not shown.

H honks, also while a turn plays. Traders and scavengers in earshot that are not hostile honk back. During a turn they answer once it ends.

Waste Of Time Radio plays on the radio panel above the log, and above the held contracts when there are some. It steps away while the hover panel of a truck would cover it. J.J. reports weather as it starts and ends, new contracts on boards of found places, raider robberies and knockouts of other drivers, dawn, noon, dusk and midnight, and road wisdom after a quiet stretch. She names a place only when the player has found it and it lies near the news. Anywhere else she gives a basin direction. She never reports the player's own fights and never quotes prices. News comes before clock calls, and clock calls before road wisdom. Broadcasts keep a gap of several turns between them, old news is dropped unsent, one place gets one raid report in a long while, and one restock gives at most one contract report, the best paid. A load or a new game starts the station over without announcing what is already on the boards. NPCs do not listen, since the radio changes no rule or decision.
