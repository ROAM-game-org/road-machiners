# Truck

The inventory grid, refits, mass, gun power draw, utilities, the claymore ram, wear, breakdowns and field repair. The principles behind them are in [DESIGN.md](../../DESIGN.md).

The truck is equipment. It can be changed, upgraded, and destroyed. The truck stays critical to progression, like the ship in Space Rangers 2.

Each truck has an inventory grid, in the style of Dredge. Parts and goods all take cells, and the player arranges them by dragging and rotating.

The grid is a top view of the truck with the nose up. Each chassis marks deck cells, an engine bay and armor edges. Weapons, scanners, utilities, cargo frames and stores all mount on deck cells, so every deck cell is a choice between hauling and fighting. The engine bay is fixed per chassis. Armor mounts sit on the front, back, left and right edges, and every front and back edge is an odd number of cells, so a 3-cell ram sits centered. A part works only while it lies fully on mounts of its own kind. Plain cells hold spare parts without installing them. Built-in parts sit on fixed cells: the cab, transmission, four wheels and fuel tank. They can be repaired but not removed. Heavier chassis carry tougher wheels, transmissions and tanks, so a gunwagon does not stall to a few machine gun bursts. An open seat takes 1x2 cells on small cars, and a closed cab takes 3x2 on the rest. The cab, the transmission and the engine keep clear of the wheel columns, and the transmission sits in the middle. Every cab is tall, the open seat too. The transmission is a 2x2 block and the fuel tank is two cells long. Empty mounts hold cargo like any other cell, so every mounted part but an accessory costs cargo room. Parts have shapes: a cannon is a 3x1 bar, an engine is a 2x2 block.

A truck has three layers. Car parts sit inside, armor sits on the edge cells, and accessories bolt on over the armor. Every armor type comes in a 1-cell and a 2-cell piece, so one type can fill any edge. Accessories mount on the edge cells on top of whatever armor or cargo is there, and take no cargo room. Rams are accessories of 3 cells that mount on the front and back only. Spikes are accessories of 1 or 2 cells that mount on any edge. A shot or crash meets the accessory first and the armor under it next, and a cell slows a round once whatever it holds. A truck that rams or is rammed hits harder with the best ram or spikes that cover a touched lane, so spikes guard only the cells they sit on.

Some long trucks are rigs: a driven front unit and a towed rear unit joined at a pivot, like a tractor and its trailer. The semi and the sorokonoga are rigs. A rig still has one grid with the nose up. The rows before its coupling row sit on the front unit, and the rest sit on the rear unit. A part on the rear unit points and fires by the rear unit's heading, so a trailer gun swings with the trailer, and hits on the trailer land on the trailer's own lanes. Cargo on the trailer weighs on the trailer. A rig wreck lies folded at the bend it had.

Any part can turn in quarter turns, clockwise, from 0 to 3. A turn of 1 or 3 swaps the part's width and height. A gun faces the way it is turned: 0 to the front, 1 to the right, 2 to the rear and 3 to the left. Its firing arc centers on that facing and turns with the truck, so a gun that faces the rear covers the rear and cannot hit a target ahead unless its arc is wide enough. Auto-mount turns a new gun to the facing with the best open fire, so it covers sides no other gun does, and keeps the front when two facings are equal. Other parts only turn between 0 and 1. A gun's barrel lies over the cells ahead of it in its lane. Those cells cannot hold another gun, and two guns' barrels cannot cross, so guns always stand side by side and never one above another. Cargo and other parts may sit under a barrel.

Stores raise how much fuel and supplies a truck carries. A jerrycan rack adds 60 L of fuel. A supply locker adds half the base supplies. Each mounts on one deck cell, so it competes with a gun. A store counts only while mounted, and a broken one still holds. Removing a store spills whatever no longer fits. Bowl and Nose sell both. The Pump Station sells jerrycan racks, and the Granary sells supply lockers.

Cargo parts add full-width rows to the grid while mounted and working. Rows per deck cell rise with tier. The scout, hauler and longbed carry 1, 2 and 3 built-in plain rows under the chassis grid, before the cargo part rows. They hold goods and spare parts, never go dead and never spill. On the Sorokonoga each cargo part gives half again as many rows, rounded down. Small carriers give one row per deck cell and fit single cells and strips: panniers add one row, a roof rack or flatbed two. Tier 2 frames take a 2 by 2 block for five rows, and the heavy frame takes one for six, so a frame beats any stack of small carriers on the same four cells. The light frame is light and fragile, the enclosed frame armored and heavy, and tall boxes block fire across them. A break spills only that part's rows, so many small carriers lose less at once. The shop card shows the cells a part gains on your truck, which is its rows times the truck's width, less the deck cells it sits on. A broken cargo part's rows go dead, at the bottom of the cargo rows, and whatever lay on them falls onto a pile beside the truck. Dead rows still slow rounds like empty cells. Repairing the part brings the rows back empty.

Goods come in crates. One crate fills one cell and weighs 50 kg, whatever the good, and inspecting a goods cell says so. Crates can be moved or dumped anywhere. Dragging an item onto another swaps them if both fit. Spare parts ride in the grid or wait in garage storage.

Equipment changes at a shop are instant. Away from a shop, a change is a refit job:

- Installing or removing a part takes 5 turns.
- Replacing an installed part with a spare takes 10 turns.
- Moving an installed part to another mount takes 10 turns.
- Installing a salvaged part takes 5 turns. The part stays in the stock until the job ends.
- Removing a part from a knocked-out truck takes 5 turns, and 10 when it goes straight onto a mount.

With the inventory open in the field, moving, turning or swapping a part that comes off or goes onto a mount only edits a plan. Goods and spare parts move between plain cells at once, with no plan. The grid shows the planned layout with a Cancel button and the planned turns. Closing the inventory starts one refit job for the whole plan, priced from the original layout, so a part put back where it started costs nothing. While a plan is pending, loot lands only on cells that are free in the drawn layout and in the real one. Taking a part off a wreck or a knocked-out truck first queues the open plan, then the part. Storing, dumping or a patch, strip or weld ends the plan.

A working patcher crane on the truck cuts 5 turns to 4 and 10 to 7. Machining shortens these times further. The old layout stays active until the whole job finishes. Moving goods or spare parts without installing them stays instant.

While a refit runs, more refits queue behind it as steps: a plan of the truck's own moves, or one part off a wreck or a knocked-out truck that costs turns. Each step is checked against the layout the steps before it leave, and no two steps may take the same part. The grid shows that layout. The step in progress has the progress meter, and queued items and loot parts have the dashed planned look. The refit banner counts the turns of the whole queue, and the HUD shows the step in progress with how many more wait. When a step finishes, the next one starts the same turn and costs what it would cost started by hand then, crane, machining and Cannibal included. A step that no longer works when it comes up, like a part someone else took, is skipped with its reason in the log. A step that costs no turns, like moving cargo crates, applies when it comes up. Driving off, combat or Cancel refit drops the whole queue. Taking goods or loose spare parts, Take all, storing and dumping stay blocked during a refit. While the queue runs, no one else loots a truck or a wreck it takes a part from.

Every part has a mass, and every crate of goods weighs the same 50 kg. A heavier truck accelerates, brakes, steers and tops out worse. Every kilogram counts: a truck lighter than its handling mass beats the listed speed, and a heavier one falls short. The rated mass is the load limit. Every truck can put tier 1 armor on all its armor cells and guns on half its deck and stay at or under it. Cargo and heavier gear use the rest of the room. Past the rated mass the loss is severe: 500 kg over a 3000 kg rating leaves about half the speed. For the same job a higher tier part weighs less, so better gear leaves more room. Trucks make tradeoffs: cargo vs armor vs fuel use. No truck is best at everything.

Every working gun draws power from the engine, and each gun slows the truck. A gun draws by its size and tier: its cells, plus a quarter more for each tier above the first. A machine gun draws 1, a long rifle 2 and a tank gun 4.5. The draw of all working guns, against the engine's capacity, cuts top speed and acceleration by up to 48%. The curve is convex, so the first guns cost little and a deck packed with guns hits the full 48%. On a stock engine, one or two light guns cost about 2 to 8%. A broken gun draws nothing. The gun and engine cards show the draw and the capacity. The truck headers in the inventory, shop and trade show the engine's power, the draw, what is spare or over, and what the draw costs in top speed. Hovering or focusing the HUD max speed lists each effect that moves it as one short line, like `Load: −10 km/h`, and a low or empty tank as its own line.

The engine is the general capability upgrade. Its capacity sets how many guns the truck carries before they slow it. A light flat-four carries few guns. A stock engine carries more, and a heavy diesel carries a fortress. The turbine and the racing V6 give raw speed, and the diesels give gun room.

Utilities are yellow deck parts with one job each. Six are active: the Sprout, Caltrops, Oil spiller, Smoke mortar, Flare cannon and Emitter. Each acts once on an order and then recharges. A utility used on turn T with a reload of 10 is ready again on turn T+10, and a miss still starts the reload. How they are used and what they do is in [Turns, driving and combat](turns.md), and the flare in [Sight and detection](detection.md). The patcher crane and the scraper's knife are passive and work while mounted. A utility works only while it lies fully on deck cells and has HP left. A broken one does nothing, but what it already put into the world plays out. Rounds and blasts damage it like any part in their lane. Several cranes or several knives count as one.

The patcher crane speeds up refits and the roadside patches its truck does for others by half, so they take two thirds as long. A roadside patch takes twice as long as field repair of the same parts, or 4/3 as long with a crane. Field repair of the truck's own parts does not use the crane. A patch's price does not depend on it. The scraper's knife makes searches find hidden loot faster, see [Economy](economy.md), and stripping a part pays 65% of its value instead of half, but a knife being stripped does not raise its own yield.

The claymore ram is a ram accessory with an explosive charge. It mounts and protects like any ram, and it also takes a slot in the utility row to be armed, or disarmed by order. Its blast is in [Turns, driving and combat](turns.md). After a blast it needs 20 turns before it can be armed again.

No part is better than another of its kind at everything but price. Each has one job and one weakness, and a content test enforces it.

Parts wear. Each part has a wear level, from pristine to the last step. A part gains one wear step each time it drops to 0 HP. Fixing a damaged part that still works adds no wear. Each step lowers max HP and makes the part worse at its job: a gun scatters more and loses damage, penetration and range, an engine gives less speed, acceleration and gun power and uses more fuel and heats faster, armor stops less, a scanner sees less far and an active utility or a claymore ram recharges 10% longer, rounded up. The crane and the knife only lose HP. Every step costs the same small share of each of the other stats, so wear tilts a fight without deciding it. Of two like trucks, the one with parts two steps less worn wins about two fights in three, and pristine parts beat parts at the last step about four times in five. A part that breaks at the last step is junk. Junk cannot be rebuilt, only stripped or sold for scrap. Built-in parts stop at the last step and never turn to junk, so old trucks slowly decline and the player changes trucks every so often. Parts move between trucks, so a change keeps some progress. Pristine parts are rare, so the player hunts for them.

Every turn the truck moves, each mounted part may lose HP. The chance grows with the tiles driven, the truck's speed, how rough the ground is and the weather. Roads wear parts least. Rarely, a part breaks down and loses a large share of its HP at once, logged in the event feed. More rarely, the engine or the transmission fails outright and drops to 0 HP, so the truck is stranded. A scout driven hard off road fails about once in two hours of play. A driver with parts patches it in the field, and one without needs a tow or a roadside patch. Wear and breakdowns never take the cab below 1 HP. The same rule wears NPC trucks, so stranded NPCs turn up on the roads in normal play.

Every player chassis has one ability that bends one rule for every truck of that chassis, player and NPC alike. The numbers are in `CHASSIS_FEATURES` in `src/data/chassis.ts`, and the shop card and inventory header name each one:

- Built-in bed (scout 1 row, hauler 2, longbed 3): plain rows that never spill.
- Nimble (buggy): 25% more acceleration, and shots at it scatter 4.5 degrees wider while it moves. The hit card lists the cause as Nimble.
- Racing gear (courier): 25% more top speed.
- Off-road drive (jeep, niva): no top speed lost on rough ground, and terrain wear above road wear is halved.
- Gun mounts (gunwagon 25%, wheel loader 50%): more gun power, so the guns slow the truck less.
- Armored frame (van +1, bukhanka +2, armored carrier +4): armor on every part but shields. It cuts the share of a round that part itself takes, by the round's penetration over its armor, so it helps most against low penetration rounds. It never raises the damage a part takes.
- Battle frame (heavy tractor): +2 armor and 25% more gun power.
- Heavy springs (bus): mounted parts count half their mass toward the load penalty.
- Open top (convertible): sight and every gun's range 20% longer.
- Grand tourer (Lincoln): sight 25%, gun range 15% and top speed 15% more.
- Versatile trailer (Sorokonoga): guns 30 degrees wider in arc and one turn faster to reload, cargo parts give half again as many rows, and parts on deck cells get +1 armor.

A gun's range, reload and arc on a truck include its chassis ability. A part's own card keeps the part's numbers.

Parts is a trade good, bought and sold like scrap or salt. It is the resource field repair spends. The standard start kit carries a few. Stripping a part is a parked job that turns it into units of parts. A mounted part first comes off, which takes as long as moving it to cargo and nothing at a garage, and it stays on the truck until the job ends. Its value sets the yield, so a broken or junk gun still pays.

A job is work that needs the truck parked for a number of turns: a refit, field repair or scavenging. The player has at most one job at a time. Driving before it ends cancels it and the turns already spent are lost. The HUD shows the current job and its turns left. A seen NPC shows its job and progress above its truck.

Field repair fixes one damaged mounted part. It spends parts and restores HP up to a field cap below full, only when the job finishes. The parts it spends are worth about the value it restores, so an expensive part costs more parts to fix. Machining shortens the job and raises the cap. A full repair to 100% still needs a shop. Scrap armor is the exception and patches to full on the road. Ceramic armor cannot be patched at all, only repaired at a shop. The inventory panel shows a Patch button on a damaged part, with its turns and parts cost.

Auto patch is on by default and toggles with P. A new game starts with it off, so the player patches the nearly dead engine by hand once. Whenever the player truck is parked and idle, it patches the most damaged part with one unit of parts at a time. It leaves alone parts promised to a roadside patch and parts carried for a haul contract.

NPCs use the same field repair. New NPCs carry up to two units of parts when their loadout has room. A damaged NPC prefers nearby shade, or repairs where it stopped. Low supplies and low fuel come before a repair detour.
